# ADR 0035: Internal Billing System — Subscriptions, Services, and Non-User Counterparties

**Status:** Accepted (2026-09-25)

## Context

`decisions/0034` established that a tier can be reached by payment or by promotional grant, and flagged as an open consequence that `commercial.md`'s tier model needs a second dimension — grant source and a review point — with "its own account-level fields... and its own lightweight admin review workflow; neither is designed yet." Per direction, that gap is bigger than grant-source alone: Traversence needs a real internal billing system for its own recurring subscription revenue (§1) and its White-Glove/add-on services revenue (§2, §10), billed through Traversence's own transaction system — explicitly **not** the same thing as `decisions/0031`'s Market (no processing, post-and-connect) or `decisions/0032`'s Quote (a mechanism for two platform users to transact with each other). This is Traversence billing its own partners for platform access, back-office, not a front-facing commerce feature. Per direction, it also has to account for the fact that not every transaction Traversence's business runs is a payment *from* a user — money also flows *to* non-user counterparties (vendors, contractors, field staff, service providers), which the existing identity model doesn't yet have a place for.

## Decision

**A distinct internal Transaction System, separate from Market and from Quote.** Subscription and service billing runs through its own back-office system for Traversence's own accounts-receivable (partner payments) and accounts-payable (vendor/contractor payments) — never through the front-facing, no-processing Market mechanism (`decisions/0031`) and never modeled as a Quote between two platform users (`decisions/0032`). Those two stay exactly as decided: no in-platform payment processing for user-to-user Market activity. This ADR's system is what Traversence uses to run itself as a business, distinct from what it offers its users as a marketplace mechanism.

**Subscriptions — the recurring tier-access billing object.** A Subscription is monthly or annual, tied to a single listing and a tier (Core/Strategic/Cornerstone) — **confirmed per direction 2026-09-25: per listing, not per account.** A business operating multiple locations carries one Subscription per location, each independently priced and independently tiered (mirroring `commercial.md` §10's existing per-location multi-location billing model), not one shared subscription covering every listing an account touches. It carries:
- **Source** — paid, promotional/comped, or trial (generalizing `decisions/0034`'s grant-source concept from a flagged gap into the actual field that closes it).
- **Description** — which tier, effective from when.
- **Terms** — cadence (monthly/annual), rate, any multi-location volume-discount band applied (`commercial.md` §10).
- **Status** — active, past-due, paused, canceled, or (for a promotional grant) pending review — mirroring the `entities.status`-style enum pattern already used elsewhere rather than inventing a new shape.
- **Process/management** — a real lifecycle: create, renew, convert (promotional → paid or vice versa), or cancel, with `decisions/0034`'s review-gated continuation as the specific workflow governing a promotional Subscription's transition, not a general one-size mechanism for every transition.

**Services — a distinct object, not a restatement of Subscriptions.** A Service covers the one-time and ongoing add-on work `commercial.md` §2 and §10.4 already describe — White-Glove Setup, the Google Maps/Local Place Management add-on ($125 one-time + $50/mo per location), custom multi-location onboarding — billed differently from a flat recurring tier fee (one-time fees, per-location fees, mixed one-time/recurring). Services get the same four fields as Subscriptions (source, description, terms, status) because the same lifecycle questions apply, but they are a separate object type from Subscriptions because their billing shape genuinely differs, not because the two need different governance.

**Both tie into the trust and review system — for internal/admin purposes only, never into the public Trust-Weighted visibility score.** Per direction, billing standing (current, past-due, a promotional grant's review status) is a real signal admins should be able to see alongside an account's trust/review history when making a judgment call about that account — e.g., reviewing whether SJ Portables' promotional Cornerstone period should convert, extend, or step down (`decisions/0034`) benefits from seeing their actual usage and any review/rating activity (`decisions/0032`) in the same view. **Hard boundary, restated from `decisions/0007`: none of this ever feeds the public Trust-Weighted visibility model.** Paid tiers sell tools and support, never placement — a Subscription's status (paid, past-due, comped) must never become a ranking input, and a business's public review/rating standing (`decisions/0032`) must never be influenced by its billing status either. The tie-in this ADR establishes is an admin-facing join between billing records and trust/review records for account-management decisions, not a new input to the score itself.

**Not all transactions are with users — a Counterparty concept, separate from `users` and from `entities`.** `decisions/0002`'s dual-accounting model stamps every write with `actor_user_id` (who did it) and, for listing writes, an `entity_id` (which listing it's authorized against) — a model built for platform users acting on their own or a listing's behalf. Traversence's own accounts-payable side (vendors, contractors, Regional Directors and Field Executives, Creative & Fulfillment Services providers — `commercial.md` §5) pays parties who are neither a `users` row nor a listing `entities` row — they have no platform account and no claimable listing. This needs its own lightweight record type — a **Counterparty** — distinct from both, so a vendor payment isn't forced into an identity model built for customer/listing accountability. The accountability side of `decisions/0002` still applies fully: which staff member recorded or approved a payment to a Counterparty is still stamped with a real `actor_user_id` — only the *receiving* party of an outbound payment is the new concept, never the accountability of who authorized it.

## Consequences

`commercial.md` gains a new formal section for Subscriptions and Services as real objects (cross-referencing this ADR rather than duplicating it), and its "What's Still Missing" per-location/per-business billing question and this ADR's Counterparty concept both bear on the same eventual schema-design pass. Concretely still undesigned, flagged here rather than assumed: a `subscriptions` table and a `services` table (source/description/terms/status fields per above), a `counterparties` table for non-user payees, and the admin-facing view joining billing status with trust/review history for account decisions like `decisions/0034`'s. `decisions/0034`'s flagged gap ("commercial.md's tier model gains a second dimension... neither is designed yet") is what this ADR resolves at the decision level — the schema/workflow build-out itself is a separate, later technical-design pass. The hard boundary against feeding the public Trust-Weighted model (`decisions/0007`) is asserted here explicitly because this is the first place billing data and trust/review data are deliberately connected in the same admin view, and that boundary needs to be enforced in the actual implementation, not just assumed to hold.
