# ADR 0005: Human-in-the-Loop Guardrails on All AI/Autonomous Ingestion

**Status:** Accepted. **Amended 2026-09-24** — see the System-Created vs. Self-Generated Partner Content amendment below; the original Decision and Consequences sections are left as written, since the amendment narrows what this ADR's staging-and-sign-off rule was ever meant to cover rather than loosening it.

## Context

Traversence needs to solve the cold-start problem (a new region has no listings or content yet) using autonomous scrapers and AI copilot tools that parse public utilities, historical data, and regional narratives. Left unchecked, automated ingestion risks overwriting organic human context, local memory, or community truth with generic or incorrect machine-generated content — directly undermining the Charter's commitment to "human accountability, local truth, or organic connection."

## Decision

All autonomous scraping (`ingestion-scrape-queue`) and AI-assisted drafting output lands in a temporary staging holding zone first — never directly into the live graph-mirror topology. No automated or AI-generated output enters the permanent graph without an explicit manual admin sign-off confirming it. This applies uniformly regardless of how confident the AI system is in its own output.

**Role note:** this is a platform-admin function (technical content-pipeline review), distinct from the community Steward role defined in `architecture.md` §11, which governs community charter enforcement and moderation, not content-pipeline approval. Earlier drafts of this and related decisions used "steward" loosely for both roles; corrected here to keep the two separate.

**Concrete case: re-scraping an already-claimed listing — corrected 2026-09-23 to the real, field-level mechanism.** `decisions/0012-unified-entities-schema-hybrid-graph-mirror.md` makes this guardrail concrete for one specific, recurring scenario: when a re-scrape matches an entity via `composite_hash`, including one that's already claimed and verified, the match does not bypass field-level protection — any field a human (owner, admin, or high-trust user) has directly edited is `is_locked` and permanently immune to being overwritten by scraped data regardless of recency. There is no separate `listing_ownership` table in the real system, and this isn't a blanket "send the whole record to the staging queue" rule — an already-verified listing can still legitimately absorb new data on fields nobody has touched. See `architecture.md` §9 for the full mechanism.

## Amendment (2026-09-24): System-Created vs. Self-Generated Partner Content

**This is a stated amendment, drawing a line precisely, not a silent loosening of the original rule.** The original Decision text above ("No automated or AI-generated output enters the permanent graph without an explicit manual admin sign-off... regardless of how confident the AI system is") was written against this ADR's own Context — the cold-start problem, autonomous scrapers, and AI copilot tools parsing *public* data to seed a new region. Read literally, its "AI-generated output" language is broad enough to also sweep in a partner writing their own seasonal update with an AI-assist tool's help, which was never the scenario this ADR was guarding against. Per direction, that ambiguity is resolved now with a real structural distinction, not left to drift.

**Two categories, distinguished by whose voice and authority the content represents — not by whether AI touched it:**

- **System-Created Content (Platform & Authority Engine) — this ADR's original rule applies in full, unchanged.** The automated Regional Guides (`commercial.md` §7's Foundational Macro & Geo-Hub Guides, Dynamic Cross-Zone Itineraries), and the scraper-to-article catalog pipeline (`architecture.md` §25 — one article per distinct product/listing variant, AI-selected topics and AI-authored drafts per §26, published into `/discovery/` under the platform's own editorial voice). This directly represents the brand's core narrative (**the Living Geography**, `brand.md` §2) and the platform's own public-facing authority — it stays strictly gated: every draft still lands in the staging holding zone and still requires the same explicit Administrator sign-off before publishing, at every tier, with no exception. Nothing about this amendment changes §25 step 3's HITL Gate.
- **Self-Generated Partner Content (User-Created) — a real exception, not a subjective-style review at a lighter setting.** A partner's own updates, seasonal notes, event posts, profile descriptions, and the Strategic-tier "custom partner content" (`commercial.md` §1) and co-created Owner Spotlight Interview — written in the partner's own voice, whether typed directly or drafted with an AI-assist tool configured to their own needs — does not pass through this ADR's staging-and-sign-off gate at all. The platform does not gatekeep this content on subjective style or narrative fit. Instead, it passes a synchronous, automated check at write-time — basic safety (no illegal content, harassment, malware/phishing links), legal compliance, and anti-spam filtering — and nothing more. Content that clears this check publishes immediately, under the partner's own name, with no staging queue and no admin sign-off. Content that fails is held back with clear, specific feedback about what failed and why (the same "immediate, explained verdict" posture `decisions/0017`'s document pre-screening already established, applied here instead of silent rejection), so the partner can fix and resubmit — never manually micromanaged for tone or quality by an admin exercising discretionary judgment.

**Where "admin/White-Glove-supported" content (Strategic and Cornerstone, `commercial.md` §1) fits — two different meanings of "admin" that shouldn't blur together.** A White-Glove team member helping a partner draft or refine their own seasonal update or Owner Spotlight Interview is a creative-services relationship — the platform's staff assisting the partner's own voice — not this ADR's compliance gate. That content is still Self-Generated Partner Content once published: co-created with staff help doesn't make it System-Created, and it still only needs the safety/legal/anti-spam check above, not a separate Administrator sign-off. The two "admin" roles are distinct: one helps make the content (a service, optional, tier-gated), the other approves platform-authored content before it goes live (a control, mandatory, tier-independent, and only for the System-Created bucket above).

**What this settles that was previously left ambiguous:** two rounds of tier-quota work (`commercial.md` §1) established that Core's AI-assist is "self-service, self-created publication," without fully specifying which content types that covered. This amendment settles it precisely — Core's profile-level assistance and (per this amendment) Strategic's custom partner content pieces are Self-Generated, safety-checked only; the AI-generated catalog-article quotas at every tier (5/25/uncapped, `commercial.md` §1) remain System-Created and go through the unchanged staging-and-sign-off gate regardless of tier or how "self-service" the surrounding access level is.

**The filter's north star, binding on whatever concrete technical subsystem eventually implements it — per direction, 2026-09-24.** The Consequences section below already flags that the safety/legal/anti-spam check's actual mechanics (keyword lists, rate limits, which subsystem if any it reuses) are undecided. What is decided now, ahead of that build, is the scope any such mechanism is required to stay inside — a real design constraint, not just framing:

- **The vision risk this guards against — corporate overreach.** A filter built like a heavy-handed social-media content-moderation algorithm — over-filtering tone, flagging colloquialisms, injecting rigid stylistic rules — would betray the platform's anti-extraction, pro-autonomy positioning (`commercial.md` §9, `brand.md` §1's "replacing legacy corporate directories"). It would make Traversence feel like exactly the kind of gatekeeping tech platform it's positioned against, to the small businesses it exists to serve.
- **The actual mandate — guardrails without friction.** The filter's scope is strictly hard safety and integrity: blocking malicious actors, illegal goods or services, explicit spam, and fraudulent representations. It protects the directory's integrity. It never questions a merchant's local flavor, regional voice, or personal expression — tone, colloquialism, dialect, and style are explicitly out of scope, not edge cases to tune conservatively.
- **Framed as a network defense shield, not a content tax.** Whatever technical subsystem eventually gets chosen or built for this check must be evaluated against this scope before it's evaluated on accuracy or convenience — a filter that's technically effective at catching spam but also flags regional phrasing or informal tone as a side effect fails this ADR's actual requirement, not just a style preference. This is the same posture `decisions/0007` already established for tier pricing (sell tools, never gatekeep visibility) applied to content instead of ranking.

## Consequences

Cold-start seeding is slower than a fully automated pipeline would be, by design — every new region's initial content has a human review step before going live, which is a real throughput cost. In exchange, the platform can credibly claim (as a stated differentiator against legacy platforms using "black-box algorithms") that no visible content was published without human sign-off, which is a trust claim that depends on this guardrail never being bypassed for speed. **This trust claim is unaffected by the 2026-09-24 amendment above** — it was always a claim about the platform's own editorial/System-Created output, not about a business owner's ability to write their own profile copy without corporate review, so narrowing the guardrail's scope to what it was actually meant to cover doesn't weaken it. The safety/legal/anti-spam check for Self-Generated Partner Content is a new, separate mechanism this amendment introduces conceptually but doesn't fully specify — the concrete filter rules (keyword lists, rate limits, which existing subsystem if any it reuses) are a first-default design task still to do, not decided here. What *is* decided is the scope that design task has to stay inside (see the north-star note above): hard safety and integrity only, never tone or style — so whoever eventually builds the concrete mechanism inherits a real constraint, not a blank slate to over-engineer from a generic content-moderation playbook.
