# ADR 0040: Delivery Method Contract & Buyer-Proposed / Staff-Verified Route

**Status:** Accepted (2026-09-26)

## Context

While designing SJ Portables' delivery-distance calculator (its own FRD, Delivery Calculator section), a gap surfaced in `decisions/0032`'s Quote model: the model assumes a buyer requests a quote against an item that already has a computed public price. That holds for anything priced under `cost_markup`, `cost_margin`, or `mirrored` (`decisions/0039`), but not for delivery — SJ's own freight items (containers, tanks, septic, culverts beyond a simple case) have no delivery cost until a human works it out, and even SJ's ABCO buildings, which do have an automatic "free within 50 miles" rule, have no defined rate for the tier beyond that radius. Per direction, the fix is not a new top-level Quote state or an eighth `pricing_model` — the item's own price is usually already known; it's specifically the delivery line that's unresolved, and delivery should be treated as a line that's always present on an order, not an afterthought bolted on only when it happens to be easy to compute.

Separately, per direction, real road-distance routing for rural delivery needs actual route geometry — not a straight-line distance, and not a bare mileage number either. Rural road data quality varies by provider, and a customer's own local knowledge of how to actually reach their property (a specific gate, a side road, a driveway a generic router won't know about) is often better information than any routing engine has. That led to a further refinement, per direction: the buyer proposes the path themselves (dragging route waypoints on a map at intake), and SJ Portables staff verifies or corrects it before the delivery line is priced and the Quote can proceed — rather than either trusting an automated route blindly or asking staff to guess a correction with no local input at all.

## Decision

**A `delivery_method` concept, configured per listing or per product line, generalizing beyond SJ Portables the same way `pricing_model` and the External Source contract did (`decisions/0039`).** Every Market item (or product line) that ships or delivers physically carries one of the following delivery method types:

- **`carrier_rate`** — a real-time rate lookup against a small-parcel carrier (USPS, UPS, FedEx), keyed on origin/destination address and package dimensions/weight. Relevant to mailable native-catalog goods; not SJ Portables' primary case.
- **`flat_distance_rate`** — a merchant-configured formula, not a fixed platform number: a free-delivery radius (miles), a per-mile rate charged beyond that radius, and an optional flat base/dispatch fee. This is SJ's ABCO-building case. The platform does not assume or hardcode a default rate — the field starts unset until the merchant enters one, the same way markup, RTO terms, and the free-delivery radius itself are already merchant-set rather than platform-fixed. Configured in the same Delivery Settings admin module already planned for lot coordinates and the free-delivery radius; editable by whoever holds `listing_access` on that listing, with an Executive-level manager able to apply one value across every listing in a Location Group if the merchant wants uniform pricing across locations rather than per-listing variation.
- **`freight_ltl`** — oversized/palletized freight, dependent on dock access, gate width, crane needs, and appointment scheduling (the site-access questions already captured at intake). Not practically automatable; stays a staff-priced manual entry. This is SJ's container/tank/septic/culvert case.
- **`manual`** — the general catch-all fallback when none of the above applies.

**The Quote object gains a per-line pending mechanic, not a new top-level state.** A Quote can be created in Requested with one or more lines (most commonly the delivery line) left unpriced; the buyer cannot move a Quote to Accepted until every line carries a real value. `Requested` continues to mean exactly what it already means everywhere else on the platform ("the buyer wants this, here's a record of it") — the only change is that a Quote isn't required to be fully priced the instant it's created. This is the mechanism that makes `freight_ltl`/`manual` delivery methods compatible with the existing Quote lifecycle without redefining it.

**Routing: real road geometry, not a distance number, with the map and the routing engine kept as separate concerns.** A map library (e.g. Leaflet) handles the pin-drop/address-search UI; a separate routing engine (OSRM, GraphHopper, Mapbox Directions, or Google Distance Matrix) supplies the actual road-following route geometry and distance. For rural delivery specifically, the routing engine's road-data quality matters more than which map library is used — a self-hosted OSRM instance is free but depends entirely on OpenStreetMap's coverage of remote/lightly-mapped roads, which can be thin in rural service areas; a paid provider (Mapbox Directions or Google Distance Matrix) tends to have materially better rural coverage, at a bounded per-lookup cost since results are cached per pin. The specific provider is left open per listing/deployment rather than fixed platform-wide, but the recommendation for a rural-heavy service area is a paid provider over self-hosted OSRM.

**Buyer-proposed, staff-verified route, per direction.** At intake, the buyer sets the delivery point (pin drop or address) and may additionally drag route waypoints (via a tool such as Leaflet Routing Machine) to show the actual accessible path to their property — they typically know this better than a generic router. That proposed route attaches to the lead/Quote with a status of `buyer_proposed`, not yet treated as final. Before the delivery line can be priced, whoever holds `listing_access` on the relevant listing reviews the proposed route on the map and either approves it as-is (`staff_verified`) or redraws it directly (`staff_corrected`) — there is no negotiation round-trip back to the buyer; staff already holds final authority over the Quote, so correction is direct rather than requested. **Waypoint-dragging only, not freehand route drawing** — dragging still constrains the corrected path to real roads at each segment via the routing engine, while freehand drawing would let a "corrected" distance stop reflecting an actual driveable road, defeating the reason real road-routing exists in the first place. If even waypoint-dragging can't represent a genuinely unusual case, a free-text mileage override is the last resort, not the default tool. Once verified or corrected, the route geometry and resulting distance lock onto the Quote — the same locking behavior already applied to RTO terms snapshots and quote lines elsewhere in this document set — so a later change to the merchant's per-mile rate or the routing provider never silently reprices an existing Quote.

A customer-facing route preview (showing the buyer the path their delivery would travel) is a nice-to-have, not required for the feature to function, and is left as a later polish item rather than part of this decision.

## Consequences

Schema needs, not yet designed (same "not yet schedulable" caveat other structural gaps in this document set carry): a `delivery_method` config per listing/product line (type + its type-specific fields — free radius, per-mile rate, base fee for `flat_distance_rate`; carrier credentials for `carrier_rate`); a route/waypoint geometry field and a verification-status field (`buyer_proposed` / `staff_verified` / `staff_corrected`) on the lead/Quote record, alongside who verified it and when, matching the audit-trail pattern (`decisions/0002`) already required for every other write on the platform; a per-line "priced/pending" flag on Quote lines, and the rule gating Accept until every line is priced.

This resolves SJ Portables FRD's open FR-7.3 item ("delivery pricing past 50 miles, per BRD, pending") structurally — the mechanism now exists — while leaving the actual per-mile rate SJ wants to charge, and the specific routing provider to integrate, genuinely open and merchant/deployment-decided rather than platform-assumed. SJ Portables is this contract's first real instance, the same relationship it has to the External Source contract (`decisions/0039`); any future Market listing needing delivery pricing plugs into the same `delivery_method` concept rather than a bespoke per-listing build.
