# ADR 0015: Claim & Verification Flow — Completing the Four Tiers, plus Dispute Resolution

**Status:** Accepted (2026-09-23), reconciled directly against the real live code (`claim.php`, `listing/api/claim.php`, `api/lib/Verification.php`, `api/lib/TrustScore.php`, `listing/api/vouch.php`, `listing/confirm-referral.php`, `listing/api/my-listings.php`, `listing/businessportal.php`) and the live database export (`entities`, `listing_access`, `entity_referrals`, `verification_attempts`, `connections`, `disputes`). **Revised several times since, same day, per direction:**
1. The original version of item 5 below wired up `search_listings.php`'s existing `PUBLIC_STATUSES` exclusion as-is, which fully hides a disputed listing from search the instant anyone files against it — flagged in Consequences as a real abuse surface rather than resolved. It's resolved: disputed listings stay visible, at a reverted "limited display" (see item 5).
2. Even limited-display is a real consequence for a legitimate business, so item 5 now gates *whether filing immediately triggers it* behind a standing check on the filer — see "Precursor check" below. This also surfaced a directly relevant piece of real project history worth knowing about regardless of this ADR: the master platform spec already tried, and explicitly dropped, a domain-email-match verification mechanism for a different purpose (Tier 1), for reasons that apply just as much here — see Context.
3. Phone verification, sketched below as future work pending SMS/telephony infrastructure, is unblocked the same day: `decisions/0016` builds it as a manual admin action instead of an automated one — see the "Precursor check" section's updated phone note.
4. Document upload for Tier 4 intake, deliberately deferred in item 6 below pending Phase 7's reviewing side existing, is unblocked the same day too — `decisions/0016` is that reviewing side, and `decisions/0017` builds the real upload against it. See item 6.
5. **Per direction: the dispute process gets a real, two-tier clock instead of a single 7-day backstop.** The existing 7-day `challenge_deadline` stays as the hard outer ceiling, but a new 48-hour first-response window now sits inside it — the claimant has to actually respond (re-verify or submit evidence), not just sit inside a 7-day grace period with no pressure to act. See the revised "Filing also" and "Resolution states" below.
6. **Per direction, three real questions answered together: guests can't dispute (confirmed, already true), claim-time association is knowingly unverified (a deliberate, platform-wide pattern, not an oversight), and multi-user businesses get a real answer via a new `decisions/0018`.** See the tightened "Who can file" bullet, a new Context bullet on claim-time association, and the "owns or manages" broadening across items 3 and 6 below. Building this out surfaced a real bug worth naming plainly: the document-upload endpoint (`decisions/0017`) only let a dispute's *filer* attach evidence, not the claimant defending against it — exactly backwards from what this ADR's own 48-hour response language promises the claimant they can do. Fixed in `decisions/0017`, cross-referenced here.
7. **Reversed, per direct correction: a dispute now genuinely freezes editing, not just public display.** The "what filing does not do" bullet below previously said filing doesn't freeze the claimant's editing access — that's no longer accurate, on direct instruction. See the rewritten bullet, the new auto-lift conditions, and a new `disputes.status` value (`claim_abandoned`) for the one place this introduces an automated status change without an admin's click — flagged plainly as the one deliberate, narrow exception to this document set's otherwise-uniform HITL rule, and why it's a safe one.

## Context

The claim/verification backend is, unusually, the most mature real code in the whole platform — far ahead of registration. Confirmed real and already documented in `architecture.md` §9:

- **The claim step itself is a two-step OTP** (`listing/api/claim.php`, actions `start`/`confirm`): it re-proves the claimant controls their own account (an email OTP to their own registered address, via `StepUpAuth`), then transactionally locks the target `entities` row, checks it's still `status = 'unclaimed'`, and sets `owner_user_id` + `status = 'claimed-unverified'`. **This step verifies the account, not the business** — the code's own comments are explicit that claiming and verifying are deliberately separate events.
- **Direct answer to "how do we know the claimant is actually associated with the listing": at claim time, we don't — and that's confirmed as a deliberate platform-wide pattern, not a gap specific to this ADR.** The master platform spec states this outright: "Claiming is frictionless/un-gated — opens editing immediately under `claimed-unverified`," explicitly confirmed there as the same "ship first, self-correct after" philosophy applied everywhere else on this platform (zero-friction crawler ingestion that publishes before review, frictionless public opt-out) — not an oversight unique to claiming. The real assurance isn't at the door; it's in what happens next: `claimed-unverified` is a real, visibly different status from `verified` (`is_verified` stays `false`, no badge, no claim of legitimacy beyond "someone with an account clicked claim"), and the actual backstop for a wrongly-claimed listing is the dispute mechanism (item 5 below) — which is exactly why that mechanism needing real teeth (the precursor check, the two-tier clock, document/phone evidence) matters as much as it does. This ADR doesn't change the frictionless-claim design — it's called out here so the trade-off is explicit rather than assumed, and so it's clear the dispute flow isn't a nice-to-have alongside claiming, it's the other half of the same decision.
- **All four verification tiers are real, working, independent logic** in `Verification.php`: Tier 1 self-confirm (a dedicated post-claim OTP, distinct from the claim OTP), Tier 2 referrals (`entity_referrals`, 3 confirmed referrals from named registered users), Tier 3 vouchers (`connections` `VERIFIED_BY` edges from a claimant's *other*, already-Tier-2+-verified listings — Tier 1-only listings are explicitly blocked from vouching, to stop the cheapest tier laundering trust into Tier 3), and Tier 4 admin override. Each independently sets `status = 'verified'`; `trust_score` bonuses (`TrustScore::awardVerificationTier`) reflect *how* a listing got verified and never gate *whether* it did.
- **What's real backend but has no real front door:** `claim.php` (the page) is an explicit, labeled placeholder — "PLACEHOLDER... The form is a disabled mock: nothing is submitted." There is no working UI to trigger Tier 1's post-claim OTP, no UI to name referrers for Tier 2 (the dashboard only *displays* referral/voucher progress, read-only, via `my-listings.php`), and no UI to pick a vouching listing for Tier 3 (`vouch.php` is a complete, real endpoint with no caller). `listing/confirm-referral.php` (the page a referred person lands on) already calls a `POST /listing/api/referral/confirm.php` contract that doesn't exist anywhere in the export — the same "front end assumes a backend that was never finished" pattern as registration's confirmation email. **`routes.md` currently lists `listing/api/verify/tier1.php` as Live — it isn't in this export; corrected below.**
- **`listing_access` is never written by any of this.** Every claim and every verification tier operates on `entities.owner_user_id` alone — the real, live capability-grant table added in `decisions/0012` isn't populated by the one code path (claiming) that should be creating its rows.
- **The `disputes` table is pure schema — zero code anywhere reads or writes it — but `entities.status` already has a real `'disputed'` value waiting for it.** `entities.status` is `ENUM('unclaimed','claimed-unverified','pending-verification','verified','disputed','pending-opt-out','opted-out')`, and `api/search_listings.php`'s `PUBLIC_STATUSES` whitelist already deliberately excludes `disputed` (and the opt-out states) from public search results, with a comment saying so. So the intended mechanism is already half-built at the schema/read layer — a disputed listing is meant to drop out of public search — it's only the write side (something actually setting `status = 'disputed'` and managing the `disputes` row) that's completely missing. Separately: a business's real owner, finding their listing already claimed by someone else, has no path today — `status = 'claimed-unverified'` is a terminal state as far as the codebase is concerned unless the claimant abandons it themselves.
- **Tier 4's consuming side is genuinely Phase 7** (`Verification::applyAdminVerification`'s own doc-comment says so) — there is no admin panel yet to call it, on top of no claimant-facing way to ask for it.
- **Directly relevant real history, found while designing the precursor check below: domain-email verification was already tried once on this platform, and explicitly dropped.** The master platform spec documents it in full: Tier 1 originally worked by matching a claimant's email domain against the listing's own `website` field, then a second "domain-ownership-challenge" redesign was tried too — both were "fully dropped," in the spec's own words, as "unnecessary complexity ('chasing our tail') given the platform's actual user base — many businesses will use Traversence's own hosted profile as their web presence rather than maintaining a separate site... and requiring domain literacy... conflicted with the platform's actual posture of giving businesses free access to their own data." Tier 1 became the simpler owner-triggered account-email OTP it is today instead, and `website` "reverts to ordinary owner-editable data with no special restriction." **The real database export backs this up empirically**: of the sampled real entities, `phone` is populated on 63 of 70 — `website` isn't populated on any of them; there is no `website` meta_key present in the live data at all today, even though the column/key is still valid schema. A phone-based alternative has real data to work with that a domain-based one doesn't, but per the same spec (Section 20.2's own build-sequencing note), it's deliberately unbuilt too: "domain-based OTP" (their term for a phone-equivalent self-serve OTP) "needs a real SMS/telephony provider integration" this codebase doesn't have anywhere yet.

## Decision

### 1. The claim page becomes real, replacing the disabled mock
`claim.php` is wired to the real `listing/api/claim.php` `start`/`confirm` contract exactly as that endpoint already expects it: enter email (pre-filled from the session) → request code → enter code → on success, land on the listing's new `claimed-unverified` state with a clear next step ("choose how to verify"), rather than a static confirmation message. No changes to the already-correct backend OTP logic — this is a front-end wiring fix, the same shape as `decisions/0014`'s registration fix.

### 2. Tier 2 (referrals) gets its missing endpoints
Two new endpoints, matching the schema and the UI copy already committed to elsewhere (`architecture.md` §9: "Claimant names up to 3 registered users; each must actively click their own confirmation link"):

- **`POST /listing/api/referral/create.php`** — claimant submits up to 3 email addresses. For each, the server looks up a matching `users` row; **the response never confirms or denies whether a given email matched** (the same anti-enumeration posture `Auth::register()` already uses for duplicate emails) — it always replies "if this is a registered user, they'll receive a confirmation link." Behind that generic response: a match creates an `entity_referrals` row (`entity_id`, `referrer_user_id`, `requested_by_user_id` = the claimant, a fresh `confirmation_token`, a 10-minute-class `confirmation_expires_at` matching the platform's existing OTP/token expiry convention) and emails the referrer a link into the already-real `listing/confirm-referral.php` page. A non-match creates nothing and sends nothing. At most 3 *pending or confirmed* referral rows may exist per entity at once, to match the "names up to 3" ceiling already documented.
- **`POST /listing/api/referral/confirm.php`** — the endpoint `confirm-referral.php` already calls. Resolves the token (unexpired, unconfirmed), requires the confirming request to come from an authenticated session belonging to that exact `referrer_user_id` (closing the gap the current page doesn't check: today's page will happily fire the request from anyone in possession of the link, whether or not they're logged in as the named referrer at all — a link alone shouldn't be sufficient to vouch as someone else). Sets `confirmed_at`, then calls the already-real `Verification::checkReferralThreshold()`.

### 3. Tier 1 and Tier 3 get their missing UI, no backend changes
- **Tier 1:** a "Verify this listing" action on `businessportal.php` for any `claimed-unverified` listing the user owns **or manages** (`listing_access` — broadened 2026-09-24, per direction, see `decisions/0018`), triggering `StepUpAuth::issue(..., 'tier1_verify', ...)` → an OTP email → a confirm step → `listing/api/verify/tier1.php` (built now; `routes.md`'s claim that this already exists is corrected) calling the already-real `Verification::applyTier1SelfConfirm()`.
- **Tier 3:** on the same dashboard, a "Vouch for another listing" action offering a dropdown of the current user's *other* listings with `status = 'verified'` and `verified_tier >= 2` (the exact eligibility `vouch.php` already enforces server-side) — the UI simply exposes the choice `vouch.php` already validates; no new endpoint needed.

### 4. Claiming now writes `listing_access`, not just `owner_user_id`
`listing/api/claim.php`'s `confirm` action, inside its existing transaction, now also inserts a `listing_access` row (`entity_id`, `user_id` = claimant, `access_level = 'owner'`, `granted_by_user_id` = the claimant themself — a self-granted initial owner record, consistent with how `admin_access`'s own bootstrap is a self-service `INSERT` too) alongside the existing `owner_user_id` write. This doesn't touch any other call site — `entities.owner_user_id` stays exactly as load-bearing as it is today — it just stops the one code path that *creates* ownership from writing only to the column that's supposed to be superseded. Existing claims made before this change still need their own backfill pass, tracked separately (this ADR only stops the hole from getting deeper).

### 5. Dispute & challenge flow (new — closes the real `disputes` gap)
- **Who can file — confirmed per direction: a guest genuinely cannot, and it's enforced server-side, not just described in prose.** Any registered, email-confirmed user (not a guest) may file one dispute against a listing that is `claimed-unverified` or `verified`, submitting a reason, via a new `POST /api/disputes/file.php` (global, not `listing/api/`, since the filer typically holds no `listing_access` grant on the target — matching the real folder-per-domain convention the platform spec itself documents: domain-specific endpoints under `listing/api/`, cross-cutting platform concerns under global `api/`). This endpoint sits behind the platform's real, already-existing `require_auth.php` guard (`architecture.md`'s documented middleware) — a guest session carries only a `guest_session_id`, never a real `actor_user_id`, and is rejected before the handler runs at all — plus an explicit `email_verified_at IS NOT NULL` check on top, since being logged in alone isn't the same bar as being email-confirmed. Filing is never something a guest submission (§7, §11's guest-boundary rules) can reach, by the same structural wall that keeps every other guest input out of the live graph. At most one `open` dispute per entity at a time — a second attempt while one is already open is rejected, not queued, to avoid pile-ons. `disputes.filed_by_user_id` stays nullable specifically for the one other real source of a dispute: an Administrator opening one directly (e.g., escalated from an `accuracy_reports` row or an external complaint) without a specific filer.
- **Precursor check, added per direction: filing is never blocked, but *automatically triggering limited display* is gated behind the filer clearing one of two standing checks.** A concern should never be silently dropped just because the filer can't clear a bar — so anyone eligible above can always file. What's gated is the *immediate consequence*:
  - **Filer standing (buildable today, the default path)** — the filer's own account must have `trust_score >= 0` (reusing `TrustScore.php`'s own already-established review-queue predicate, not a new number) and have been email-confirmed for at least 72 hours (borrowing the exact figure the platform spec's own earlier, never-built dispute sketch already used for its challenge window, for a little internal consistency rather than inventing a fresh number). Clearing this auto-flips `entities.status = 'disputed'` immediately, exactly as below.
  - **Domain match (opportunistic fast path, only when the data exists)** — if the target entity has a non-empty `website` on file, the filer can instead prove standing by completing a one-time OTP to an address at that same domain (reusing `StepUpAuth`/`Mailer.php`'s existing delivery infra — no new integration — and the `'domain_match'` value already provisioned in `verification_attempts.method`). This is **explicitly not the primary or only gate** — per the real history above, `website` isn't populated on any of the sampled real listings today, so requiring it universally would make the fast path unusable for most of the platform, the identical mistake the platform's own build history already reversed once for Tier 1. It's offered as an accelerant when it happens to be available (larger or paid-tier businesses more likely to have a separate site), not the default expectation.
  - **Neither cleared → admin triage first, not silent drop.** The `disputes` row is still created (`status = 'open'`), but `entities.status` is left untouched — display stays exactly as it was. The filing instead lands directly in `admin/claims.php`'s queue (`decisions/0016`) as a third row-type, distinct from Tier 2's retroactive audits and Tier 4's intake requests, for a human first look. An admin who finds it substantive is the one who then flips `entities.status = 'disputed'` themselves, at which point the same `challenge_deadline`/re-verify process below runs exactly as if the filer had cleared a fast path. **Revised, 2026-09-23: phone verification of the listed number is no longer purely future work waiting on SMS/telephony infrastructure.** `decisions/0016` builds it now, manually — the same admin triaging this row gets a "Called listed number" field (`phone_verified: true|false|null` plus a note) as part of the same decision, giving them real evidence before they escalate or dismiss, rather than judging the written `reason` alone. This is explicitly the interim form of what was originally sketched here as a future automated fast path — real listings have `phone` populated far more often than `website`, so it's the stronger long-term direction once a real telephony integration is actually affordable; for now it's a human placing the call instead of a provider, which costs no new infrastructure and is available immediately.
- **Editing freeze — reversed 2026-09-24, per direct correction.** This ADR originally said filing doesn't freeze the claimant's editing access. It does now: the moment `entities.status` actually flips to `disputed`, the claimant's ability to edit the listing's content — description, photos, `entity_metadata` generally — is suspended, on top of the already-existing limited public display. The reasoning for the reversal: since claiming grants real edit control the instant someone clicks claim, with zero proof of association (see the new Context bullet above), an illegitimate claimant keeping full edit rights *while actively disputed* is a real risk — they could keep altering content, adding fake material, or covering their tracks for the entire week the case is open. **What stays open regardless of the freeze — this is the part that has to survive, or the whole due-process design collapses:** the specific actions that let the claimant actually defend themselves — Tier 1/Tier 3 re-verification (item 3) and document upload (`decisions/0017`) — are explicitly exempt from the freeze. A dispute is an allegation, not a finding, so locking out the ability to *respond* to it (as opposed to *editing public content* during it) would make disputes themselves a griefing tool, which is the one part of the original reasoning that still holds.
- **Three ways the freeze lifts, made concrete, per direction:**
  1. **The defense validates.** The claimant reaches `verified_tier >= 3` while the dispute is open — the existing `claimant_reverified` path below — and the freeze lifts the instant that happens, same moment full display resumes.
  2. **The claimant goes silent for 48 hours — the one deliberate, narrow exception to this document set's otherwise-uniform HITL rule.** If `response_deadline` (item above) passes with `claimant_responded_at` still `NULL`, the listing auto-reverts to a real `unclaimed` state — not just "flagged unresponsive" as before, but an actual, automatic status change with no admin click required. This is a genuine, narrow departure from `decisions/0005`'s "no automated consequence without a human sign-off" rule everywhere else in this document set, and it's worth being explicit about why it's safe here specifically: reverting to `unclaimed` is the *same* low-stakes, no-penalty, freely-reclaimable state this platform already treats as its default safe fallback everywhere — it carries no trust penalty and doesn't hand the listing to the disputer (same principle as an admin-driven revocation below). The one thing that stays strictly human-gated is the *punitive* consequence: a trust-score penalty only ever applies when an admin actively finds a claim illegitimate (`claim_revoked`, below), never from silence alone — silence is evidence of abandonment, not proof of fraud, and shouldn't be treated as the latter. This gets its own resolution state, `claim_abandoned`, distinct from `claim_revoked`, precisely so the audit trail doesn't conflate "went quiet" with "an admin found this fraudulent."
  3. **An admin dismisses the dispute.** The existing dismiss/resolve path (below) lifts the freeze immediately, restoring the listing to whatever status it held before the dispute.
- **What filing does — revised, 2026-09-23, twice:** when the precursor check above is cleared, it sets `entities.status = 'disputed'` (a real, pre-existing enum value — this ADR doesn't invent it), but **`disputed` is added to `search_listings.php`'s `PUBLIC_STATUSES` whitelist rather than left excluded from it.** The listing stays visible. What changes is *what's shown*: a disputed entity is rendered at the same **limited display an unclaimed, crawler-prepopulated listing already gets** — `is_claimed` and `is_verified` both force to `false` regardless of the entity's actual `owner_user_id`/`verified_tier`, and only the baseline crawler-sourced fields (name, address, category, prepopulated phone) are returned, not any owner-supplied `entity_metadata` (custom description, photos, `ai_enhanced_summary`) or paid `plan_tier` display perks. This is a pure display-time rule keyed off `status = 'disputed'` — no new column, no separate freeze/unfreeze step to remember, since the moment `status` changes again (to `'verified'`, `'claimed-unverified'`, or `'unclaimed'` — see resolution states below) the richer display resumes automatically, computed fresh from whatever's actually stored. **Deliberately no public "disputed" badge or label** — an unproven allegation shouldn't carry a visible stigma; from a visitor's side, a disputed listing simply looks exactly like it hasn't been claimed yet, nothing more pointed than that. This replaces the original version of this ADR's decision to fully exclude `disputed` from search (see Status above) — that version's own flagged consequence (a bad-faith dispute could disappear a business entirely, at zero cost to the filer, before any admin looked at it) is what this revision closes.
- **Filing also:** sets the `disputes` row's own `status = 'open'` and adds the case to the admin review queue (reusing the existing `verification_attempts`-style pending-queue pattern rather than inventing a second one). The moment `entities.status` actually flips to `disputed` — immediately, if the filer cleared the precursor check above, or later, at the moment an admin escalates an untriaged filing — two clocks start together, both on the `disputes` row: `challenge_deadline = NOW() + 7 days` (the hard outer ceiling — unchanged in value, but now a deliberate SLA target per direction, not just a placeholder number) and a new `response_deadline = NOW() + 48 hours` (a first-response window inside it, added per direction so the process has real pressure to move, not just a distant backstop). The current claimant is emailed at that same moment: a challenge has been filed, they have 48 hours to respond (re-verify at Tier 3+, or submit supporting evidence via `decisions/0017`'s document upload) and 7 days total before the case is decided either way.
- **The 48-hour clock, made concrete — revised 2026-09-24 to actually resolve the case, not just flag it.** A new `disputes.claimant_responded_at` (nullable) is cleared to `NULL` every time `response_deadline` is set or reset, and populated the moment the claimant does one of the two things that count as a real response: uploads a supporting document against this dispute (`decisions/0017`), or reaches `verified_tier >= 3` while the dispute is open (which, per the resolution states below, closes the whole thing outright in the claimant's favor rather than merely "counting" toward it). **If `NOW()` passes `response_deadline` with `claimant_responded_at` still `NULL`, per direction: the case now auto-resolves as `claim_abandoned`** (see the freeze section above and the new resolution state below) — `entities.status` reverts to real `unclaimed`, the editing freeze is moot since there's no active claimant left, and no trust penalty applies. This is the one automated, admin-click-free status change in this whole ADR, deliberately scoped to a non-punitive, freely-reversible outcome — the punitive consequence (`claim_revoked`, with its trust penalty) stays exactly as human-gated as before. **When an admin uses the "Request more info" action** (`decisions/0017`, on either the document or the underlying request) on an open dispute, the clock resets: `response_deadline = min(NOW() + 48 hours, challenge_deadline)` — capped so a chain of "request more info" cycles can never quietly extend the case past the real 7-day ceiling — and `claimant_responded_at` clears again, giving the claimant a fresh, but still bounded, window before the same auto-abandonment logic would otherwise fire again.
- **Resolution states, made concrete:**
  - `open` → **`claimant_reverified`** — the current claimant reaches `verified_tier >= 3` (voucher or admin) *while the dispute is open*, automatically closing it in their favor and restoring `entities.status = 'verified'` — full display resumes immediately, the same read-time computation that limited it now shows the real data again. Tier 1/2 re-verification is deliberately not sufficient to close a dispute, since Tier 2's own referral system already carries the platform's explicit "auto-flag for retroactive admin review" caveat as weaker evidence — a live *disputed* claim needs the stronger tiers, not the same weak evidence being disputed.
  - `open` → **`claim_revoked`** — an Administrator actively finds the claim illegitimate (whether before or after `challenge_deadline` passes). This stays fully human-gated, unchanged by the 48-hour auto-abandonment path below: passing the 7-day deadline with no admin action does **not** auto-revoke — consistent with the HITL principle already governing every other automated-consequence path on this platform (`decisions/0005`) — it only moves the case to "deadline passed, awaiting a required admin decision" in that same queue; the listing stays `disputed` (editing frozen, limited display, but never hidden) until that decision lands. On revocation: `entities.owner_user_id` is cleared, `status` reverts to `'unclaimed'`, `verified_tier` is cleared, the `listing_access` owner row from item 4 above is deleted, and the (former) claimant's account takes a flat fraudulent-claim trust penalty via `TrustScore::adjustUserTrust()` (a fixed penalty, not an attempt to precisely reverse whatever verification bonus they'd been awarded — reconstructing an exact reversal isn't something `TrustScore`'s award function was built to support, and a flat penalty is more defensible than an approximation dressed up as precise). **Revocation never auto-grants the disputer the listing** — the listing simply returns to `unclaimed` (which, display-wise, looks identical to what it was already showing while `disputed`) and anyone, disputer included, has to claim and verify it through the normal path. This is deliberate: it removes any incentive to file a dispute purely to skip the real claim process.
  - `open` → **`claim_abandoned`** — new, added 2026-09-24, per direction. The claimant never responds within the 48-hour `response_deadline` (see above): `entities.owner_user_id` is cleared, `status` reverts to `'unclaimed'`, `verified_tier` is cleared, and the `listing_access` owner row is deleted — the same mechanical cleanup `claim_revoked` performs — but **automatically, with no admin click, and with no trust penalty.** This is deliberately a distinct state from `claim_revoked`, not a reuse of it: silence is evidence of abandonment, not a finding of fraud, and the audit trail should be able to tell the two apart later. Same as revocation, this never auto-grants the disputer the listing.
  - `open` → **`resolved`** — the catch-all closing state for anything that isn't the outcomes above (the disputer withdraws, an admin decides some other resolution such as directing both parties toward `listing_access` manager/owner coexistence, etc.); `entities.status` returns to whatever it was before the dispute (`claimed-unverified` or `verified`) unless the admin's resolution says otherwise, and display and editing access both follow automatically.

### 6. Tier 4 intake, without the Phase 7 admin panel
A "Request manual review" action on `businessportal.php`, available for any `claimed-unverified` listing the user owns **or manages** (broadened 2026-09-24, same as item 3, see `decisions/0018`) regardless of Tiers 1–3 progress (copy nudges toward the faster tiers first, but doesn't gate the request behind them). Submits a short free-text justification plus a contact phone number, and creates a `verification_attempts` row (`method = 'admin_override'`, `status = 'pending'`) — reusing the exact queuing mechanism `Verification::checkReferralThreshold()` already uses for its own auto-flagged rows, so Phase 7's eventual admin panel needs one query surface for all pending manual-review cases, not two. Document upload was deliberately **not** built as part of this pass — it needed its own secure-storage design (outside the webroot, access-controlled) that made more sense once Phase 7's reviewing side actually existed to consume it. **That precondition is now met: `decisions/0016` is that reviewing side, and `decisions/0017` (same day) builds the real upload** — a claimant can now attach a supporting document (utility bill, business license, etc.) directly to this request, stored outside the webroot and viewable only by the hub admin reviewing it, with the admin's monitored-inbox email fallback above kept only as a backstop, not the default path.

## Consequences

`routes.md` needs `/claim` updated from "no public page yet" to reflect the real wiring above, and its `listing/api/verify/tier1.php` row corrected from "Live" to "built as part of this ADR" — it wasn't actually present in the live export despite the prior row's claim. New rows are needed for the referral-create/confirm endpoints and the dispute-filing entry point.

Item 4 (`listing_access` on claim) means `entities.owner_user_id` and `listing_access` will diverge for any listing claimed *before* this ADR ships versus after — worth flagging the same way `decisions/0014`'s login-gate change flagged its own pre-existing-account edge case, so whoever ships this also plans the backfill pass rather than assuming it's implied.

The dispute flow's `challenge_deadline` (7 days total) and `response_deadline` (48 hours to first respond, added 2026-09-24 per direction to keep the process moving rather than let cases sit for a week with no pressure) are stated as deliberate SLA targets, not just first defaults — but their exact numbers are still not researched thresholds, and the filing-eligibility rule (one open dispute per entity, registered users only) remains one too. All are reasonable starting points to revisit once real dispute volume exists to tune against, the same posture `decisions/0009` already takes toward its own soft/hard-action thresholds.

Tier 4's intake acceptance criteria (what actually justifies manual review) is intentionally left to admin judgment rather than specified here, since the reviewing side that would apply any such criteria doesn't exist yet (Phase 7) — this ADR only guarantees the request lands somewhere real and queryable when that panel is finally built.

**Resolved (2026-09-23, per direction): the dispute-visibility abuse surface.** The original version of this ADR flagged that reusing `search_listings.php`'s existing `PUBLIC_STATUSES` exclusion meant a single unreviewed accusation could take a business off the map entirely, for free, before any admin looked at it. That's closed now: `disputed` is a visible status with reverted, limited display (item 5) rather than an excluded one — a bad-faith filer can cost a legitimate business its polish (photos, description, claimed/verified badges, paid-tier perks) for the `challenge_deadline` window, but never its presence in search. What's still a first default, not a researched threshold, going forward: the exact 7-day/48-hour numbers, and whether limited display is *enough* deterrent against pure harassment filing (someone who doesn't care about visibility, only about denying a competitor its badges, still gets that for the cost of one filing) — worth revisiting once real dispute volume exists to tune against, same posture `decisions/0009` already takes toward its own thresholds.

**Added 2026-09-24, per direction: a real clock on the whole process.** The 7-day `challenge_deadline` had been sitting alone as the only time pressure in the flow — a claimant technically had a full week with nothing pushing them to actually do anything before that final deadline. A new 48-hour `response_deadline`, nested inside the same 7 days, now requires an actual response (a re-verification or a submitted document) rather than passive waiting, and resets — capped at the 7-day ceiling — every time an admin asks for more information, so a case can't quietly stall through repeated "request more info" cycles. Passing the 48-hour window with no response never auto-decides anything, matching the exact same HITL posture the 7-day deadline already holds — it only pushes the case to the front of the admin queue, since a claimant who's gone quiet on a live complaint is a meaningfully different situation than one who's actively responding. This makes "fast and efficient" a real, enforced property of the process rather than just a description of the outer bound.

**Reversed 2026-09-24, per direct correction: disputes now freeze content editing, not just public display, and a 48-hour silence auto-resolves the case.** This ADR previously stated plainly that filing a dispute doesn't freeze the claimant's editing access — that's no longer the design. The reasoning: since claiming grants real edit control the instant someone clicks claim with zero proof of business association (see the new Context bullet on this), letting an actively-disputed claimant keep full content-editing rights for up to a week is a real risk this platform didn't need to carry. The freeze is scoped narrowly — the specific defense actions (Tier 1/3 re-verification, document upload) stay available throughout, or the due-process design this whole ADR is built around collapses — and it lifts through exactly three paths: the defense validates (`claimant_reverified`), an admin dismisses, or the claimant goes silent for the full 48-hour `response_deadline`, which now auto-resolves the case as a new `claim_abandoned` state rather than sitting in a queue waiting for a human look. That last path is a genuine, narrow exception to `decisions/0005`'s "no automated consequence without a human sign-off" rule, applied everywhere else in this document set — it's deliberately scoped to be safe: reverting to `unclaimed` carries no trust penalty and hands the listing to no one, the same low-stakes default state this platform already treats as harmless everywhere else. The one thing that stays strictly human-gated is the punitive consequence — a trust-score penalty only ever follows an admin's actual finding of fraud (`claim_revoked`), never from silence alone. `decisions/0016`'s earlier note about unresponsive disputes getting queue priority is superseded by this — there's no longer a row left to prioritize once the case auto-resolves. `decisions/0017`'s retention rule is updated to purge documents on a `claim_abandoned` resolution the same as any other. `routes.md` and `architecture.md` §9/§11 need the editing-freeze behavior reflected on `listing/businessportal.php`'s row.

**Added 2026-09-24, per direction: three real questions, answered rather than assumed.** Guests were already structurally unable to file a dispute — confirmed here explicitly, with the actual enforcement named (`require_auth.php` plus an `email_verified_at` check), not just described. Claim-time association is knowingly, deliberately unverified — the master spec itself confirms "frictionless/un-gated" claiming as an intentional platform-wide pattern, not a gap — which is precisely why this ADR's dispute mechanism carries the real weight of catching a wrong claim after the fact, not before. And "what if two people work for the same business" gets a real answer in new `decisions/0018`: `listing_access` already reserved a `manager` access level that nothing wrote to until now — an owner can invite a colleague, and Tier 1/Tier 4 actions (items 3 and 6 above) now check for owner-or-manager access rather than sole ownership. Building that out surfaced a real, fixed bug: `decisions/0017`'s document-upload endpoint only let a dispute's filer attach evidence, never the claimant defending against it — backwards from what this ADR's own "submit supporting evidence" language already promised the claimant. `decisions/0017` is corrected to check `listing_access` on the disputed entity, not just `disputes.filed_by_user_id`.
