# ADR 0019: DMCA Notice-and-Takedown — a Distinct Flow from Ownership Disputes

**Status:** Proposed, not Accepted — the technical mechanism below is ready to build, but two real, non-technical prerequisites (flagged plainly in Consequences) sit outside what this document set can settle on its own, and this ADR shouldn't be read as "safe harbor achieved" until those are done. **Revised 2026-09-24, per direction, four times same day:** (1) retention changed from "several years, not fixed here" to permanent/indefinite, per direct instruction, grounded in the 3-year copyright statute of limitations (17 U.S.C. § 507(b)) as the evidentiary-defense rationale; (2) the designated-agent Consequences item gets the actual registration procedure and fee structure (online directory, $6 per legal entity covering all alternate names/domains/DBAs, public display requirement, 3-year renewal cycle); (3) that same item gets the concrete step-by-step registration process at `dmca.copyright.gov` (account creation, required Primary + Secondary contacts, "Add Service Provider," checkout) — still a real action for you to actually go do, not something this document set performs on its own, but no longer left vague; (4) a new §5, **Registration & renewal management** — per your direct point that a real, recurring legal-compliance obligation like this shouldn't rely purely on someone remembering an external three-year date, the platform itself now tracks the registration (a new `dmca_agent_registration` table), gives a super_admin a real page to view/edit it (`admin/dmca-settings.php`), serves the publicly-required agent contact info from that table instead of hardcoded page copy, and reuses the existing daily-cron pattern to flag and email super_admins as the renewal date approaches — a second, internal layer of reminder on top of (not instead of) the Copyright Office's own Secondary Contact requirement.

## Context

`decisions/0015`'s `disputes` table answers a different question than this one: "is this person really the owner of this business." Copyright/IP takedown asks a different question entirely: "does specific content on this platform infringe someone else's copyrighted work" — and it comes with its own federal statute (17 U.S.C. § 512), not a policy choice this platform gets to design freely the way the ownership-dispute flow was. Per direction, laid out precisely: a valid DMCA notice needs six specific statutory elements, a facially-valid notice obligates expeditious removal, the accused gets a counter-notice right with a mandatory 10–14 business day standoff before restoration, and — easy to miss, stated in the framework but worth calling out explicitly since it's not obvious — **maintaining a repeat-infringer policy isn't optional flavor, it's a legal precondition for safe harbor at all** (§512(i)). None of this exists anywhere in the platform today; there is no DMCA intake path, no counter-notice mechanism, and no repeat-infringer tracking.

This also isn't the same shape of problem as `disputes`. An ownership dispute targets `entities.status`/`owner_user_id` — the whole listing's claim. A copyright complaint almost always targets one specific piece of content — a photo, a description — not the listing's ownership. Targeting the whole entity for a single infringing photo would be a far blunter, more disproportionate action than the statute requires, and this platform already has the field-level precision to do better: `entity_metadata`'s per-field structure (`decisions/0012`) is exactly the granularity a takedown should act on.

**Automated Content ID-style fingerprinting (the fourth item in the framework provided) is explicitly out of scope, and worth saying why rather than silently dropping it.** That technology exists to handle upload volume no manual process could review — YouTube/TikTok scale. This platform has roughly 4,376 listings total (§18) and its ingestion pipeline already routes scraped content through a human-reviewed staging queue before anything publishes (`decisions/0005`'s HITL guardrail) — a real, existing safeguard against inadvertently publishing infringing scraped material that most platforms this size don't have at all. Building fingerprinting infrastructure here would be solving a scale problem this platform doesn't have.

## Decision

**A separate mechanism from `disputes`, reusing infrastructure already built rather than duplicating it, and — where the statute itself dictates the process — deliberately less discretionary than everything else in this document set.**

### 1. Intake: the form does the statutory compliance check, not a human
A new public form, `/dmca-notice.php` → `POST /api/dmca/file.php`, structurally requires all six elements before it can even be submitted — this handles "check for missing elements" as ordinary form validation rather than a manual completeness review:
- Identification of the copyrighted work.
- The specific target: an `entity_id` and, where applicable, the specific `entity_metadata` row (photo, description) — not just "this listing," matching the field-level precision above. A notice that can't be matched to real content on the platform is rejected outright with a specific reason, the same "reject and request correction" posture the framework describes.
- The complainant's contact information.
- A good-faith-belief statement (a checkbox plus required text, not just a checkbox — the statute wants a statement, not an acknowledgment).
- A statement under penalty of perjury that the information is accurate and the filer is authorized to act — **a typed full legal name is a legally valid electronic signature for this purpose under the statute; no signature-pad or e-signature vendor is needed**, worth stating plainly so this doesn't get over-built.
- Optional supporting documentation (proof of copyright registration, a screenshot of the original work) via `decisions/0017`'s existing upload endpoint — `verification_documents.related_type` gains a `dmca_notice` value rather than this ADR inventing a second upload path.

A new table, `dmca_notices`: `id`, `entity_id`, `metadata_id` (nullable — null means the whole listing is the target), `filer_name`, `filer_contact`, `work_description`, `good_faith_statement`, `perjury_signature`, `status` ENUM (`received`, `removed`, `counter_noticed`, `restored`, `litigation_hold`, `invalid`), `received_at`, `removed_at`, `counter_notice_at`, `restore_eligible_at`, `resolved_at`.

### 2. Removal: fast execution, not a merits review — a deliberately different posture from everything else in this document set
**This is the one place in this platform's governance where less human deliberation, not more, is the correct and legally-preferred design** — worth flagging explicitly since it looks, at first glance, like it contradicts `decisions/0005`'s HITL principle. It doesn't: the statute doesn't ask the platform to judge whether the claim is *true* — the perjury statement shifts that liability onto the filer — it asks the platform to act *expeditiously* on a *facially complete* notice. So the admin step here (`admin/dmca.php`, **super_admin only** — a deliberate, different access scope from every other admin action in this document set, reasoned about below) is narrow and fast: confirm the notice targets real content, then execute removal (the specific `entity_metadata` row is suppressed, or, if `metadata_id` is null, the whole entity reverts to baseline crawler-display, the same limited-display mechanism `decisions/0015` already built for disputes — reused, not reinvented). It is not a review of whether the underlying copyright claim has merit; that's exactly what a court decides, and exactly why the platform isn't supposed to try.
**Why super_admin only, not hub-scoped like every other action in this document set:** a regional admin's hub scoping matches geography, which has nothing to do with copyright law. Centralizing DMCA execution with the smaller, more consistent super_admin group reduces the chance of a regional admin misapplying an unfamiliar legal process, and concentrates the platform's real legal exposure with the people actually positioned to escalate to counsel when something looks unusual.
The affected `listing_access` holder(s) (owner or manager, `decisions/0018`) are notified via `Mailer.php` with a copy of the notice's substantive contents (the filer's identity and claim — the accused needs this to file a meaningful counter-notice), the moment removal executes.

### 3. Repeat-infringer tracking — a legal precondition for safe harbor, not an optional feature
A `dmca_strike_count` on `users`, incremented each time a notice against that user's content resolves as `removed` and is never successfully counter-noticed-and-restored (a restored notice doesn't count as a strike — the content wasn't conclusively found infringing). At a threshold of 3 (matching the common, easily-defensible practice the framework itself named), the account is suspended via the already-real `users.is_suspended` flag — no new enforcement mechanism needed, this reuses what's already load-bearing elsewhere. **This threshold, and the policy itself, has to actually be posted somewhere users can see it (typically Terms of Service) to satisfy "reasonably implemented" under §512(i)** — a real requirement, not a suggestion; see Consequences.

### 4. Counter-notice and the 10–14 business day standoff — the second deliberate exception to this platform's HITL default, and legally mandated rather than a policy choice
`POST /api/dmca/counter-notice.php` — the affected `listing_access` holder submits their own contact details, a consent-to-jurisdiction statement, and a perjury statement of good-faith belief the removal was in error. Sets `dmca_notices.status = 'counter_noticed'`, `counter_notice_at = NOW()`, `restore_eligible_at = NOW() + 10 business days` (the statute's floor; an admin can hold up to 14 if genuinely needed, but restoration is never delayed past 14 without a logged reason), and forwards a copy to the original filer via `Mailer.php` — the platform is a neutral conduit here, not a judge, exactly as the framework describes.
**Restoration is automatic when the window closes, unless an admin has logged a `litigation_hold`.** A daily cron job (the same low-tech scheduling pattern already used for `decisions/0017`'s retention purge) checks for notices past `restore_eligible_at` still in `counter_noticed` status and restores the content — this is the second deliberate, narrow exception to "no automated consequence without a human sign-off" in this document set (the first being `decisions/0015`'s `claim_abandoned` auto-resolution), and it's legally required rather than a convenience: the statute obligates restoration absent a filed lawsuit, and there's no way to automatically detect a federal lawsuit filing, so a `litigation_hold` field exists specifically for a super_admin to set — manually, the moment they actually receive notice of one — as the only thing that suppresses the automatic restore.

### 5. Registration & renewal management — a real system feature, not just an external process to remember
**Added 2026-09-24, per direction: "since dmca is a real thing to manage we should have the super admin have a way to manage it within the system."** Everything above assumes the Copyright Office registration in Consequences item 1 already exists and stays current — but nothing in this platform tracked that, which means its only safeguard against lapsing was a human remembering a three-year-out date, exactly the kind of single point of failure this document set otherwise designs against everywhere else (it's also *why* the Copyright Office's own portal requires a Secondary Contact — even they don't trust one person's memory for a three-year cycle). This closes that gap inside the platform itself, distinct from the external registration process in Consequences item 1, which still has to happen on the Copyright Office's own site — this section is about the platform *tracking and surfacing* that registration, not performing it.

A new table, `dmca_agent_registration`: `id`, `legal_entity_name` (supports the multi-entity case from Consequences item 1 — one row per separately-registered legal entity, though most deployments of this platform will only ever need one), `agent_name`, `agent_address`, `agent_email`, `agent_phone` (the **publicly-displayed** Designated Agent contact — what `/dmca-notice.php` and any other public DMCA-contact surface render, rather than hardcoded page copy), `portal_primary_contact_name`/`_phone`/`_email` (the Copyright Office portal's own account-management contact — may or may not be the same person as the public agent), `portal_secondary_contact_name`/`_phone`/`_email` (same, for the required backup contact), `registered_at`, `last_renewed_at`, `renewal_due_at` (defaults to `registered_at` or `last_renewed_at` + 3 years, but stored explicitly rather than computed on the fly, since the Copyright Office's own renewal window is the actual authority, not this platform's arithmetic), `status` ENUM (`active`, `renewal_due_soon`, `lapsed`), `updated_by_user_id`, `updated_at`.

**`admin/dmca-settings.php` — super_admin only, matching this ADR's own access scoping for everything DMCA-related.** A distinct page from `admin/dmca.php` on purpose: that page reviews and executes individual notices (an operational queue), this one manages the registration record itself (a compliance/configuration surface) — the same "different action, different page" reasoning already applied to keeping `admin/dmca.php` separate from `admin/claims.php`. Lets a super_admin view and edit the current registration row(s), record a completed renewal (which updates `last_renewed_at` and recomputes `renewal_due_at`), and see at a glance how close the next renewal is. `POST /admin/api/dmca/agent-info.php` handles the save, gated by the same `Auth::requireSuperAdmin()` check `admin/dmca.php` itself already uses.

**`/dmca-notice.php` (and any other place the Designated Agent's contact is legally required to be public) reads `agent_name`/`agent_address`/`agent_email`/`agent_phone` from this table live, rather than being hardcoded copy on the page.** This matters beyond convenience: Consequences item 1 requires the public-facing contact info to actually match what's on file with the Copyright Office, and a super_admin editing one place (this table) rather than hunting down every page that mentions the agent is what keeps that true over time instead of drifting the way `decisions/0016`'s stale password-policy copy or `decisions/0014`'s pre-fix registration form did elsewhere in this platform's real history.

**Renewal reminder — reusing the same low-tech daily-cron pattern already established for `decisions/0017`'s purge and this ADR's own restoration cron, not new infrastructure.** The existing daily job gains one more check: any `dmca_agent_registration` row whose `renewal_due_at` is within 90 days (a first default, not a researched threshold, same posture as the 10MB upload cap and the 3-strike repeat-infringer threshold elsewhere in this document set) gets `status` flipped to `renewal_due_soon` and triggers an email via `Mailer.php` to every `super_admin` — repeating on some cadence (weekly, tightening as the date closes) rather than a single easy-to-miss notice, since the entire point is not repeating the single-point-of-failure risk the Copyright Office's own Secondary Contact requirement already flags as real. A row past `renewal_due_at` with no `last_renewed_at` update flips to `lapsed` and escalates to a more urgent notice — a lapsed registration means the platform has **no safe harbor at all**, so this is treated as a genuine incident, not routine housekeeping. `admin/adminportal.php`'s DMCA tile (already flagged as needed for `admin/dmca.php`) surfaces this status directly — a visible badge when `renewal_due_soon` or `lapsed`, not just a count of pending notices — so a super_admin sees it on every visit to the dashboard, not only in an email that might get missed.

**What this deliberately doesn't do:** it doesn't call the Copyright Office's systems, submit the renewal, or pay the $6 fee on the platform's behalf — none of that has an API this platform could integrate with, and the actual renewal is still a human going to `dmca.copyright.gov` and doing it, exactly as described in Consequences item 1. This section's whole job is making sure that human knows it's due, from inside the system they already use daily, instead of depending solely on an external portal's own reminder email arriving and being read in time.

## Consequences

**This ADR does not, by itself, secure DMCA safe harbor — two real, non-technical prerequisites sit outside what this document set can resolve, and shipping the code above without them would be a false sense of protection:**

1. **A designated DMCA agent must actually be registered with the U.S. Copyright Office's online directory** before any of this mechanism confers safe harbor protection at all — this is a legal/business action for you to take directly, not something this ADR or its code can do, but per direction the actual procedure is specific enough to record here rather than leave as "a real filing, a real fee, periodic renewal":
   - **Register the administrative account** at `dmca.copyright.gov` — the official portal. Create a Login ID/password (or use existing Login.gov credentials if prompted).
   - **Designate account contacts.** The portal requires a Primary Contact (name, phone, address, email — the representative who manages the account and handles official Copyright Office communications) and a **Secondary Contact** (a required backup, not optional — it's what actually receives the critical three-year renewal alerts if the primary contact misses one; the two contacts can share an address/phone). Verify the account via the confirmation email before proceeding.
   - **Add the service provider and pay.** From the dashboard: "Add Service Provider" → the company's legal name, corporate address, all alternate names (domains, subdomains, app names, DBAs), and the actual public contact info for the designated agent → checkout, $6 by credit card or electronic check.
   - **The $6 fee is per legal entity, not per filing.** One filing/fee covers the service provider and *all* of its alternate names (different domains, subdomains, mobile app names, DBAs) — no extra charge for those. But if the business operates through more than one distinct, separately incorporated legal entity (a subsidiary, a parent company), each one is legally its own service provider and needs its own separate $6 registration.
   - **The registration's contact info must be publicly displayed on the site** (this ADR's repeat-infringer/notice-intake pages are the natural place) — an accurate registration that the public can't actually find doesn't satisfy the statute either.
   - **Mandatory renewal every 3 years.** The registration lapses if not renewed/reconfirmed through the online system on that cycle, and letting it lapse loses safe harbor protection entirely, exposing the platform to direct and secondary copyright liability for user content from day one — this is why the Secondary Contact requirement above matters operationally, not just as a form field.

2. **The exact statutory language in the notice/counter-notice forms, and the repeat-infringer policy's public posting in Terms of Service, need real legal review before this goes live** — the perjury and jurisdiction-consent statements are legally load-bearing text that has to track the actual statutory declarations, not be drafted from product intuition or finalized unreviewed by this document set.

**Retention, resolved per direction: keep DMCA records permanently, never on `decisions/0017`'s 72-hour clock.** A `dmca_notices` row and any attached documents (`verification_documents` with `related_type = 'dmca_notice'`) are exempted from that purge cron entirely — see the corresponding carve-out now in `decisions/0017`. The reasoning is the same evidentiary-defense logic Jason gave directly: civil copyright claims carry a 3-year statute of limitations under 17 U.S.C. § 507(b) (which can run longer via the discovery rule or ongoing-damages claims), so a notice, its counter-notice, and the resolution trail have to still exist if a copyright holder or an improperly-restored/removed user later brings a legal challenge — the platform's whole defense that it acted as a compliant, neutral intermediary under safe harbor depends on those records being intact. Since there's no way to know in advance which record will end up needed for that defense, and the downside of deleting one that turns out to matter is much worse than the downside of keeping one indefinitely, the simplest correct rule is permanent retention for this record type specifically — a deliberate, narrow exception to `decisions/0017`'s general purge-everything-eventually posture, not a contradiction of it.

`admin/adminportal.php` needs a new tile for `admin/dmca.php`, distinct from the existing "Claims" tile — this is a different queue with a different access scope (super_admin only) and a different action set (execute/restore/litigation-hold, not approve/reject/escalate), and folding it into `admin/claims.php` would blur that distinction rather than clarify it. That same tile now also needs to surface §5's renewal-status badge, not just a notice count. `routes.md` needs new rows for the notice/counter-notice endpoints, the admin execution page, and (§5) `admin/dmca-settings.php`/`admin/api/dmca/agent-info.php`. `architecture.md` gets a new §29 for this mechanism, cross-referencing §9 (dispute flow, for contrast), `decisions/0012` (field-level `entity_metadata` targeting), `decisions/0017` (shared upload infrastructure, and its own daily-cron pattern reused for §5's renewal check), and `decisions/0018` (who gets notified — owner or manager).
