# ADR 0020: Tier 2 Dependency Mapping, Value/Effort Sequencing, and Sub-Phase Tagging

**Status:** Accepted (2026-09-24).

## Context

`prd.md`'s own "What's Still Missing" section already named this gap in its first draft: "Prioritization within tiers. Tier 2 alone has eleven items; nothing indicates which ship first," and "Explicit launch-vs-later line. Tier 1/2/3 tracks build sequence, not necessarily what counts as 'MVP launch.'" Per direction, laid out precisely: a flat tier with no internal order creates three real problems — unclear development sequences (Feature B needing data Feature A produces, with nothing marking that dependency), resource-allocation bottlenecks (no way to know what to staff first), and MVP confusion (teams building everything in a tier at once instead of iterating through it). The proposed fix, adopted directly: dependency mapping (sequence by real technical prerequisite), value-vs-effort analysis (rank by impact relative to cost), and milestone tagging (sub-phases like `2.1`, `2.2` to fix a firm release order) — all three used together below, not just one.

This isn't a one-off fix for Tier 2 alone. `phases.md` already states the general principle this ADR is really an instance of — "Linear Milestone Enforcement... a feature belonging to a later phase gets explicitly isolated or tagged 'preview' until every preceding phase has 100% test and migration coverage" — but that rule only ever governed the six macro-phases, never the tiers *inside* a phase's own feature roadmap. This ADR extends the same discipline one level down, and `phases.md` is updated to say so explicitly, so the next tier that grows past a handful of items doesn't quietly repeat the same gap.

**Two Tier 2 items also turned out to be stale while this pass was being done, worth fixing here rather than leaving for a separate correction:** `decisions/0015` shipped the real `/claim` page on 2026-09-23, and `decisions/0016` shipped the real `admin/claims.php` review queue the same day — both while `prd.md` still listed "Public claim entry point" and folded "review queue" into an unbuilt "admin sub-tools" bullet in Tier 2. `prd.md`'s own cross-reference rule (`routes.md` is the single source of truth for route status) already would have caught this if the two documents had been diffed against each other since; they're reconciled now.

## Decision

**Dependency mapping, reasoned from what's already established elsewhere in this document set, not assumed:**

- **`/listing/{slug}` (public business detail pages)** has no dependency on any other unbuilt Tier 2 item, and several other items depend on *it*: the claim entry point already links to it, the AI-generated per-listing articles link back to it, and Chameleon Filter routing has nothing to route traffic *toward* without it. It's `routes.md`'s only Tier 2 item marked flatly "Not built" (everything else marked "Scoped, not built" already has real underlying data) — the true root of this dependency graph.
- **Hub / Geo-Hub / Micro-Cluster landing pages** are the other root: no dependency on other unbuilt Tier 2 work, real data already exists (Micro-Cluster: 40 rows), and three other items can't function without them — the "Let's Explore" narrative entry point has nothing to narrate through, the interactive corridor map has no waypoints to plot, and Chameleon Filter's dual-behavioral content adaptation (`architecture.md` §22) has no page to adapt content *on*.
- **The Dual-Gateway landing page (`/`)** depends on nothing new — `/directory` and `/discovery` are already live routes underneath it (`routes.md`) — so it's cheap to ship once someone gets to it, but low-leverage until the pages beneath it (hub/geo-hub/cluster) exist, or splitting the gateway just routes people into placeholder content.
- **The AI-generated per-listing article pipeline explicitly depends on the editorial article template** — `prd.md`'s own line already says as much ("builds on the editorial article template above rather than replacing it") — which in turn depends on business detail pages existing for the template's `[directory_feed]` shortcode to feed from.
- **User-side safety controls (block/filter/report) are a prerequisite for profile feeds and messaging, not a follow-on to them — a real dependency this document set stated but never sequenced.** `decisions/0010` (Accepted) resolved the harassment/retaliation risk of public identity attribution specifically by giving the *targeted* user block/filter/report controls, not by hiding identity. Shipping profile feeds or cross-entity messaging — both of which increase how much of a user's activity and identity is visibly exposed to others — before that mitigation exists would put the exact risk `decisions/0010` already flagged into production unmitigated. This is the one dependency in this list that isn't about data availability, it's about a stated risk mitigation needing to exist before the feature that creates the exposure does.
- **Communities & Groups isn't just unscheduled, it's unschedulable yet** — `architecture.md` §27 and this document set's own prior rounds already flag it as needing "its own technical-design pass" before implementation, with no schema or API contract written. It's sequenced last not because it's low value, but because "sequence it" doesn't apply until that design pass produces something with dependencies to map.
- **The remaining admin sub-tools (hub/geo-hub management, cluster management, review queue beyond Claims, pending-listing review, user management)** have no hard technical dependency on other Tier 2 items — but their real urgency scales with how much of Tier 2.1–2.3 is live generating content and traffic for admins to actually need to manage. Lower sequencing priority, not lower value.

**Value-vs-effort read, folded into the same pass:** the two root items (business detail pages, hub/geo-hub/cluster pages) are simultaneously the highest-leverage (most other items depend on them) and, per `routes.md`, already have real data behind them — the best effort-to-value ratio in the tier, which is why they anchor sub-phase 2.1 rather than being sequenced by "which looks easiest" in isolation. Communities & Groups sits at the opposite end: real user value once built, but effort is currently unknowable (no design pass has scoped it), so it can't be honestly weighed against the rest yet — flagged rather than force-ranked.

### Milestone tagging — the firm sequence, `prd.md`'s Tier 2 broken into sub-phases

- **Tier 2.1 — Foundational, ship first, no dependency on other Tier 2 work:** public business detail pages (`/listing/{slug}`); Macro Hub / Geo-Hub / Micro-Cluster landing pages.
- **Tier 2.2 — Completes the core Directory/Discovery loop, depends on 2.1's pages existing to route to and adapt content on:** Dual-Gateway landing page (`/`); Chameleon Filter dual-behavioral content adaptation; top-down "Let's Explore" narrative entry point; interactive corridor map.
- **Tier 2.3 — Content pipeline, depends on 2.1's business detail pages:** editorial article template with `[directory_feed]` shortcode; AI-generated per-listing variant articles (depends on the template directly, per `prd.md`'s own stated relationship).
- **Tier 2.4 — Safety infrastructure, sequenced ahead of the social features it protects, not after:** user-side safety controls (block/filter/report), per the `decisions/0010` dependency above.
- **Tier 2.5 — Social/engagement features, depend on 2.4 being live first:** user profile feeds and connection/watch mechanics; the Centralized Communications Center.
- **Tier 2.6 — Remaining admin sub-tools, no hard dependency but lower urgency until 2.1–2.3 generate real content/traffic to administer:** hub/geo-hub management, cluster management, the rest of the review queue beyond Claims, pending-listing review, user management.
- **Tier 2.7 — Not yet schedulable, needs its own technical-design pass first, tracked here so it isn't silently dropped or silently assumed next:** Communities & Groups.

**Two items move out of Tier 2 into Tier 1, reconciling `prd.md` against real shipped work:** the public claim entry point (`decisions/0015`, real and live since 2026-09-23) and the admin Claims review queue specifically (`decisions/0016`, real and live the same day) — the latter carved out of the broader "admin sub-tools" bullet, which stays in Tier 2.6 for its still-unbuilt remainder.

## Consequences

`prd.md`'s Tier 1 section gains the two reconciled items; Tier 2 is restructured into the seven sub-phases above in place of the flat list; the "What's Still Missing" bullets on prioritization and launch-vs-later are resolved and replaced with a pointer here. `phases.md` gets a short addition noting this sub-phase-tagging approach is the standing method for sequencing any tier that grows past a handful of undifferentiated items, not a one-time Tier 2 fix.

**What this doesn't settle:** an "explicit launch-vs-later MVP line" is still a separate, real open question `prd.md` flagged and this ADR doesn't answer — sequencing *within* Tier 2 says what's built first, not which sub-phases must exist before the platform is ready to launch publicly. That's a business call (how minimal is "minimum viable" for this specific launch), not a technical-dependency one, and stays open for you to set explicitly rather than being inferred from build order here.

The sequencing above is reasoned from real, already-documented dependencies and existing-data signals — it is not a committed engineering estimate, and effort within each sub-phase (beyond the tier-level value/effort read above) still needs real scoping once someone actually picks a sub-phase up.
