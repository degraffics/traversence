8: ## Getting started
9: 
10: ### What is Traversence?
11: Traversence is a regional travel guide and local directory in one place. **Let's Explore** tells the story of a place and the journeys people take there. **Get Local** is the directory of businesses, services and places. You can use both without an account.
12: 
13: ### What's on the home page?
14: The search comes first, in two parts: **What are you looking for?** (type what you're after; × clears it) and **Let's get local** (where: tap it and the search's window opens at the place picker, to pick a town, ZIP or area and **Distance**, how far around it to look; or tap the target to use your location. It then shows the place you're searching). Searching opens its own window over the page, with its own box for your words, the place you're searching (tap it and the place and Distance open right there in the same window, in place of the results; pick a place, or tap it again or × to go back) and the results, so nothing behind it gets in the way. On a phone it fills the screen. Pick a result, or click outside the window, tap ×, or press Esc to close it. Then:
15: - **Four ways in**, one for each main section: **Let's Explore** (places, trails, history), **Get Local** (businesses and services), **Connect** (groups) and the **Marketplace**.
16: - **Community Groups** on Connect. Next to the groups already running are a few a place usually needs but doesn't have yet (your town's neighbors, the outdoors, local history, families, volunteers, business owners), each with **Start this group**.
17: - **Events** near you, from the places' own calendars. Until there are enough, a few cards marked **Sample** show how events will look. Buying tickets is coming.
18: - **What's new**, newest first, with five tabs: **Events**, **Deals**, **Gallery** (public photo albums), **Journey** (journeys and guides) and **Business** (businesses run by their owners). Tap a tab to see only that kind, and again to see everything. Deals from businesses are coming.
19: - **Have a GO Code?**: type the code from a sign or flyer to open its page.
20: 
21: Nothing on the home page is paid placement: it's newest first.

23: ### How do I contact Traversence?
24: Email **support@traversence.com**. It's at the foot of every page, and in the emails we send you (or just reply to one of them: replies come to us too). Ask anything: your account, a listing, a GO Code order, or your privacy and data.
25: 
26: ### Where did the big logo and icons at the top go?
27: The top bar starts collapsed, so the page gets the whole screen: no bar, just the compass in the top-left corner and the menu (☰) hanging from the top-right corner, with search between them on pages that have it (on the home page the search sits in the page itself, under the title). Signed in, the slim account bar sits above it all (see "How do I know which account I'm acting as?"). On a phone the two corners do different jobs. The **compass** is *where you can go*, the same on every page: Let's Explore, Get Local, Connect, Marketplace, the Trip planner, your Dashboard, and the Help Center (its big logo takes you home); not signed in, **Create account** and **Sign in** are at its top. The other corner (☰) is *what you can do here*: this part of the site's tools (tap a heading like Things to do to fold it open or shut), with **Dashboard** first so you can always get back. On pages with no tools of their own, like the FAQ, it isn't there. In your account, the layout switch (your full profile, or just your Pulse) sits in that corner too. Around the site the brown bar stays; in your account the page runs to the top with just those two corners. Notifications are in your Pulse (the bell there); an orange dot on the menu means something is waiting. To bring back the full logo or the row of icons, tap the small tab next to the compass or next to the menu. We remember your choice on this device.

29: ### Do I need an account?
30: No, not to look around. You need one to save favorites, write journeys, comment, like, report something, or make suggestions under your name.
31: 
32: ### How old do I have to be?
33: Traversence accounts are for adults: you must be 18 or older. You'll confirm it when you sign up. If your account was made before we asked, or you confirmed an earlier age, we'll ask once, the first time you comment or become a contributor.

35: ### How do I choose where I'm looking?
36: Open the place picker at the top of the page (on the home page, **Change** under the search). You can:
37: - open the **Trip Planner** to plan a trip: where, when, how long and what you're after;
38: - pick a **region**;
39: - type a **destination**, or a **state, city or ZIP** anywhere in the U.S.;
40: - use **Auto-detect** to start near you;
41: - tap **Search everywhere** to have no place at all. In search, the **×** beside the place clears it the same way.
42: 
43: In **Destination** you can type a town, a town area, a local name, or a landmark like **Grand Canyon**, **Lyman Lake** or a museum. Towns come first. You can add the state after a comma ("St. Johns, AZ"); the list keeps up while you type it. Choosing a landmark takes you to the town area around it.
44: 
45: Signed in, you can make a place your default (**My default**), so you start there next time: whenever you're signed in and haven't chosen a place yet this visit, every page starts from it (shown as "your default"). A region you arrive through, or choosing everywhere, still wins for that visit.
46: 
47: ### Does Auto-detect share where I am?
48: No. Auto-detect only picks a starting place for you on this visit. Your location is never shown to anyone. When the place it finds (or a town or ZIP you set) is in one of our town areas, search treats it as that town in its area, just as if you'd chosen it: what we list there comes first, and you never see "We have no listings in <your town> yet" for a town where we do.

50: ## Exploring places
51: 
52: ### Why does Let's Explore ask before showing my default place?
53: Your default place (the ZIP saved on your account) never picks the page by itself. Let's Explore offers it first: **Explore** the area it's in, **Choose another place**, or stay where you are. A place you choose yourself, or with Auto-detect, is followed straight away.
54: 
55: ### How do I see more of a list?
56: Lists show 10 at a time; a list of cards side by side rounds that up to whole rows (four across shows 12), so a page never ends on a half row. **Show more** under a list adds the next ones; the bar under it has the page numbers, which ones you're seeing ("11–20 of 64"), and how many a page (10, 25, 50 or 100), which this browser remembers for that list. A list of 10 or fewer has neither. This works the same in the directory, Let's Explore, the Marketplace and Find groups.
57: 
58: ### What's on the North America page?
59: The big picture: the story of the continent and its regions, plus the newest journeys and guides from everywhere. From there you can browse by **Regions**, **Experiences**, **Parks & Landmarks** or **Map**, or open the **Trip Planner**. **Map** shows what's around the place you've set, everything at first, then narrowed to what you'd do (Hike, Walk, Drive, Exhibits, On the water) or eat and stay; beside ours, the museums, galleries, gardens, parks, zoos, theatres and attractions OpenStreetMap knows there show as lighter pins (credited to OpenStreetMap), so the places people go are always on it; outside our areas it opens around you (or asks where first). **Parks & Landmarks** follows you outside our areas too: the landmarks near the place you've set, and the national parks nearest first, each with how far. It's where Let's Explore starts when you haven't chosen a place.
60: 
61: ### How do I change the place I'm exploring?
62: Tap the pencil next to **Explore: <place>** at the top of Let's Explore or of any place page, and choose another place. On a place page, you go straight to the new place's page.
63: 
64: ### Whose photos are at the top of a place?
65: Photos from journeys people have shared about that place, or about places inside it. A region shows photos from all its towns. Share a journey with photos and yours can appear there. Until there are any, a labelled stock photo may show instead (see [Why do some photos say Stock photo?](/faq.php#why-do-some-photos-say-stock-photo)).

67: ### What are Experiences?
68: Things to do that are worth the trip: paddling a lake at sunrise, a rodeo weekend, a night under dark skies. You take part, it belongs to the place, and someone local can make it happen. Find them in **Let's Explore → Experiences**, in season first. Each one shows when to go, how long it takes, how hard it is, who can take you, and journeys from people who did it. An old link to traversence.com/experiences/ opens that list too.
69: 
70: ### How do I post an experience?
71: Sign in and open **Dashboard → Your experiences**. Say what you do, where, what it's like and when to go, and add the local businesses that make it happen. You need to be 18 or older. Our editors review your first experiences before they show. If they don't approve one, you'll see their note and can edit it and send it again. Once two of yours have been approved, a well-described experience from you can go up at once.
72: 
73: ### Where do the other experiences come from?
74: Some are posted by members. Some we suggest from what we already know: outdoor places and their activities, local guides, outfitters and museums, and journeys people shared. Our staff add others. Each one gets a credibility score from who posted it and how complete it is. The most credible go up at once and our editors check them afterwards. The rest wait for an editor.
75: 
76: ### What's the difference between Experiences and National Parks?
77: National Parks are places. Experiences are what you do, in a park or anywhere else.

79: ### What's on a place's own page?
80: Each place has its own guide: a **Regional guide** for a region, an **Area guide**, a **Community guide** for a group of towns, a **City guide** for a town and a **Neighborhood guide** for a local name. They all follow the same order: essentials, things to do, getting there, where to eat and stay, events, itineraries, what living there is like, and its local businesses. On a wide screen, at a glance, the places one step down (its towns, say) and landmarks & heritage run in a column beside the guide. A community of just one town (Farmington) opens that town's guide. A part with nothing in it yet is left out. In Let's Explore, **This place** in the ☰ menu opens it.
81: 
82: ### How are places organized?
83: From large to small: North America, then regions, areas, communities (groups of towns), towns, and neighborhoods (the local names people use). Each has its own guide with its story, its journeys, and what's there.
84: 
85: ### What do Visiting and Live here do on a guide?
86: They show the same guide in two orders. **Visiting** starts with the essentials and things to do, then what the place is like, getting there and where to eat. **Live here** starts with the essentials (who to call, road and weather alerts), local businesses and support. Your choice is remembered; coming from Get Local starts you on Live here.
87: 
88: ### Where do a guide's weather and road conditions come from?
89: Weather and alerts come from the National Weather Service. Tap the weather by the guide's name to open the forecast (it starts folded): the next 24 hours as temperature, rain or wind, and the next seven days. It stays on the page; the weather service's own forecast is a link inside it. Arizona road work, lane closures, detours, incidents and alerts come from AZ 511 (its work zone feed uses the national WZDx format). Our server asks for the place's weather, not yours: your location is never sent. Weather updates every half hour, roads every ten minutes. Check AZ 511 before you drive.
90: 
91: ### What is "Walking in the footsteps" in a guide?
92: The place's story, told where you can stand today: the peoples and families, trades, building materials, water and hard times that made it, each tied to a street, a building, a river crossing or a view you can visit. It's written from our heritage catalog: facts drawn from historic records (the Library of Congress's newspapers, photographs and maps, linked, never copied), the place's public records and our research, and only those backed by a historic record or two independent sources (several pages of one newspaper count as one). Archaeological, sacred and burial places follow the law of the place, at three levels. A place its managers invite visitors to (a state park's ruins tour, a museum, a town's public cemetery) is told like any other, with how to get there. One that public sources name but that isn't open to visitors has its story told, never where it is or how to reach it, with a reminder that it may be visited only with the authorization of the nation or the land's managers. One no public source names isn't named at all; a guide may say only that the area holds places sacred to a nation and that they need its permission. On a tribal nation's land nothing is told until the nation says. Where a record's source asks to be credited (the Library of Congress), it's named under the chapter, and a fact we may only use to confirm something, never to tell it, isn't shown. Every guide shows when it was first published and when it was last checked. We check each guide every month, and sooner when something changes: a place it names closes, someone suggests a change, new historic records turn up, the season turns, or many more people than usual start reading it.
93: 
94: ### Where do the events under "Coming up" come from?
95: From each event's own announcement. We read the town's or county's own events and calendar pages first and, for what they don't list, look on the web, keeping only events with a date ahead and a page that announces them. Each shows with its dates, time, where it is (linked when it's a place listed with us), its price when the page gives one, and a link to that page: check it before you go, since plans change. An event shows in the guide's Events section (a community's guide includes its towns') and on its venue's page under **Coming up here**, until the day after it ends. A ceremony or feast at a sacred place is listed only when the nation or parish announces it as open to the public, never with directions, and anything whose name may mark a protected place is read by a person first. We're switching this on place by place, so some guides don't show events yet. Something wrong or missing? Tap **Suggest a change** on the guide.
96: 
97: ### What does a highlighted name in a guide do?
98: Point at it (tap it on a phone) and a small card shows what it is, where it is, its phone number if it has one, and **See more**, which opens its page. Every business, landmark and outdoor place a guide names is highlighted this way, and so are the names in journeys, place stories, experiences and how-tos, so you can go deeper without losing your place. You can also search for any of them by name: a landmark, park or museum a guide names is in search as soon as the guide has it.
99: 
100: ### Why are names in my journey highlighted?
101: When you publish a journey (or an experience), we look for the places and businesses it names that are on Traversence, link them, and your piece shows under **Featured in** on their pages. If you've turned on AI Assist & Learning and have a paid plan (your own, or one on a business you own), Claude reads your words to find the names; if not, we only match names from our own directory, and nothing you wrote leaves Traversence. Right after you publish, and any time on your journey's page or its editor, you'll see what it's tagged with: tap × on any that aren't right and it comes off at once. Only you see that list. A name we don't have yet isn't added on its own: a person checks it first.
102: 
103: ### What is "Featured in" on a listing or a landmark?
104: The published guides, journeys, place stories, experiences and how-tos that name it, so you can read what's said about it there. Group posts never show here: they're for the group's members. Nobody pays to be named.
105: 
106: ### What do I get when I follow a business or a place?
107: Besides its own posts: when a guide, journey or story names a business you follow, or a place you follow gets a new guide or story, it shows in your feeds and your Pulse. A member's journey that names a business reaches that business's followers only if they also follow the person who wrote it or the place it's about.
108: 
109: ### Who hears about it when I @mention someone?
110: The person you mention, once, in their Pulse, if they're in the group you posted in (so they can read it). Nobody else is told, and your post doesn't show anywhere outside the group.
111: 
112: ### What's in a guide's Essentials?
113: Weather alerts and road conditions, who to call, and the nearest places for what you might need, three of each, nearest first. Everyone sees emergency and urgent care, pharmacies, police, fire and ambulance, fuel and charging, groceries, banks and ATMs, and visitor information and libraries. **Visiting** adds car trouble, getting around, pet emergencies, and laundry, mail and shipping. **Live here** adds the town hall and government offices, utilities and trash, schools and childcare, doctors, dentists and counseling, home repairs, and community help. **See more** opens the full list in Get Local. For an emergency, call 911.
114: 
115: ### How do you choose the businesses a guide shows?
116: By what the section is about, how close they are, the kind of place (a quick bite or a sit-down meal), the season (winter leads with snow sports, summer with the lakes) and the weather right now: when rain, a storm warning, a heat wave, a deep cold or a strong wind keeps people in, museums, galleries and other indoor places come first, with a **Right now** note saying why; on a fair day the outdoors leads. Places our writers name come first. A paid plan never moves a business up. We only show public listings that are open, one per business: a business marked closed drops out of every guide.
117: 
118: ### Where do I find a place's guide?
119: On the place's own page (open the place from search or Regions), and in **Let's Explore → Stories & guides**, which lists the guides for the place you're searching and the towns in it. A place without one says so; guides are added as they're written and checked.

121: ### Who writes the guides?
122: Traversence compiles them. Our crawler first gathers what's public about the place, free: its Wikipedia article (read for facts, never copied), its facts on Wikidata, its town or county's own website, the places near it on the National Register of Historic Places, the landmarks and outdoor places we hold nearby, and the Library of Congress's records of it, including pages of historic newspapers (Chronicling America) that name it. A new guide waits up to two days for these, so its history is drawn from them first. Claude, the AI we use, then researches the place in depth, told what those records already hold so it searches only for what they don't (its peoples and founding, the families and events that shaped it, its faith, food and crafts, the festivals of its year and what it's like to be there) and keeps that research with the place. It writes the guide from, first, what people on the ground know (our team's notes from being there, and the journeys and experiences people have published about the place, a member's only if they've turned on AI & Learning), then that history, then what visitors search for there (counts only, never who). It searches the web only for what the crawler didn't find, and names only places it can back up. What the crawler gathers is kept with the place, so it's never fetched twice, and its facts (when the town was founded, its population) show under At a glance. Each guide gets a confidence score, from how sure Claude is, what it was written from, its sources and how many of its names we have. When the score is high enough, the guide goes live by itself, marked "Compiled by Traversence from public sources and local knowledge" with the date, and our team corrects or takes it down whenever something's off. Every month each live guide is checked again, the ones people have been reading first: whether what the crawler knows about the place changed, whether a date in it has passed, whether a place it names has closed, and whether its research is a year old. When something has, the parts Claude wrote are written again and scored the same way; anything our team wrote is flagged for them instead. If all that's wrong is a date that has passed, a business that has closed or a link that no longer works, just those sentences are corrected (by the least costly AI, checked before it's kept) rather than rewriting the guide. See something wrong? Tap **Suggest a change** on the guide.
123: 
124: ### What are suggested journeys on a guide?
125: Routes through the guide's own places, under **Suggested journeys**, labelled "Suggested by Traversence". The guide decides what kinds there are: whatever runs most strongly through it (the outdoors, history and heritage, food, the arts or shopping) becomes a journey, and a guide with enough of everything also gets "A day in" the place. Each stop is one the guide recommends, in an order that makes sense to travel, and timed: breakfast in the morning, lunch at midday, a museum while it's open, a trail or viewpoint in daylight. Between stops you see how long it takes to drive or walk and how far; "about" means it's our estimate. Tap **See the stops** for the list, the arrow beside each for directions, or the route icon to send the whole journey to Google Maps or Apple Maps (see below). They're timed for a Saturday, so check hours on other days. They're built by rules from what we know, with no AI, and rebuilt every week or when the guide changes, so a place that closes drops out. A business is in a journey because it fits the route, never because it pays. Tap **Make it my trip** to copy it into a trip of your own and change it (see "How do I save places to a trip?").

127: ### How do I save places to a trip?
128: On a place, a business or an outdoor place, tap ⋯ then **Add to a trip**, and pick one of your trips or **New trip** (named for the place, like "Trip to Show Low"). On **Get Local** it's in each card's ⋯. On a ready-made journey, **Make it my trip** copies its stops into a trip of your own. It says "Added to Trip to Show Low" with **Open it** and **Undo**. Your trips are under **Your trips** in your account's menu (and beside the Trip Planner on Let's Explore): take a stop out (with Undo), rename a trip, send it to Google Maps or Apple Maps, or delete it (it's kept 30 days, so you can put it back). Each trip has its own page (see "What's on a trip's page?"); sharing a trip and publishing it as a guide come after.
129: 
130: ### What's on a trip's page?
131: Open a trip from **Your trips** (or **Open it** after adding a place). Pick the day it **starts**, how many **days**, and when **each day starts**. Stops are listed by day, in order: drag one to move it (or use ↑ ↓, and the day beside it, on a phone), and choose how long you'll **stay** at each. Every stop gets a time, worked out from the drive before it; with a date, each is checked against its hours that day ("Closed when you'd arrive: Opens 11 AM", with **Move to day 2**), and parks and outdoor places against daylight. Times are in the time at the trip's first stop, always labelled (MST, PDT…); a stop in another time zone shows its own. The map numbers the stops, one colour a day, and draws the chosen day's route in lime; tap **Day 1**, **Day 2**… over the map, or one of a day's stops, to see another. The miles and times between stops are straight-line estimates, so they say "about"; driving routes on our own map are coming. **Send to Maps** sends the whole trip to Google Maps or Apple Maps for turn-by-turn directions, and **Print** prints the list.
132: 
133: ### Can I start a trip from the Trip Planner?
134: Yes. On Let's Explore's **Trip Planner**, pick where, when and how long, then **Save as a trip**: it becomes a trip with those days, and its page offers the ready-made journeys for that place as a start (**Use these stops**). Searching for ideas works as before, and **Add to a trip** is on a place's **Peek** in the results and on the map's card.
135: 
136: ### Who can see my trips?
137: Only you, unless you share it. A trip is private: nobody sees who added a place to a trip. On a trip's page, **Share** makes a link: anyone with it can view the trip (its days, times and map), not change it, and it shows nothing about you. **Turn off** stops the link at once; sharing again makes a new link. Stops that can't be shown publicly (a place whose location is kept private, one marked closed) are left out of a shared trip. You can also publish a trip as a Personal Guide (see below), only when you choose. Deleting your account deletes your trips.
138: 
139: ### What's a Personal Guide?
140: A trip you publish for everyone: on a trip's page, **Publish as a guide**, give it a title and a few sentences about it, and **Publish**. It's credited to you, like a journey, and it changes as your trip does. You need to be a contributor first (**Your journeys → Become a contributor**: 18 or older, with a public profile). Your words get the same automated check as a journey's: if something is held, it says why and you can change it. Readers can like it, comment and report it, and our team can hide it, telling you why. **Withdraw** takes it down; **Publish again** puts it back. Places whose location is kept private never show in it. If you delete your account, your guides are taken down and their words removed.
141: 
142: ### Can a business see who added it to a trip?
143: No. A business sees only how many times it was added to a trip ("Added to trips" in its insights, with its calls and directions), never who. A place, a landmark or an outdoor place shows it on its page, beside its views ("Added to 12 trips this month"), never who. Nobody on our team is counted.
144: 
145: ### Why can't I add some places to a trip?
146: **Add to a trip** is greyed, with the reason, when a place can't go in one: a place whose location is kept private (a shelter: call them instead), a place we tell about but don't put on the map, a business closed for good, or one with only a mailbox address. A stop that becomes one of these later stays in your trip, greyed with the reason, and is left out when you send the trip to Maps.
147: 
148: ### Can I make a trip without an account?
149: Yes: as a guest, one trip is kept on this device (in this browser only). Make an account to keep it and see it anywhere: when you sign in, the trip on this device moves into your account. Private browsing may not keep it.
150: 
151: ### How do I send a whole journey to Google Maps or Apple Maps?
152: Tap the route icon on a suggested journey, or **Send to Maps** under "Make a day of it" on a place's page. Pick **Google Maps** or **Apple Maps** and where to start: **My location** or the first stop. Your choices are kept on this device for next time. Every stop goes, in order. One link holds ten points, so a longer route is split into legs ("Leg 1 of 3"): open them in order, each starting where the last one ended. Nothing is left off. Several stops in Apple Maps need iOS 18.4 or macOS 15.4 or later. It's a plain link: we send nothing to Google or Apple, and it costs nothing. A trip you've saved goes the same way, from **Your trips**.
153: 
154: ### Why do more names in a guide link now, even in places you don't cover?
155: When a guide names a beach, park, museum or historic place we don't have yet, we look for it in Wikidata, the free public database behind Wikipedia, near the guide's place. When it's there, it gets its own page with us (what it is, its story from Wikipedia, a free photo when we find one, and the guides and journeys that name it), and the name in the guide links to it. Journeys can stop there too. We also look on our own: around each of our pilot towns (once a year), across each pilot state bit by bit (each month), and for any name people search for that we don't have yet ("Four Corners Monument", "Laguna Art Museum"), anywhere in the US. Museums, monuments, historic sites, harbors and parks with a Wikipedia article or on the National Register of Historic Places get a page with us and can be found by name, usually within minutes of the first search. Where a place is (its town and state) comes from the same source. So does what it is: each one is filed under one of our categories (Museums, Parks, Churches…) by what its source says it is, never guessed from its name, so tapping its kind shows more like it and a search like "museums near me" finds it. Until a category fits, it shows the source's own word for it ("Historic district"). Only the words of a search are looked up, never who searched, and never a search with a number or an email address in it. Businesses aren't added this way: they go through the same checks as every listing. A place that may be sacred, a burial site or otherwise protected is never named or located.
156: 
157: ### Can I get directions to a place a guide names?
158: Yes. Tap or hover a highlighted name: its card shows what it is, a map pin to see it on our map, and an arrow for directions in your own maps app (Google Maps). **See more** opens its page.
159: 
160: ### Where do the travel times come from?
161: From OpenStreetMap's roads, through a free routing service (openrouteservice), asked once for the places in a town and then kept with us, so the next journey, trip or card reads our own record instead of asking again. They're renewed only when a place moves, someone tells us a route is wrong, or the time is two years old. When we don't have one yet, we estimate it from the straight-line distance and say "about".
162: 
163: ### Is everything named in a guide real?
164: Every business, place, landmark and road a guide names that we have links to its page with us. When a guide names something we don't have yet, it shows as plain text while we look for it: first on the map (OpenStreetMap) and its own website, then by one web search for its own website, and only if that finds nothing, by Claude on official sources. What we find gets a confidence score: when it's high and the place has an address, it joins the listings our crawler finds and goes through the same checks (a real place, a way to reach it, a category that fits, and not listed already). Once it passes, it's published and the name links, usually within the quarter hour; anything the checks can't settle, our team looks at. A shelter or safe house is never looked up outside: a person links it. A place that shares its phone or website with a listing under another name (a county office's line) is always checked by a person. A guide never names a place that may be sacred, burial or protected, or a business that's closed: it waits as a draft until those are taken out.

Each Start in the run log has **Download report** (a text file) and **Open** (the same report as a page you can read, save or share, on a phone too). A workflow held only by its own gate, such as W14 waiting for a hub's licence terms to be read, shows amber (it waits) and doesn't count against launch. Before it, **System Configuration → Pre-flight** tests every service, setting, workflow and task; **Run all** runs on the server, so it carries on if you leave the page, and the page shows how far it has got when you come back (a run the server cut off waits for you: the page asks whether to continue it from where it stopped or cancel it, and Pulse has both as buttons). Every run, finished or not, is one line in the run log with its status, its start and end, who started it, and its report as far as it got.
