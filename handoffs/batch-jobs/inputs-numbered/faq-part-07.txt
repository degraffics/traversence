1729: ### How does auto-renew work?
1730: Two weeks before your domain runs out, we charge the card you chose for it and renew it for a year. If the card is declined, we try again 3 and 7 days later and email you each time; replacing the card tries again at once. You can turn auto-renew off any time: the domain stays yours until its date, and you can **Renew now** whenever you like.

1733: Wherever you like: **forward** it to another web address, set its own **DNS records** here (an A record for a website, MX for email, TXT to prove it's yours), or put in **nameservers** from another DNS host (Cloudflare, your web host). Using one of your domains for a Traversence storefront is coming with the Storefront app.

1736: Yes. Open the domain and choose **Transfer away**: we unlock it and show its transfer code once (it isn't kept here), and auto-renew stops. Give the code to the new registrar; the domain shows as **Moved away** once it has left.

1739: Yes: in **Your domains**, choose **Add a domain you own**. It's listed for pointing only, since its records stay where it's registered (GoDaddy, Namecheap and so on).

Your card provider, QuickBooks Payments, holds them; we keep only a label like "Visa ··4242" and when it expires. One card is your default, offered first at checkout. Your plan and your domains' renewals each pay from a card in the wallet. Test accounts (and our team while testing) have a separate **test wallet**: its cards are saved in QuickBooks' sandbox and only ever charged there, and no real card can be used while testing, so a test never moves real money.

It unlocks by itself once nothing renews on it (the plan ends or is set to stop, auto-renew is off, or the domain moves away). We email you 30 days before a locked card expires.

1752: ### How do I find a group?
1753: On **Connect → Groups** (https://traversence.com/community/), search by a group's name, topic or place, or choose a topic from **All topics**. **Most active** puts first the groups more people joined in the last 7 days, then the biggest; **Most members** lists the biggest first, and with a place, **Nearest first** lists groups there and nearby first. A card's **+12 this week** is how many joined in the last 7 days: a count only, never who. **Join group** (or **Ask to join**) opens the group's page at **What you're agreeing to**, its charter, because joining is agreeing to it. **Start a group** is at the top right.
1754: 
1755: ### How do I start a group?
1756: Open **Community** and fill in **Start a group**: a name, what it's about, its kind (interest, local or support), and its charter: the purpose (required), who it's for, house rules, and what happens if you step down. You need a confirmed email to start one. One person can run up to 5 groups. If you run a business on Traversence, you can start its group instead (see Can a business run a group?).
1757: 
1758: ### How do I stop posts from a group reaching my Pulse?
1759: Open a group post message and tap **Mute its posts**. You can also use the shield on a group post's row in your Pulse: **Mute the group**. Mentions of you still come through. Posts from a group gather into one unread message per group, so a busy group doesn't fill your Pulse.
1760: 
1761: ### Who sees join requests and reports in my group?
1762: Its owner and moderators, or the business's team for a business's own group. Join requests and reported posts waiting in groups you moderate show in your own Pulse under **Needs you**, named by the group, and **Groups ▾** shows one group at a time. Each arrives as its own message with **Accept** / **Decline** or **Keep** / **Remove** inside it.
1763: 
1764: ### What's a group's charter?
1765: The rules the group runs by, written by whoever starts it, and what you agree to when you join. Every group's page shows **What you're agreeing to**: its purpose, house rules, who sees that you're in it, what happens if the owner leaves, and how joining works, with **Read the whole charter** for everything else and every past version. Each charter also carries Traversence's ground rules, which no group can remove: our Terms, no harassment, no one's private information, no sacred or restricted sites, and selling only through Traversence. Groups started before charters show the ground rules until their owner writes one.
1766: 
1767: ### What's on a group's page?
1768: At the top, the group's name with **Join** (or **Ask to join**) on the right; a local group shows photos people have shared of its place. Under it: what kind of group it is and its topic (tap either to find more like it), what it's about, **Share** and a **QR code** for the group, and its numbers at a glance: members, posts this week, how joining works, and since when. Then the charter (**What you're agreeing to**), and, for members, the posts and the member list. A group's own cover photos are coming.

1770: ### What happens when a group's charter changes?
1771: Members are told once, in their Community feed and on the group's page, with what changed. You don't have to agree again and nobody is removed by a change; if you don't like it, you can leave. Most changes take effect at once. Two give 14 days' notice first: what happens if the owner leaves, and opening the member list to all members (you can hide your own name meanwhile). Making the member list more private happens at once.
1772: 
1773: ### Who can see that I'm in a group?
1774: It depends on the group's charter, which you see before you join. Usually members see each other's names; in some groups, such as support groups, only the moderators do. Nobody in a group ever sees your email or where you live. When a group is about to open its member list, you can hide your name from it; the moderators still see it, to run the group.
1775: 
1776: ### Can a business run a group?
1777: Yes, one group per business, run by its team. Its Owner or a Manager starts it from **Start a group**, choosing the business under **Start it as**. The group shows **Run by** and the business's name. The Owner and Managers run it (only the Owner can close it), Creators and Support moderate it, and Sales answers as the business's rep. A business's places and topics, such as Support or How-tos, will be chapters of its one group (coming). The business contacts you outside the group only if you ask it to or Link with it.
1778: 
1779: ### Why does a post say "Sales, Pine Cafe"?
1780: In a business's group, its team can post as the business (the Owner, Managers and Creators), or as its sales rep, shown as their name with "Sales" and the business's name, so you always know who's speaking for the business.
1781: 
1782: ### How do reports work in a group?
1783: Tap **Report** on a post and choose why. Reports on a group's posts go to its own moderators (or only its owner, if the charter says so), never to Traversence, and nobody learns who reported. A post is hidden while it's looked at once enough members have reported it (the charter sets how many, counting only different members who've been in the group more than a week), or at once if it shares someone's private information or a sacred or restricted site. Its author still sees it, marked. The moderators keep it, remove it, or remove it and its author. Two kinds of report come to Traversence's Safety team instead, where our Terms apply: **Breaks the law or puts someone in danger**, and a report on a whole group.
1784: 
1785: ### Can a group ask Traversence for help?
1786: Yes. A group's owner and moderators can use **Ask Traversence for help** on the group's page for what the group can't settle, such as someone harassing people across groups or a moderator's account taken over. It reaches our support team as a case, and we reply by email. If reports in a group wait 14 days with nobody deciding, we're asked to help on our own.
1787: 
1788: ### What notices will I get about groups, and can I turn them off?
1789: Each one goes to your Pulse once, when it's about you or something you follow: your request to join a group was answered (with the group's welcome message), the charter of a group you're in changed, a group's moderators removed your post, a new group started in a place you follow, or one of your connections invited you to a group. If you run a group or lead a chapter, you also hear about its chapters; if you run a business, you hear when a group lists it among the places it's about. Switch any of them off in **Dashboard → App Settings → Communications**. There you also choose, for each one, whether it also comes by email, and whether you'd like one weekly email of them.

1791: ### How do I stop emails from Traversence?
1792: Every email we send for a group, a business or your notices has **Stop these** at the foot: one tap stops that kind (it still reaches your Pulse; a business's email stops from that business). You can also choose kind by kind in **Dashboard → App Settings → Communications**. Your mail app's own Unsubscribe button does the same. Emails about your account itself, such as sign-in codes and receipts for what you buy from us, can't be switched off. We never send to an address that bounced or marked us as spam.

1794: ### What happens to a group if its owner leaves?
1795: Whatever its charter says: the group ends (the usual choice, with no elections), a member the owner named is offered it, the longest-serving moderator is offered it, or the moderators choose one of themselves. The steps that carry this out, and closing groups that have gone quiet for 30 days, are coming.

1797: ### What are a group's tags?
1798: Up to 5 words that say what the group is about, under its topic: baking, cast iron, trail running. The owner and moderators set them, and existing tags are suggested as you type so spellings stay together. Tap one to find more groups like it; the Community page shows the popular ones for each topic. Tags help people find a group; they never rank it higher, and they're never taken from posts. A tag that might name a sacred or protected site waits for our team before it shows.

1800: ### What does "Places we're about" mean?
1801: A group can name up to 10 places it goes to or talks about: towns, businesses, outdoor places and public landmarks, each with a short note ("planned for June"). They show on the group's page, and each place's page shows the group (under **Featured in**, or **Talked about by** on a town's page) with its name and member count, nothing more. Only the group's own choice puts it there; its posts are never read for it. If you run a business a group names, you're told once, and you can take your business off its list from the group's page.

1803: ### What's a chapter?
1804: A branch of a larger group, for a place or a topic: *World Travelers* can have *World Travelers: White Mountains*, or *Cast Iron Cooks: Beginners*. A chapter has its own members, posts, moderators and lead, starts with the whole group's charter and tags, and shows on its place's page with "part of" its group. The group's owner opens chapters, or approves a member's request to start one; the person who leads it needs Verification. The group's charter says how chapters are joined: joining a chapter joins the whole group too, each chapter is joined on its own, or members of the whole group only. The group's owner and moderators can post to all its chapters at once and help moderate them. An existing local group can become a chapter when both owners agree, and a chapter can leave to stand on its own after 14 days' notice to its members. Closing quiet chapters and bringing them back are coming.

1806: ### How do I invite people to a group?
1807: On the group's page, under **Share & invite**: tick connections from your Address Book and send. Each person gets one invitation from a group (again after 30 days if they don't answer), can say no thanks, and can switch group invitations off in App Settings. Someone who blocked you never gets one. The group's owner and moderators can also make **invite codes**: a link for a meeting, a class or a list, with an end date, that can be switched off at any time. Share and the QR code are under the group's name for anyone. Inviting a business's customers by email, flyers and badges for your own site are coming.

1810: Only if the group's charter says so. In a group that approves its members, the charter decides whether an invitation lets you straight in (from the owner and moderators, or from any member) and whether invite codes can. Otherwise your request goes to the moderators marked with who invited you. You always see the charter first and agree to it yourself. Some groups ask a few questions when you ask to join; only the moderators see your answers.
1811: 
1812: ### Can anyone read a group's posts?
1813: Only members, except for one: the group's owner can pin a **welcome** post that anyone can read, members or not, so visitors can see what the group does. The owner can also write a welcome message each new member gets when they join.

Groups come nearest first: those in that town or area, then nearby ones, then groups that talk about places nearby, and, if you picked a topic or typed a word, groups anywhere that match, marked "Anywhere". Groups with more active members come first within each; no one can pay to be shown higher.

2015: ### Does Traversence have a password vault?
2016: Our own team has one now: the logins for the outside services we use are kept in a vault that only its owner's browser can open. Each password is sealed (encrypted) in the browser before it's saved, so what we store can't be read by anyone at Traversence, or by anyone who got into our server. Setting it up takes five short steps: the device's passkey (fingerprint, face or PIN); a vault passphrase, which isn't a password (it never leaves the browser, so it has its own rules: four or more words, or 20 characters, hard to guess, and used nowhere else; the page can suggest one); the recovery words; and two recommended extra checks, an authenticator app (whose code is then needed to add a device or use the recovery words) and a spare passkey or security key. Opening a password takes the passkey and the passphrase; each password opens only for its exact sign-in page; and every password opened is emailed to its owner with a link to lock the vault. If you lose your devices or passphrase, 24 recovery words you saved at the start (printed or downloaded, never emailed) get you back in, once: using them makes new ones. You type three of them back from your saved copy before they're kept, so a mistyped kit is caught at the start. We keep nothing that could open a vault, so lost words with no other device means the vault can't be opened, by anyone. A vault for members and businesses is coming, only if you choose it and agree to its terms, alongside 1Password through us or the password manager you already use. Our team also has a browser extension for Chrome and Edge: connected from the vault for up to 12 hours (the key stays in the browser's memory, gone when the browser closes or the vault is locked), it lists the saved logins under a site's sign-in box only on that exact site, so a look-alike site gets nothing, fills the one picked, and after a sign-in offers to save it or update the saved password. A public version for members and businesses comes with their vault. The vault page's bar across the top shows how long it stays open, with Close and Lock at its end: after 5 minutes unused it asks if you're still there and closes 2 minutes later, like a bank's sign-in. Reloading the page keeps it open (in that tab only, for that time); leaving by a link asks first, since leaving closes it, and closing the tab ends it. A connected extension shows how long it has left. The extension keeps its connection for 12 hours or until the browser closes, in every tab; every 30 minutes it checks with the vault and lets go at once if the vault was locked or it was disconnected. When it isn't connected, its Connect button opens the Vault page, and unlocking brings you back to the tab you were on, connected. On a sign-in that asks for the email first, Fill puts in the email and then the password when the next screen asks for it. In a new-password box it offers a strong password (within the length the page states), and after a password change on a page with no user name it asks which saved login the new password is for. The vault opens three ways: with the passphrase, with the authenticator app's code, or with a code emailed to the email on the account; the second and third are then finished by the device's own PIN, fingerprint or face (or a phone or security key), whose secret never leaves the device, so a code alone opens nothing. PIN unlock turns on for a computer the first time the vault is opened there with the passphrase, and can be turned off under Devices. Its owner chooses what the vault emails and how often (its Emails button): passwords opened, logins the extension saved, the extension connected, and the vault unlocked after a lock can each be emailed each time, the first time each day, in an hourly or daily summary, or not at all (always kept in the vault's own record). Each email, summaries included, says who did each thing: the person and the staff login, or the extension and who connected it. What would show someone else in the vault (devices, recovery words, the authenticator app, a lock, many deletes, and changes to these settings) is always emailed at once. In the list, each login has **Copy password** beside it; the rest (**Show the password for 10 seconds**, **Copy the user name**, **Change it** and **Delete it**) is under its **⋯** (2026-11-20). The extension downloads from the vault page, with its install steps for Chrome and Edge.
