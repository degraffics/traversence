389: - **A trip is what the system couldn't fix itself.** When something fails (a server error, a failed run, a job that stopped, a worker gone quiet), the system first tries again by itself, and the alarm is a **Warning** saying which try it's on. After 3 tries, or an hour, whichever comes first (both set in **System Configuration → Thresholds & gates**), it's a **Trip**. The hour counts even if the failure doesn't come again (a task that runs only at night, for instance): once it's up, the board's watch makes it a trip, saying its tries ran out (2026-11-20). A refusal or running out of budget is a trip at once: there's nothing to try. A trip is the system placing a pause (2026-11-20): it pauses what its fault touches, and only that: one workflow when its task keeps failing, one task, or one outside service out of budget; all gathering only when the crawler itself is down (runs that fail outright or never report). It never turns anything off: every switch stays as the person who set it left it, and the pause shows beside it, "On · Paused by the system (a trip): …", on Automation, on the service's card and on the board. A slow call or job is never a trip: it's a warning that says how slow, stops nothing, and clears by itself when the speed returns. A call that brings in records is judged per record, so a big batch isn't called slow. 450: ### I'm on the Traversence team. Why does an alarm read the same on the board, in Pulse and on my phone?
451: Since 2026-11-20 (decisions/0176) an alarm is one thread, drawn in one shape wherever you see it:
452: - **Title:** what happened, in plain words.
453: - **Status:** where it stands now: "Warning since …", "Paused by the system (a trip) since …", "Escalation Notice …", or "Closed … Recovered", with its times labelled.

456: - **Details ▾:** the raw evidence (status codes, the server's own words), folded.

458: The board shows all of it. Pulse shows everything but the details. A push shows only the title and status. The logbook keeps the finished story.
