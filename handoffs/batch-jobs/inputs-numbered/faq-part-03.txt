636: ### Can I just describe what's going on?
637: Yes. Type it the way you'd say it: "the power went out", "stuck on the highway", "lost my keys", "job interview coming up", "I can't pay my bills". 645: ### What shows while I'm typing a search?
646: Only predictions, so nothing half-finished gets in the way. First **Search for "…"**, then ways your words could carry on (what you typed, with the rest in bold), and sometimes a **Maybe try**: a phrase the search noticed works well, offered as its own suggestion (only from words it understands, never anything personal), then **Go straight to**: what you might be thinking of ("Power out", "Gas for the car"), things whose names start with what you've typed, kinds of things, and towns and counties anywhere in the US. Places come first, right under the site's own tools (ours first, then the bigger towns elsewhere, marked "see what's there"), since where you're looking shapes everything else. It offers as many as match, up to 15, and keeps up with every letter until you stop, press Enter or pick one. While it's still one word ("for"), places and kinds of things come first, then listings and outdoor places that start with it, then stories, groups and experiences. When your words end in a town you're still typing ("moving to sea"), it offers them with each town that fits ("moving to Seattle, WA"). States show too, from two letters or the state's code ("ok" offers **Oklahoma**): "wash" offers **Washington**, the state, and its searches stay on the state, never Washington, DC. When something's name matches only past your Distance setting ("Ford": Show Low Ford, 38 mi, with 10 mi set), one row says how far the nearest is; tap it to widen to that distance. A word search doesn't know, like a model number ("Ford F150"), doesn't stop it: it suggests from the words it knows ("Ford") and the kind of place those names are (car dealers), and asks if you'd like to suggest the new word. When no name has all your words ("hearing test"), it shows the places the full search would find instead, so there's always something to tap. Many places that share a name at one spot ("Big Lake Dam…", "Big Lake Boating…") show as one line, **Big Lake · 9 places**; tap it to see them all. Until you type a space, search treats your last word as unfinished and finishes it the way you most likely mean: "live mu" offers **live music** and shows music venues, "self stor" shows Self Storage. Words that only describe a thing ("new", "live", "best") narrow what it is instead of changing it, so "new music" means music places. When your words clearly mean a kind of place, that kind and its places come first; a business whose name only shares a word comes after (a nurse named Musick isn't music). When many visitors who typed the same words picked the same kind, its line says "most picked": a label only, it never moves anything up. That's counted by the words and the kind only, never who, and each visit counts once, so one person repeating never changes it. When your words name a situation ("can't find my car"), its answer shows right there with the nearest places and **Call**. Tap any of them, or press **Enter**, and the full results appear with Suggestions and Quick Search. Clicking into the box selects what's there, so typing replaces it. **Esc** clears the words; press it again to close. On a phone, the **Location** line at the top also shows what Search Directory and Distance are set to. Tap it to open them, or tap ✎ to change the place. The likeliest come first: a name you've typed in full before one you've only started ("phoenix" puts Phoenix before Phoenixville, which is only 7 of its 12 letters), whole words before part of a word, and what's in the place you've set before the same name elsewhere ("plum" in Show Low offers Plumbers before Plum City, WI). Suggestions start once you've typed three letters; before that, your recent and quick searches show. When nothing fits the letters yet, it says so and Enter still searches them. On a computer, the place at the top of the search has a **×** beside it: tap it to search everywhere.

Words that name a kind of place come first as that kind: "dog park" (or "dog p") offers **Dog parks**, nearest first; "playg" offers Playgrounds. Tap the one you mean. The page you're on sets the order, never what's offered: on Let's Explore places, the outdoors and culture come first; in the Directory, businesses and food come first. Top to bottom, it answers in order: **where** (the Location line, asking there when your words need a place, like "near me"), **what your words mean** (a spelling offer, the full words a partly typed word is becoming, a situation like "Found someone's belongings" with its first answers, and the kinds of place it may mean), then ways to keep typing ("found a dog"; a word not finished yet, like "jalapen", offers the word it finishes), then the places and listings themselves. Press Enter (or the search icon) for every result; on a phone, **See all results for …** at the foot does the same. On the Directory page, **See them all in the directory list** at the foot of the results puts your words into the list and map below; Enter does the same. The words searched stay on the list as a filter ("“Found” ×"); opening the search again starts empty, for new words. Closing the search with × (or Escape, or a tap outside it) clears that filter and shows the whole directory again. After Enter the results open the same way as while typing: a word that starts a situation ("found": Found someone's belongings, Lost or found pet) leads, then everything that matches.

650: ### What happens when I search "near me"?
651: "Near me", "nearby", "closest" and "close to me" tell search where to look, so they aren't matched as words: "concerts near me" searches for concerts. If you've set a place, it shows the **nearest**, each with how far it is, with no distance cut-off: our listings first, then what OpenStreetMap has. For everyday errands (coffee, groceries, gas) near means within 5 miles; for the outdoors (hikes, camping, fishing, parks), within 30. If fewer than 3 are that near, it says so above the results ("Our results for coffee near Phoenix are lacking: the nearest of ours is 12 mi away"), and we hear about it: the words and the town (never who) go to our team, and the town goes to our crawler to find more. A hike counts by what a place is, not just its name: peaks, canyons, trails, preserves and parks come up for "hikes near me", and so do OpenStreetMap's trailheads. The Location line says **Near you** when the place came from your device. If you haven't set a place, **Where are you?** opens as soon as you type "near me" (and again if you press Enter), and it tries **Auto-detect** straight away (your browser asks permission, once; if you've blocked it, it says so). Or type your town or ZIP. It shows only what sets where you are: no trips, regions or distance, since "near me" is 2 miles around you. Nothing from everywhere shows until you set one, on any page, the business directory included: "near me" only makes sense around you. Just "near me", with nothing else, shows everything near you, nearest first (up to 25; with fewer than 3 of ours, what OpenStreetMap has too), then asks **Is there something specific you'd like to search for?** with one-tap choices (coffee, places to eat, groceries, gas, a pharmacy, things to do, where to stay, parks, health care). Close the picker and the results keep **Set my location** and **Use my location**. While you're still typing "near m", it offers **near me** to finish it. We don't keep your location; it's only used to set the place you search from.

664: ### What happens if I misspell something?
665: Search never changes your words on its own. If a word isn't one it knows, it asks **Did you mean…?** and offers up to three of the closest words, the likeliest first: the ones that keep the most of what you typed, then the same first letter and the same sound. Each option says what it would find, like "bathroom: A restroom". Tap the one you meant. It asks while you type, too, once a word is finished. It only offers real slips: one letter off in a short word, two in a longer one. A word search understands, like "cardio" or "x-rays", is never "corrected", and neither is a word used here: a town's or a landmark's name, or a word in the names and descriptions of the businesses we list and the pages we read ("Blue Hills" is never "blue halls"). Two letters swapped count as one slip ("motle": motel). The words it offers come first from what's used here, the more often the likelier (our listings, the places named on pages we read, our towns and landmarks), then from the site's own dictionary (the same one behind spell check in forms), so a common word nobody has listed yet still gets offered: "swiming" offers **swimming**. When a word looks like a slip, "Did you mean" comes first, before anything else in the results. A slip always gets an offer: when nothing closer is found, the closest words our listings use are offered ("resturants": restaurants), and words run together are offered apart when they name a kind of place ("icecream": ice cream). While you're still typing a word, finishing it comes first: "jala" offers jalapeno, not a guess like "java"; a guess is offered only when nothing starts with what you've typed. The word it would finish is listed first too, above **Search for** what you've typed so far.

What people open after a search keeps improving it.
