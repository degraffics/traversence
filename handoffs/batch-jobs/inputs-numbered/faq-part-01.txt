The **master switch** pauses everything that gathers or creates content at once: the crawler's runs on Railway end as soon as they start (even if Railway's schedule comes back), the site's own workflow jobs skip (Find it and guide drafts, guides on demand, suggested journeys, the heritage catalog, the listings pipeline, landmark photos), and the AI features and outside calls that serve them wait. Below it you can pause one **workflow** (W0–W14; W0 is the indexer, which Start the system switches on first), switch off one crawler **task**, or pause one outside **service**. Pausing asks why; each switch shows its reason, who set it and when, and the page keeps a list of the changes. A paused thing shows as paused, never as a failure, and raises no warning in Pulse. Since 2026-11-14 the master switch is off ("Paused by Jason, 2026-11-14, until the crawler review"), so nothing gathers until someone switches it on.

339: ### I'm on the Traversence team. What are workflows, tasks, routes and services on the Automation page?
340: Four layers, and a call happens only when every one of them is on. A **workflow** is a job of work start to finish, such as Listings (W1) or Guides on demand (W5). A **task** is one piece of a workflow's work: one of the crawler's capture tasks, a scheduled job on the site, or an AI feature. A **route** is a task's use of one outside service: AI use sends each feature to its providers in order (a menu to Google Document AI, then AWS, then Claude), and the crawler's coverage task asks OpenStreetMap and Wikidata. A **service** is the company or registry called, with its card in Outside services. Pause a service and every route to it closes: the crawler holds its addresses (a task that needs it reports "held"), the site's callers skip it, and an AI feature routed to it waits or takes its next provider. Services that business operations depend on (billing, payments, email, files, sign-in, the site itself, guides' weather and roads) aren't paused from there, and each says why. The ⓘ on the page explains it too.

351: ### I'm on the Traversence team. What does a service's stage mean, and how do I pause one?
352: Every outside service on **System Configuration → Service register** (https://traversence.com/admin/config/register) has a stage, shown in its row and first on its card, and the list filters by it. **Added**: a release brought it in and it waits for its key (one that came in switched off also waits for a person to activate it). **Key in**: its key is in place, and it waits for **Activate**. **Active**: its workflows can call it, inside its caps and paces. **Paused**: nothing of ours calls it until it's activated again; its key stays. **Retired**: kept as a record of why it stopped and what replaced it; the first is Overpass, OpenStreetMap's live query server, retired on 2026-11-19 at its community's request and replaced by our own copy from Geofabrik (OpenStreetMap itself stays active). **Pause** (with why) and **Activate** are on the service's card and on its row on the System Control Board; Activate runs the switch-on check first and stays paused, saying why, if a line is red. Each change is kept on the card under **Its stages**, with who, when and why. Services whose calls are business operations (Bluehost, payments, email and the like) aren't paused from here, and the card says so. A service's key is entered on its own card (see *How do I add or change a service's key?*). **Retire it…** (folded on the card, with why and what replaced it) stops everything of ours calling it and keeps its card, record and data; its row says **Retired**, nothing can switch it on until **Bring it back** (which leaves it paused until it's activated, through its check), and the Retired filter lists them. Under the list, **Proposed** is where you name a service you'd like added (its name, its site and what it would be for): a build request only, with no address, key or call until a release adds it; **Drop it**, and **Propose again**. **Removed** keeps the record of a service whose code and key are gone (none yet). Each service is defined in one place in the site's code, so a new one is added in one go (decisions/0173).

354: ### I'm on the Traversence team. How do I add or change a service's key?
355: On the service's card in **System Configuration → Service register** (https://traversence.com/admin/config/register), under **Its key**: each key is named, never shown, with whether it's in place. **Add its key** (or **Replace**) takes it in a hidden box and writes it into the site's settings for you, no cPanel trip, then runs the service's **Check** at once: a pass is the end of it; a fail says what the service answered, and **Put the old one back** undoes it (the old key is kept until a Check passes or you **Forget the old one**). **Remove the key** takes it out, kept the same way. Only the Founder can do these, never on staging, and the site's log records who changed which key and when, never the key itself. The Park Service's key (NPS_API_KEY, free from https://api.data.gov/signup/) is optional: it's for the Park Service's photos, and its records site needs none. Keys the crawler keeps on Railway (Google Places, Tavily, Brave, Recreation.gov, the Hugging Face token) are set there: the card links to the crawler's Variables on Railway, and **Check** confirms it once the crawler reports it has the key (never its value); Railway restarts the crawler when a variable changes.

366: ### I'm on the Traversence team. What happens when an outside service refuses us or stops answering?
367: The site and the crawler notice it, stop asking, and tell you, while everything that doesn't need that service carries on. Each failure is sorted by what it is: refused us (a block, like "406 Not Acceptable"), our key refused, over its quota, asked us to slow down, didn't answer, a server error, or an answer we can't read, and kept with its evidence (what the server said, when, and whether it was the crawler on Railway or the site). A block, a refused key or a used-up quota stops every call to that server until someone marks it resolved, or, for other services, until its light check says it's lifted; OpenStreetMap's servers get no contact at all while they refuse us, not even their status page, until a person marks them resolved; we never change our name to get round a block. When a server asks us to slow down (a 429, or a 503 that says so, as Common Crawl's data server's "SlowDown: Please reduce your request rate" does), we wait twice as long as for other failures, longer each time (or exactly as long as it says), so we ask that server half as often; it's a warning that clears itself when the server answers, never a trip, however long it lasts. A service's card says where its wait stands now (2026-11-20): "Backing off until …" while it waits, "Its wait ended …: the next call tries it again" once it's over, and "Paused by the system (a trip): no calls until its check passes" when a trip holds it, never a date that's passed or one far off. While it waits it doesn't hold what needs that service: only a refusal, a refused key, a used-up quota, or a wait that trips at its limit (6 in a row or 6 hours) holds a workflow's switch-on check. Its **Check it now** is greyed, saying when the next try is: pressing it would ask that server again straight after it asked us to slow down. Timeouts and server errors wait a little and try again by themselves, longer each time; after 6 in a row, or 6 hours, it's a trip on the Control Board, and calls to that server wait for an admin's **Resolve**. Each issue that needs a person raises one alarm on the **System Control Board** (and so in **Pulse**) naming the exact issue and the evidence, with **Check it now** (or, once it has tripped, **Check and start again**) and the service's card a press away; a passing check is what closes it (2026-11-19: no Mark resolved); it also shows on the service's card in **System Configuration → Outside services**. OpenStreetMap's Overpass servers are retired (2026-11-19): we no longer ask them at all, so there's no alarm for them; anything open about them closed as "Retired: we no longer use Overpass". A crawler task whose service is held says "Held", never "Failed", and the crawler's other work runs as usual. That holds for the call that trips the hold too, so a crawler run whose only trouble is a held service ends ok on Railway, never "Crashed". OpenStreetMap's main server (overpass-api.de) has refused our crawler since November 13: it stays on the list but isn't asked, or checked, while it refuses us. When OpenStreetMap tells us we may, a person presses Check and start again; it then comes back at half our budget for two weeks. Each service's servers are listed on its card, where the team can add one or pause one and see why one is held. The crawler now asks the other servers for one kind of place at a time, in small queries, one after another, and doesn't ask again for the same town and kind for three months. First, though, it reads our own copy of OpenStreetMap: Arizona's and New Mexico's map files, downloaded each week from Geofabrik (a free mirror), hold the kinds of place it looks for in a town (places to eat and stay, things to do, shops, services, groceries and gas, places of worship, campgrounds and most health care), so it asks the servers only for what our copy doesn't hold (a kind the files don't carry, or a town in another state). While every server refuses us, it carries on from our copy, and what it can't find there waits for a server, nothing lost. Those places are still OpenStreetMap's: a lead and a link, checked on each business's own website like any other. Web searches work the same way: when Tavily fails or is held, Brave answers instead, and the switch is logged. A paid service stands in for a free one only when a director switches it on, for one need, with its own monthly cap (**System Configuration → Thresholds & gates**, off until then): Google Places to confirm a business when its own website, the registries and both searches found nothing (only Google's map link is kept, never its details; a match raises the business's confidence, but never publishes a new find on its own), and Claude to read pages into facts when Gemini's free allowance can't. Each use goes on the cost ledger, and the pre-flight shows it on that need's line. Our own use stays at a quarter of what OpenStreetMap's public servers allow (2,500 queries a day, the crawler and the site together, and half that while a server blocks us and for two weeks after); at most one query a minute, spread over the day; Google checks and web searches are spread evenly through the day too, and when a service tells us to wait, we wait exactly as long as it says. The team sets these numbers in **System Configuration → Thresholds & gates**. Our requests carry our address (support@traversence.com) so the services we use can reach us.

369: ### Why doesn't Traversence ask OpenStreetMap's servers any more?
370: Because OpenStreetMap's community asked us not to. Its public Overpass servers are run by volunteers to help people who edit the map, not as a back end for websites and apps; their answer to our statement (https://community.openstreetmap.org/t/150369) was that an app's fair share is about 50 requests a day, and to stop using them altogether. So since 2026-11-19 we don't, from our site or our crawler, and nothing retries them. We read our own copy of OpenStreetMap instead: Geofabrik's free weekly files for Arizona, New Mexico, Utah, Colorado and Nevada (the last three, and health care, added the same day). What that means: places from OpenStreetMap show in those five states only, and a kind of place the copy doesn't carry comes from our other sources. In the admin, a crawler run that met one says so on W1's row of the System Control Board ("some places aren't in our OpenStreetMap copy"), and OpenStreetMap's card in **System Configuration → Service register** (https://traversence.com/admin/config/register#svc-osm) lists its servers as retired.
