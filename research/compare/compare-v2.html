Traversence vs Wix and Squarespace

A business listing that sells online with its own store domain: costs against benefits. Prepared 2026-11-27 for the Founder's review with Gemini. Version 2: adds Basic against plain websites, a SWOT, where to compete, pricing levers and a suggestion (§6–10). Our figures: commercial.md and the decisions on main, checked against the live mirror (Sync 2026-11-27 12:50 UTC). Their figures: their official pricing and help pages, as of 2026-11-27 (see §6 for how they were read).

1. Bottom line

On price alone, Traversence Core ($79.95 a month) costs more than the comparable Wix and Squarespace plans ($29–$49 a month billed yearly). Card processing is the same for all of them at about 2.9% + 30¢, and none of the three takes a platform cut on its standard store plans (Squarespace Basic is the exception, at 2%). At $2,000 a month in sales, Core costs about $51 a month more than Wix Core or Squarespace Core. At $10,000 it costs about the same $51 more, except against Squarespace Basic, which Core beats by about $139. What Core adds that they don't is a place in a regional directory and editorial guides, GO Codes and short links, and a team close to rural shops. But today card checkout, Connect your domain and domain sales are built and switched off, the site is closed to search engines, and the directory's traffic is unproven. Wix and Squarespace have mature site builders, templates, app markets, abandoned-cart emails and unlimited products. The case for Core is local reach, not a cheaper store, and it can't be made on traffic until the site opens to search and the 1A content is live. Basic ($29.95) is not cheaper than a plain Wix or Squarespace site, but most of what it sells is live today, so it's our strongest proof point now (§6). The suggestion in §10: keep list prices, lead with Basic for presence and Core for commerce, run 60-day Core trials through the chambers, and decide on any price cut on April 30, 2027, with data.

4. Benefits, and where they're stronger

Only Traversence

  • A place in a regional directory and its stories. The listing, its items in the shared Marketplace and in guides (the Connection Layer). Wix and Squarespace bring no audience. The value depends on our traffic, which is unproven (§5).
  • No paid ranking. Free and paid listings rank on trust alone (0007), so a small shop doesn't compete with bigger budgets.
  • GO Codes and short links on trvgo.us, dynamic from Core, with scan counts. A printed code never breaks: when a plan ends it falls back to the free listing (§1.3, 0094).
  • Manual payments on every plan, Free included. Zelle, transfer, check, pay at pickup. Fits counter shops with no card account (Plan.php manual_pay: free).
  • Square catalog and stock sync from Basic (built, waiting on Square's live keys) for shops that already use Square at the counter.
  • Quotes and invoices, discount codes, sales tax, shipping and pickup, customer records and store policies from Basic, live in the business portal (portal.php).
  • Local people: field staff, and White-Glove setup from Strategic; print, signs and video sold as add-ons (§2).
  • No ad pixels or retargeting on trvgo.us; scans are counted, never who scanned.

Where Wix and Squarespace are stronger

  • Price: $19–$49 a month billed yearly against $79.95. Core's extra $51 a month has to be earned in traffic.
  • Mature site builders and templates: many templates, drag-and-drop editing, years of polish. Our storefront has four ready themes and a script-free page (0139 5a); Storefront Studio's blocks come later (0159).
  • App markets (Wix App Market, Squarespace Extensions) for shipping, accounting, reviews, booking and more. We have none.
  • Store features we haven't built: abandoned-cart recovery emails (Wix from Core; Squarespace on all four), built-in email campaigns, and unlimited or 50,000 products against our 100.
  • Payments live today, including Apple Pay, Google Pay and PayPal on Wix. Ours need the platform keys set; PayPal comes later (0135 §6).
  • A free domain for the first year and a domain that works today. Ours is built and switched off.
  • Search traffic now: their sites can be indexed from day one. Ours are closed to search engines until the lock lifts.
  • Scale and 24/7 support (Wix: priority on Business Elite).

8. B. Where we win, and where we shouldn't compete

Compete here

  • Local discovery traffic: the directory and guides bring visitors no builder brings (§0.B). It has to be shown in counts once the lock lifts.
  • Regional trust: ranking by trust, never by payment (0007), and the "made in <region>" label (launch plan §10).
  • Physical to digital: GO Codes on doors, counters and handouts, counted back (0094; 0181's multiplier kit).
  • Zero commission: 0% to us on every plan (§0.A).
  • Local people: the Founder with chambers and venues, and White-Glove from Strategic (0181; §2).
  • Presence at Basic's price for shops with no website (§6 above).

Don't compete here

  • Site builders and templates: they have years of design tools; our storefront is four themes on purpose (0139).
  • App markets and integrations: we have none.
  • Large catalogs: caps of 25 to 1,000 against unlimited (0135 §7).
  • Lowest plan price: a builder will always undercut a plan that carries content and people.
  • Store automation: abandoned-cart emails and campaigns aren't built or planned at Core.

For a business that already has a good Wix or Squarespace site, the pitch is "keep your site, add the listing." A free listing links to it, Basic adds presence, and GO Codes can point at either. It isn't "switch."

10. D. Recommendation (a suggestion for the Founder, not decided)

Suggestion: keep list prices. Prove the case with what's already decided and live, in two moves.
  1. Lead with Basic for presence; keep Core for commerce. Most of what Basic sells is live today, at about a plain website's price (§6). Core's headline features are switched off. Offer Basic at list price, yearly at $299.50 (lever 0 + 3, no ruling). Move a business to Core when it wants card checkout and its own domain, and Core's live keys are set (launch plan F.4).
  2. Run Core trials through the chambers. Issue 60-day Core trial codes as GO Codes through the up to 3 co-op chambers and the other multipliers (levers 2 + 6; 0108, 0034, 0181; no ruling). Start them only once card checkout is live, so a trial shows what it sells. Extra cost for 4a: about $800 deferred, and AI allowances held within E1.
Hold levers 1, 4 and 5 (Tier 5) until there's data.
Proof metrics, weekly, counts only (launch plan §9.5):
  • claims;
  • taps per listing, our referral traffic;
  • Basic payers, and Basic → Core upgrades;
  • Core trial → paid;
  • the share paying yearly.
Checkpoints:
  • March 31, 2027, against the CEO's projection: 25–40 claims, 4–7 paying.
  • Decision point April 30, 2027 (4a's date). If 10 Core trials have converted, keep prices. If trial → paid is below 1 in 3 and owners name price as the reason, asked in person by the Founder (no new tool), the CEO brings a Tier 5 brief on a Core-lite (lever 1).
"Dominant over Wix and Squarespace" can't be measured directly with what we count. What can be proved is that our listings get visitors and that businesses pay for that.
