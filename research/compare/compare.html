Traversence vs Wix and Squarespace

A business listing that sells online with its own store domain: costs against benefits. Prepared 2026-11-27 for the Founder's review with Gemini. Our figures: commercial.md and the decisions on main, checked against the live mirror (Sync 2026-11-27 12:50 UTC). Their figures: their official pricing and help pages, as of 2026-11-27 (see §6 for how they were read).

1. Bottom line

On price alone, Traversence Core ($79.95 a month) costs more than the comparable Wix and Squarespace plans ($29–$49 a month billed yearly). Card processing is the same for all of them at about 2.9% + 30¢, and none of the three takes a platform cut on its standard store plans (Squarespace Basic is the exception, at 2%). At $2,000 a month in sales, Core costs about $51 a month more than Wix Core or Squarespace Core. At $10,000 it costs about the same $51 more, except against Squarespace Basic, which Core beats by about $139. What Core adds that they don't is a place in a regional directory and editorial guides, GO Codes and short links, and a team close to rural shops. But today card checkout, Connect your domain and domain sales are built and switched off, the site is closed to search engines, and the directory's traffic is unproven. Wix and Squarespace have mature site builders, templates, app markets, abandoned-cart emails and unlimited products. The case for Core is local reach, not a cheaper store, and it can't be made on traffic until the site opens to search and the 1A content is live.

Billed monthly instead, Wix totals rise by $7, $7 and $13, and Squarespace's by $6, $10, $16 and $40. At $2,000, for example, Wix Core is $109 and Squarespace Core $112. What it shows. Because processing is the same everywhere, the difference is the plan price. Core costs $50.95 a month more than Wix Core or Squarespace Core at any volume: $611 a year. Core comes out ahead only against Squarespace Basic once sales pass about $3,050 a month, where Basic's 2% fee overtakes Core's higher price. To justify the gap, Core has to bring in sales the shop wouldn't get otherwise. At a 30% gross margin, that's about $170 a month in extra sales, or about four $40 orders.

4. Benefits, and where they're stronger

Only Traversence

  • A place in a regional directory and its stories. The listing, its items in the shared Marketplace and in guides (the Connection Layer). Wix and Squarespace bring no audience. The value depends on our traffic, which is unproven (§5).
  • No paid ranking. Free and paid listings rank on trust alone (0007), so a small shop doesn't compete with bigger budgets.
  • GO Codes and short links on trvgo.us, dynamic from Core, with scan counts. A printed code never breaks: when a plan ends it falls back to the free listing (§1.3, 0094).
  • Manual payments on every plan, Free included. Zelle, transfer, check, pay at pickup. Fits counter shops with no card account (Plan.php manual_pay: free).
  • Square catalog and stock sync from Basic (built, waiting on Square's live keys) for shops that already use Square at the counter.
  • Quotes and invoices, discount codes, sales tax, shipping and pickup, customer records and store policies from Basic, live in the business portal (portal.php).
  • Local people: field staff, and White-Glove setup from Strategic; print, signs and video sold as add-ons (§2).
  • No ad pixels or retargeting on trvgo.us; scans are counted, never who scanned.

Where Wix and Squarespace are stronger

  • Price: $19–$49 a month billed yearly against $79.95. Core's extra $51 a month has to be earned in traffic.
  • Mature site builders and templates: many templates, drag-and-drop editing, years of polish. Our storefront has four ready themes and a script-free page (0139 5a); Storefront Studio's blocks come later (0159).
  • App markets (Wix App Market, Squarespace Extensions) for shipping, accounting, reviews, booking and more. We have none.
  • Store features we haven't built: abandoned-cart recovery emails (Wix from Core; Squarespace on all four), built-in email campaigns, and unlimited or 50,000 products against our 100.
  • Payments live today, including Apple Pay, Google Pay and PayPal on Wix. Ours need the platform keys set; PayPal comes later (0135 §6).
  • A free domain for the first year and a domain that works today. Ours is built and switched off.
  • Search traffic now: their sites can be indexed from day one. Ours are closed to search engines until the lock lifts.
  • Scale and 24/7 support (Wix: priority on Business Elite).

Other risks. (1) If we sell Core as "your own store on your own domain" before checkout and domains are on, we promise what isn't live, so the sales wording should follow the switches. (2) Without a free first-year domain, the first-year comparison looks worse still. (3) Card checkout runs on our page, with the gateway's own card fields; if our site is down, so is the shop's checkout. (4) The legal texts (Terms, Seller Agreement) are drafts awaiting the attorney (launch plan F.7). (5) Squarespace's prices rose in 2026, but both still undercut us on plan price.

Possible responses, for the Founder (suggestions, not decided): turn on Stripe and Square live keys and Connect your domain before Core is sold for e-commerce; consider a first-year domain credit on yearly Core (it would change 0139/0169 pricing, so a Founder ruling); raise Core's item cap or add item packs (0135 §7 rules packs out, so a Founder ruling); measure referral traffic per listing once the search lock lifts, so Core's value can be shown in numbers.
