# Reaching rural businesses that are short on money and not online: a proposal (2026-11-27)

Jason, 2026-11-27: "Based on your pdf it seems we may have a market penetration issue, rural economics face severe
financial struggles and are not effective online, so draft a proposal for addressing how we overcome these obstacles."

The CEO's proposal, for Jason. **Decided** marks what's already ruled, with its source; **Suggest** marks what's new,
for his ruling. Facts on 1A from `research/compare/` (v2): Apache County, about 60% broadband, 442 employer
establishments, 29.3% poverty.

## The problem in one line
The businesses we most want can't pay $79.95 for a promise. Many aren't online at all. And our proof, local visitor
traffic, can't show while search engines are locked out.

## The answer: we bring them online at no cost, prove the value with their own numbers, then sell
**1. Get online at $0, and we do the work (Decided).**
- The Free tier is a real, operating product: directory presence, manual publishing, community messaging, 5 GO Codes
  and selling with manual payments (commercial.md §1.1, the Free Tier spec). No card, no website and no skills needed.
- The system builds the listing before the owner arrives: the crawler, the 1A towns and guides (0164; 0181 D; the
  launch plan's stream D). The owner only claims it, checks it and adds hours (launch plan D.4).
- Selling works without a card account: Zelle, check, cash or pay on pickup, marked paid (§1.1). Zero commission
  (§0).

**2. Offline first, for a county where 4 in 10 aren't on broadband (Decided channels).**
- GO Codes on doors, counters, menus and packaging bridge the street to the listing (0094; launch plan 9.1:
  "offline channels carry real weight").
- The Founder's in-person time goes to the multipliers (chambers, guide influencers, venues, civic cornerstones),
  never to one-by-one selling (launch plan 9.0).
- **Suggest:** pages light enough for slow rural connections, checked on the phone at 390px and on a throttled
  connection in the path checker (gate 1).

**3. Prove value with their own numbers before asking for money (Decided mechanism).**
- Every tap on an unclaimed listing is counted (counts only), and the claim invite carries it: "37 people called or
  emailed you through Traversence this month" (0172 §3.3, 0108; launch plan 9.3).
- **The blocker:** the search lock. Until it lifts, the counts stay near zero (launch plan 9.3).
  **Suggest:** set the lock's lift date as early as gates 1, 3 and 6 allow, because every week locked is a week with
  no proof (0181 §4, Brief D).

**4. Make paying fit a thin margin.**
- **Decided:**
  - Basic at $299.50 a year (two months free) for presence;
  - Core trials for Core only;
  - promotions within 0108's profit floor, issued as GO Codes;
  - up to 3 chambers get a comped Co-Op Master Hub (0034).
- **Suggest: someone else pays.** A chamber, a tourism office or a town buys listings for its members through the
  co-op hub, so the business pays nothing. The money comes from the multiplier's budget, not the shop's (extends
  0034; a pricing change, so Tier 5).
- **Suggest: grants, researched before any promise.** Federal and state rural business and tourism programmes may fund
  digital presence for small rural businesses. Data & Content researches which apply (the research tools, 0160 §5),
  with sources; nothing is offered until a person confirms it. $0 to research.
- **Suggest: pay when it works.** A Core trial that converts only when the business's own counts pass a threshold
  (for example, its first orders through the listing). It costs deferred revenue only. Tier 5, a pricing rule.

**5. Help them become good online (Decided services, new delivery).**
- White-Glove setup and agency services exist (commercial.md §2, the agency menu).
- **Suggest:** free "get your business found" sessions at chambers and libraries, run by the Founder or a chamber
  partner with a printed GO Code kit. A cost in time only.

## What to watch, and when to decide
- **Measures:**
  - claims;
  - taps per listing;
  - Basic payers;
  - Core trial → paid;
  - sponsored (co-op) listings;
  - the share of listings with hours.
- **Ladder checks:** 4a (10 paying Core by about April) on 2027-04-30; review on 2027-03-31.
- **Fallback:** if trial → paid is under 1 in 3 and owners name price, a Core-lite brief goes to Jason (the v2 comparison's
  recommendation).

## What needs Jason's ruling (Tier 5)
1. A sponsored listings price for co-op partners (beyond 0034's three comped hubs).
2. A pay-when-it-works Core trial.
3. The search lock's lift date (already Brief D).

Everything else above is decided already, or is research or time only.

## Revised: content to commerce, by the platform's own power (Jason, 2026-11-27)

Jason: "Not sure if that is really the solution. Events using our tools, marketplace features and showcases? We need
to really look at how to harness the power of our platform to push content to commerce success."

His direction replaces the approach above as the lead. The sections above stay as supporting tactics. **The
platform makes the demand and the sales; businesses join because it already sells for them.** It builds on the
decided pipeline: the Authority Engine (content) → the Connection Layer → the Conversion Engine (direct-to-operator
commerce; commercial.md §0).

### 1. Events: one event brings many businesses at once (0134, 0135)
- **Decided, designed, not built:** 0134 makes events one site-wide tool:
  - one store, with the crawler's Live events as a source;
  - pages at `/event/`, a calendar, Going and Interested;
  - GO Codes for events;
  - tickets and registration as catalog items on our order system: from Core through the business's own gateway, on
    Free and Basic paid the business's way (0134 §6a; 0135).
- 0135's note puts **event tickets, bookings, harvest and made-to-order** in Jan 1's Experiences & Regional Goods.
- **Suggest, the wedge:** we run the digital side of 1A's community events (markets, fairs, rodeos, art walks,
  festivals) for the organizer, who is a decided multiplier:
  - the event page and map;
  - a vendor list, each vendor a listing (claim or free);
  - booth GO Code signs;
  - pre-orders and pickup through the Marketplace;
  - tickets through our cart.

  One event signs up its whole vendor roster in a day, and every vendor sees sales tied to its own listing.
- **After the event:** a story (the magazine model, 0164 and 0056 notes) features the event and its vendors, linking
  to each listing and its items. It's content that ranks and feeds the next event.

### 2. Showcases: features that sell (decided parts, new packaging)
- The magazine model: stories about makers and businesses from public sources, published without approvals, within
  the privacy line (a public role and public act).
- The Strategic tier's Connection Feature (the Owner Spotlight Interview) and storefronts on trvgo.us (0139, live).
- **Suggest:** "Made in <geo hub>" collections (0135's regional label) and seasonal showcases (harvest, holiday,
  rodeo). Each is a story, a collection and a GO Code poster. Free cornerstone businesses are featured first, because
  a feature is the proof that brings the claim.

### 3. Trails and journeys: a route that buys at every stop
- Journeys and trips are live (0174 R1–R4): Add to a trip, Send to Maps, sharing.
- **Suggest:** food, art and made-here trails in 1A, with a GO Code passport (a scan at each stop). Each stop links to
  that business's items and bookings.

### 4. The Connection Layer: every guide has a buy button
- Guides and stories already surface listings (commercial.md, the Connection Layer).
- **Suggest:** where a business sells, its card in a guide carries **Book, Order or Tickets** straight to its catalog,
  not only Call and Directions. The commerce is in the content.

### What it changes in the plan
- **Jan 1:** a minimum of events: crawler events plus host-posted event pages, tickets as catalog items (0135's Jan 1
  kinds), GO Codes, and one pilot event in 1A with its vendors. The full 0134 tool comes in Q1.
- **Lanes:** the CMO with Editorial and Marketing for showcases, trails and stories; Sales for event organizers and
  vendors; IT and Frontend & Shop for the events build.
- **Measure:** orders and bookings through listings, vendors per event, vendors claimed, and showcase → claim. These
  join the ladder metrics.
- **Tier 5:** scheduling the 0134 build before Jan 1 is a scope change against 0181's gates, so it's Jason's call. The
  rest are suggestions within decided lanes.
