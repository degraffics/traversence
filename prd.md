*Version: 2026-09-25*
*Governance tier: PRD — the "what to build" layer. This document changes per release. It did not exist as a standalone file before this draft; everything below was assembled from status/tier notes already present in `routes.md` (formerly `Page-Structure-and-Routing.md`) and `SAOP.docx`. ## Tier 2 — Active Development

**Restructured into sub-phases 2026-09-24, per direction — see `decisions/0020-tier2-dependency-mapping-and-sequencing.md` for the full dependency mapping and value/effort reasoning behind this order.** The flat list this section used to be is now a firm build sequence, not just a bucket of "not Tier 1, not Tier 3."

**MVP Launch Line, per `decisions/0021` (Accepted):** everything in Tier 1 above, plus Tier 2.1 immediately below, is IN — required before public launch, not just "next to build." Tier 2.2 onward is OUT — deferrable to an ongoing post-launch roadmap, with one hard rule: if Tier 2.3 (the AI article pipeline) ever ships before launch, its HITL review counterpart has to ship in the same release, never split apart. **The launch trigger itself is a readiness threshold, not a calendar date, per direction:** as soon as the IN list above is genuinely working and deployable, even at a basic level, that's the launch signal — everything else continues as post-launch refinement rather than a backlog that has to clear first. Confirmed, per direction: the pilot region's listings were seeded via direct SQL, not the live crawler pipeline, so there's no current guardrail gap — but the crawler pipeline now has a standing prerequisite of its own before it's ever turned on for real new ingestion: its admin review-queue counterpart (`admin/listings.php`/`admin/review-queue.php`) has to exist first, per the same "No Split Loops" reasoning. See `decisions/0021`.

**Tier 2.1 — Foundational, ship first, nothing else in Tier 2 depends on anything upstream of these — also part of the MVP Launch Line, not deferrable:**
- Public business detail pages (`/listing/{slug}`) — the tier's true dependency root; several later items link to or route toward it.
- Macro Hub, Geo-Hub, and Micro-Cluster landing pages — the tier's other dependency root; real data already exists (Micro-Cluster has 40 rows). Three later items (2.2's narrative entry point, corridor map, and Chameleon dual-behavioral routing) need these to exist first.

**Tier 2.2 — Completes the core Directory/Discovery loop; depends on 2.1's pages existing to route to and adapt content on:**
- Dual-Gateway landing page (Get Local / Let's Explore split) — currently a single "Get Local" hero. Technically cheap (`/directory` and `/discovery` are already live routes underneath it) but low-leverage until 2.1 ships, or it just routes people into placeholder content.
- Chameleon Filter dual-behavioral content adaptation between `/directory/` and `/discovery/` (`architecture.md` §22).
- Top-down "Let's Explore" narrative entry point.
- Interactive corridor map.

**Tier 2.3 — Content pipeline; depends on 2.1's business detail pages:**
- Editorial article template with `[directory_feed]` shortcode.
- AI-generated, per-listing variant articles (the scraper-to-article pipeline: a partner's catalog is ingested, drafted into one article per product/listing variant, HITL-reviewed, then published into `/discovery/` with traffic routed back to the listing) — mechanism documented in `architecture.md` §25–§26; **explicitly depends on the editorial article template above**, not a parallel build.

**Tier 2.4 — Safety infrastructure; sequenced ahead of the social features it protects, not after (a real dependency `decisions/0020` surfaced, not just a preference):**
- User-side safety controls: block, filter, and report/request-removal of unwanted engagement. Surfaced by `decisions/0010-identity-exposure-and-ai-consent.md`'s resolution that harassment risk is handled through user-side control rather than hiding identity, since public content is attributed to a visible front-end profile (`architecture.md` §7, §11) — which means this mitigation needs to exist *before* 2.5 ships the features that increase that exposure, not after.

**Tier 2.5 — Social/engagement features; depend on 2.4 being live first:**
- User profile feeds and the connection/watch mechanics.
- **Person search, added 2026-09-25, per direction:** find a specific user by name/handle to send a connection request — the lookup half of the existing +Add/Disconnect protocol (`architecture.md` §5), which otherwise assumes the other user is already discovered some other way. Subject to the same no-direct-ID-lookup/profile-based discovery principle `decisions/0010` already sets for public authorship — search matches against a user's public profile/display name, never the internal `actor_user_id`.
- The Centralized Communications Center (cross-entity messaging).

**Tier 2.6 — Remaining admin sub-tools; internally sequenced 2026-09-24 per `decisions/0022`, not a flat bucket:**
- Admin portal shell — already Live as a real placeholder (`admin/adminportal.php`); the shell itself isn't blocking anything below. Every tile pointing at an unbuilt sub-tool below shows a shared "Coming Soon" placeholder rather than a dead link, and every route below sits behind the real `Auth::requireHubAdmin()`/`requireSuperAdmin()` guard from its first commit, stub or not.
- **2.6.1 — `admin/listings.php` (pending-listing review), first.** Not just a bottleneck in the abstract — this is `decisions/0021` §3's standing prerequisite: the crawler pipeline can't turn on for real new ingestion until this exists.
- **2.6.2 — `admin/review-queue.php` (automated-seed cluster review), second.** The other half of `decisions/0021`'s hard coupling condition on Tier 2.3's AI article pipeline.
- **2.6.3 — `admin/hubs.php` (hub/geo-hub management), third.**
- **2.6.4 — `admin/clusters.php` (cluster management), fourth — can safely wait, per direction; structural, not content-gating.**
- **2.6.5 — `admin/users.php` (user management), last.** Real moderation levers (`is_suspended`, `dmca_strike_count`, `listing_access`/`admin_access`) already work through their own existing endpoints; this is a convenience console over mechanisms that already function, not a missing capability.

**Tier 2.7 — Not yet schedulable; needs its own technical-design pass before it can even be sequenced, tracked here so it isn't silently dropped or silently assumed next:**
- Communities & Groups: user-created, charter-governed community spaces (creation gated by trust score, penalty history, and structural creation friction; three-tier guest/registered/member visibility; join-gated threads; a per-community, user-authored charter distinct from `charter.md`) — architecture and governance model documented in `architecture.md` §27. The Level 1/2/3 user-escalation ladder (peer self-serve → Group Charter Steward → System-Level Admin) is the associated conflict-resolution mechanism. No schema or API contract exists yet — that design pass is itself the prerequisite, not just "get to it eventually."

**Tier 2.8 — Market, the fourth primary construct; sequenced last, deliberately bare-bones, per `decisions/0030-four-construct-build-order.md` and `decisions/0031-market-post-and-connect-model.md`:**
- **Launch model decided (`decisions/0031`): post-and-connect, no transaction processing.** A seller (personal or business) posts what they have; an interested party takes a "connect" action; everything past that — negotiation, payment, fulfillment — happens off-platform. Traversence never becomes a payment-processing party at this stage.
- **Minimum real feature shape, per `decisions/0031` — Facebook Marketplace as the UX reference:** a browsable/filterable post grid (photo, price, title, location) as the default view; location + radius filtering reusing the existing ZIP-centroid Directory search (`architecture.md` §4); category browsing alongside search; a "Create new listing" posting flow; personal "Buying"/"Selling" views inside the existing account dashboard (Tier 1); and a "connect" action routed through the existing messaging/inquiry mechanism (the Centralized Communications Center, Tier 2.5) rather than a "Buy Now" or checkout flow. No payment, escrow, or order-management surface at this stage. Launch one side first (B2C, businesses posting to consumers) rather than all of C2C/B2C/B2B/D2U at once, per `decisions/0031`.
- **Explicit exclusion from the reference model, per `decisions/0031`:** no engagement-ranked or paid "picks"/featured placement — post visibility follows the same Trust-Weighted model (`architecture.md` §7) as every other part of the platform, never payment or engagement signals, even though Facebook Marketplace's own equivalent surface works that way.
- **Item detail page, per `decisions/0031`:** title, price, "listed [when] in [city, state]," a photo gallery, structured fields (e.g. Condition) plus free-text description, a "Seller details" view kept separate from the post itself, and the "connect" action surfaced twice — a primary Message button and a lower inline quick-message composer pre-filled with a low-friction starter line. No Buy/checkout/quantity control anywhere on the page.
- **Location privacy distinction, new for Market, per `decisions/0031`:** a personal (C2C) post shows only an approximate area, never a pinpoint address — unlike a Directory listing, which is a business with an already-public storefront address. A business posting through its verified listing can still show its real address. This needs to be built as the default for personal posts, not inherited from however the Directory currently displays location.
- **Quote and inventory mechanics, per `decisions/0032`:** item pricing carries cost/markup (private, `listing_access`-gated, both flat-dollar and percentage markup supported per item) and a computed public price plus tax status; Quote is a real stateful object (Requested → Accepted/Rejected), not a message; completion requires dual confirmation (both seller and buyer confirm) before an item is marked sold — never a unilateral seller self-report; a completed Quote is the platform's first review/rating anchor point. Response-time expectations are self-defined per listing (no platform-wide SLA) — a business sets its own sales process/response expectations inside its own listing profile settings. Personal sellers get a streamlined single-item flow; business listings get a tiered multi-item catalog — **catalog cap resolved 2026-09-25: 250 items, per listing, full stop — there is no account-level or combined/group cap of any kind** (an earlier 500-item combined-group figure was floated and then explicitly removed per direction); Core/Strategic per-listing caps still open. Multi-location inventory management is Strategic/Cornerstone-only, and is never inferred from shared `listing_access` — it requires an explicit **Location Group** link (`decisions/0036`), gated to the listing's owner or a single owner-designated **Executive**-level manager (`decisions/0037`), with each linked listing individually holding its own Cornerstone subscription (`decisions/0035`). Linking listings for shared searchability/admin visibility has **no effect on catalog capacity** — each listing keeps its independent 250-item cap whether linked or not. Communications route per listing to a specific email or assigned listing manager (staff assignment is `listing_access` itself — no separate staff/role system) even inside a linked group; notification channel is in-app (always on) plus opt-in email via SMTP with deep links back to the specific item/thread.
- **Delegated roles and ownership, per `decisions/0037` and `decisions/0038`:** below manager, a listing can have configurable delegated roles — the owner (or the one designated Executive) configures exactly which capabilities a role has, rather than picking from a fixed named-role menu; no role below manager, however configured, ever gains linking or role-creation authority. Ownership itself can never be removed, only transferred to another active/registered user who must explicitly accept (mirroring the existing manager-invite accept pattern); financial responsibility (the Subscription) transfers with it on acceptance, and what happens to existing delegates on transfer is a real choice for both the outgoing and incoming owner, with the incoming owner's choice winning on disagreement.
- **Pricing model typing and the External Source plug-in contract, resolved 2026-09-25 (`decisions/0039`):** catalog items carry a `pricing_model` tag rather than assuming one universal cost/markup shape — `cost_markup` (`decisions/0032`'s original percentage/flat-dollar-on-cost model), `cost_margin` (a distinct calculation targeting profit as a percentage of retail price, never a synonym for markup — the two produce different prices from the same stated percentage), `landed_cost_plus` (shipping and platform/referral fees folded in before a markup or margin target applies), `variant_priced` (cost and price computed per attribute combination — this is what resolves the "Product variants... single-SKU only" gap previously flagged here), `bundle_priced` (a multi-item combination priced as its own object, not any single item's cost), `build_to_order` (no static catalog price; resolved externally at configuration time), and `mirrored` (fully external, read-only price, no local computation). Any listing whose inventory or pricing lives in a third-party system — SJ Portables/ShedSuite is the first real instance, a future OTA/channel-manager integration on the Discovery side is a named future one — plugs in through a generic External Source contract (sync cadence, field mapping, `pricing_model` tag, order-hand-off mechanism, inbound webhook contract), not bespoke per-integration code.
- **Delivery Method contract and buyer-proposed/staff-verified routing, resolved 2026-09-26 (`decisions/0040`):** delivery is always a line on a Market order, not an afterthought, and is priced through a `delivery_method` tag distinct from the item's own `pricing_model` — `carrier_rate` (real-time USPS/UPS/FedEx lookup), `flat_distance_rate` (a merchant-configured free radius, per-mile rate, and optional base fee — never a platform-fixed number), `freight_ltl` (oversized/palletized freight, staff-priced), and `manual` (general fallback). Quotes can now be created with one or more lines left unpriced (most often delivery) — the buyer can't reach Accepted until every line carries a real value, resolving the gap where `decisions/0032`'s Quote model assumed an item's price (and therefore its total) was already known at Requested. Real road-distance routing separates the map/pin-drop UI from the routing engine that supplies actual route geometry (rural road-data quality varies meaningfully by provider — a paid provider tends to beat self-hosted OSRM for remote coverage); a buyer can propose the delivery route themselves by dragging waypoints at intake (their own local knowledge of site access is often better than a generic router's), and staff verifies or directly corrects it (waypoint-dragging only, never freehand) before the delivery line is priced and locked to the Quote.
- **Still needed before this is buildable:** real schema (an item/offer-listing table distinct from `entities`, a `quotes` table, a `location_groups` table, the Executive/delegated-role permission structure, the ownership-transfer mechanism, the `pricing_model` field and its per-model inputs, the External Source contract's own schema, and the `delivery_method`/route-verification schema, per `future-considerations.md` and `decisions/0032`, `0035`–`0040`) and its own technical-design pass — same "not yet schedulable" caveat as Tier 2.7 applies to the design work, even though the model itself is now decided.
- **Sequencing:** after Directory (presence), Discovery (reach), and Connect (relationship) — see `decisions/0030`'s presence → reach → relationship → transaction reasoning. Full in-platform payment / merchant-of-record processing is explicit future expansion, not part of this Tier — see `future-considerations.md`.

## Tier 3 — Future Scope

Pulled from `SAOP.docx` Section 12:

- Public informational pages: services, FAQ, careers.
- E-commerce & subscription infrastructure (`pricing.php`, Stripe subscription processing) — see `commercial.md`, which is currently a skeleton.
- Advanced integrations: direct-to-operator webhooks, automated booking extensions. **Requirement flagged for when this is built:** external consumers (partner APIs, webhooks) should receive app-scoped/contextual identifiers, never the raw internal `actor_user_id`/entity ID — the same no-direct-ID-lookup principle behind `architecture.md` §7's profile-based public authorship, extended to third-party integration surfaces.
- **Website Builder — offsite static export/redeploy, added 2026-09-25 per `decisions/0033`:** a client who wants their own hosting/domain, rather than a Traversence-hosted page, gets a generated deployable site bundle instead of a live API — Traversence stays the sole admin/write path for inventory, content, and communications, and the offsite site is a one-way generated artifact, redeployed either manually or on an auto-sync schedule (client's choice). The offsite site's inquiry/quote form writes back to the Communications Center and Quote workflow (`decisions/0032`) through one lightweight, app-scoped endpoint — not a full public API — so business conducted offsite still flows into the same home system. Distinct from hosted API access above (a separate, developer-facing concept) and from Cornerstone's existing Traversence-hosted "Primary Digital Presence" deliverable (`commercial.md` §2) — this is the option for a client who specifically wants their own hosting. **Real client-facing SEO value, per direction:** Traversence's own Directory/Discovery listing links out to the client's independently-hosted site with a real, followed link, which — alongside the client's own Facebook presence and Google's organic indexing of a real external domain — is inbound authority a page living only inside Traversence's own domain couldn't accumulate for the client. This is the existing Conversion Engine (`commercial.md` §0) delivering concretely for this deployment model, not a new mechanic. **Separately, confirmed per `decisions/0033`:** the generated site also carries a "Powered by Traversence" attribution/backlink pointing the other way, by default — for the aggregate SEO value it gives Traversence itself. Removable upon request for Cornerstone tier only; Core and Strategic keep it regardless.
- **Personalized Insight & Productivity Engine** *(placeholder — not yet designed)*: per-user pattern recognition over a registered user's own activity, meant to streamline their productivity within the system and surface customizable solutions they can deploy across the Resident/Traveler/Community tracks for better engagement. The technical substrate for this already exists (`architecture.md` §5, ADR 0008) — every user's activity already carries `actor_user_id` through the telemetry/DIKW pipeline without being flattened. **Corrected 2026-09-24:** this used to cite `architecture.md` §23, but that section was rewritten to document an unrelated, real feature (Shared Place Context — location resolution for search/navigation, not the telemetry substrate) and no longer covers this ground; the actual dual-attribution mechanism this sentence describes lives in `architecture.md` §5. Shared Place Context (§23) may still end up as a separate, additional input to a future recommendation layer built on top of this substrate — a possibility `decisions/0010` already governs — but it isn't the substrate itself. What's undefined: what an "insight" or "solution" actually is, where it surfaces, what triggers it, and the interaction loop for how a user actually consumes or dismisses one once it surfaces (added 2026-09-24, per direction — a real, previously-unstated piece of "product definition," not just placement) — its consent posture is no longer undefined (see below). **Checked directly against your four-requirement summary, 2026-09-24: three of the four (privacy/compliance disclosure, the consent framework's default, and the classify-at-intake/abstraction-out storage rule) were already tracked here and cross-referenced to their governing ADRs before you listed them — the consent one is now substantially resolved, not just tracked (see below); only "product definition & surface architecture" was genuinely still open, and stays open pending the design pass.** **Confirmed 2026-09-24, per direction: stays filed exactly here, Tier 3, awaiting its own design pass — deliberately not promoted to a new "Phase 8" or any other macro-phase, to avoid a third numbering scheme colliding with `phases.md`'s SDLC stages and this document's own Tier 1/2/3 build-readiness tracks (the same collision `architecture.md` §27 already named "Level 1/2/3" specifically to avoid).** **Standing pre-work item, added 2026-09-24, per direction: even though the feature build itself stays parked, the compliance framework around it doesn't have to wait for the design pass.** Data-mapping, the disclosure language `architecture.md` §24 will need, and the rights-handling structures (access/deletion requests) should be drafted now, so that when the design pass does happen, the storage rules and abstraction layers it has to work within are already settled rather than needing to be re-engineered around a framework written after the fact. **A first concrete piece of that pre-work is already built into the plan: `decisions/0014`'s registration flow now captures the opt-in control itself** (`users.ai_personalization_opt_in`, default off) as a single, low-friction toggle at signup and a matching one in Account Settings — so the consent mechanism and its schema exist correctly, for every user, well before this engine has anything to actually consume that consent for.

## User Stories (First Draft)

Drafted from the features and tracks already documented, not from new input — treat these as a starting point to correct, reprioritize, or throw out, not a finished spec.

**Resident ("Get Local")**
- As a resident, I want to search for local businesses within my ZIP radius, so I can find services near me without wading through irrelevant results. *(Tier 1)*
- As a resident, I want to see a business's current hours and contact info in one place, so I don't waste a trip. *(Tier 1)*
- As a resident, I want to save frequently used places to my account, so I can get back to them quickly. *(Tier 1)*
- As a resident, I want to suggest a correction to a listing's info, so the directory stays accurate for my neighbors. *(Tier 2.1 — needs `/listing/{slug}` to exist first)*

**Traveler ("Let's Explore")**
- As a traveler, I want to explore a region's historical narrative and points of interest along a corridor, so I can plan a multi-day trip with context, not just a list of pins. *(Tier 2.2)*
- As a traveler, I want the interactive map to show me nearby clusters as I move along a route, so I don't have to re-search at every stop. *(Tier 2.2)*
- As a traveler passing through a sparse rural area, I want the platform to still surface something useful nearby, so thin coverage doesn't leave me with an empty page. *(Tier 2.2 — relies on the taxonomy fallback in `architecture.md` §18)*

**Community ("Connect")**
- As a registered user, I want to message a business owner directly through the platform, so I don't have to find their contact info elsewhere. *(Tier 2.5 — behind 2.4's safety controls, per `decisions/0020`)*
- As a community member, I want to follow updates from businesses or topics I care about, so I see relevant activity without searching for it each time. *(Tier 2.5)*

**Business owners**
- As a business owner, I want to claim my existing listing and verify it's really mine, so I control what customers see. *(Tier 1 — live since `decisions/0015`)*
- As a verified business owner, I want to switch into "acting as my business" mode when I make an edit, so my personal account and my business's actions stay clearly separated. *(Tier 1)*

**Administrators**
- As an administrator, I want to review and resolve concurrent ownership claims on the same listing, so only one legitimate owner controls it. *(Tier 1 — live since `decisions/0016`)*

## What's Still Missing

- **Prioritization within tiers — resolved 2026-09-24 for Tier 2.** `decisions/0020-tier2-dependency-mapping-and-sequencing.md` maps real dependencies and breaks Tier 2 into sub-phases 2.1–2.7 above with a firm build order. The same sub-phase-tagging method is now the standing approach for any tier that grows past a handful of items (see `phases.md`), not a one-time fix.
- **Explicit launch-vs-later line — resolved, `decisions/0021` (Accepted).** The boundary is drawn by a "No Split Loops" rule rather than ranking features individually: the launch line can never fall between the two halves of an otherwise-live loop (an intake and the action that completes it) — which is why Tier 2.1 (business detail pages, Hub/Geo-Hub/Micro-Cluster pages) is pulled into the MVP line above even though it's technically still Tier 2 build sequence. The launch trigger itself is a readiness threshold ("as soon as we have a working and deployable platform"), not a fixed date. The one open question this resolution surfaced — whether the crawler pipeline currently bypasses its own HITL guardrail — is answered too: it doesn't; the pilot listings were SQL-seeded, and the pipeline now carries a standing prerequisite (its review-queue counterpart must exist first) before it's ever turned on for real new ingestion.
- **Cross-reference:** full route-by-route status and URLs live in `routes.md` — that stays the single source of truth for routing; don't duplicate it back into this file as it's updated.
- **Privacy/compliance dependency:** the Personalized Insight & Productivity Engine above touches an identified user's own behavior, which is a stronger transparency case than the purpose-limited, anonymous guest-pattern detection in `decisions/0009-guest-activity-attribution-and-pattern-detection.md`. Its design pass should account for `architecture.md` §24's disclosure position before this ships.
- **Consent dependency — fully resolved 2026-09-24.** `decisions/0010-identity-exposure-and-ai-consent.md` is now Accepted with no open parameters — user-contributed content is de-identified by default for AI grounding (§23, §26), and the narrower case (identity actually attached to AI output) needs an explicit control that **defaults to opt-in (off)**, per direction, for the stricter GDPR-aligned posture and a cleaner compliance baseline. **A build-order rule follows from this, per direction:** if and when the Personalized Insight & Productivity Engine's design pass happens, the opt-in control and de-identification toggles are built as the foundational layer *before* the recommendation logic itself, not bolted on after — the same reasoning `decisions/0021`'s "No Split Loops" rule and `decisions/0022`'s auth-guards-before-logic rule already apply elsewhere in this document set: a privacy control retrofitted onto working recommendation logic is a real regression risk, one built before that logic exists is not.
- **Data-minimization dependency:** the Personalized Insight & Productivity Engine above, and the AI topic-extraction pipeline it shares a substrate with (`architecture.md` §26), must follow the classify-at-intake, abstraction-out storage rule in `decisions/0011-context-in-abstraction-out-data-minimization.md` — raw activity detail is never what's stored or surfaced back to a user as an "insight," only classified, frequency-gated abstractions are.
- **GDPR/CCPA jurisdictional scope, cookie posture, and DSAR handling — resolved 2026-09-24.** `decisions/0025-gdpr-ccpa-jurisdictional-scope-and-cookie-posture.md` closes out `architecture.md` §24's first three open items (GDPR out of scope on intent/targeting, CCPA out of scope on a revisitable threshold basis, cookie consent likely not triggered at all per a direct code audit); `decisions/0026-dsar-export-and-erasure-pipeline.md` closes the fourth (export is new, erasure is synchronous-on-verification rather than cron-first, with a legal-hold exception tied to `decisions/0019`'s DMCA retention); `decisions/0027-international-registration-gate.md` is the registration-time mechanism (home location + silent IP check) that operationalizes the GDPR position with an actual safeguard.
- **Global commerce / marketplace monetization — partially resolved 2026-09-25.** Market is now a named, sequenced Tier (2.8 above), fourth and last of the four primary constructs, per `decisions/0030-four-construct-build-order.md` — no longer just an out-of-scope idea. What's still open: the actual commerce model (full merchant-of-record vs. the narrower payment-free framing), which needs its own ADR and real design pass before Tier 2.8 has anything more than a sequencing placeholder. See `future-considerations.md`'s Global Commerce / Marketplace Monetization entry for the full reasoning and status.
