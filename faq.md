## Getting started

### What is Traversence?
Traversence is a regional travel guide and local directory in one place. **Let's Explore** tells the story of a place and the journeys people take there. **Get Local** is the directory of businesses, services and places. You can use both without an account.

### How do I contact Traversence?
Email **support@traversence.com**. It's at the foot of every page, and in the emails we send you (or just reply to one of them: replies come to us too). Ask anything: your account, a listing, a GO Code order, or your privacy and data.

### Where did the big logo and icons at the top go?
The top bar starts collapsed, so the page gets the whole screen: no bar, just the compass in the top-left corner and the menu (☰) hanging from the top-right corner, with search between them on pages that have it. On a phone the two corners do different jobs. The **compass** is *where you can go*, the same on every page: Let's Explore, Get Local, Social, Marketplace, the Trip planner, Sign in or your Dashboard, and the Help Center (its big logo takes you home). The other corner (your photo, or ☰ if you're not signed in) is *what you can do here*. Signed in, its top line is you and the account you're acting as: tap it to switch (your personal account, a business you manage, or staff) right there in the menu, and tap again to fold it away. Then **Dashboard**, so you can always get back, this part of the site's tools (tap a heading like Things to do to fold it open or shut), and **Sign out** at the bottom. On pages with no tools of their own, like the FAQ, it holds just you, and isn't there when you're not signed in. In your account, the layout switch (your full profile, or just your Pulse) sits next to your photo. Around the site the brown bar stays; in your account the page runs to the top with just those two corners. Notifications are in your Pulse (the bell there); an orange dot on the menu means something is waiting. Everything is still in the menu (☰). To bring back the full logo or the row of icons, tap the small tab next to the compass or next to the menu. We remember your choice on this device.

### Do I need an account?
No, not to look around. You need one to save favorites, write journeys, comment, like, report something, or make suggestions under your name.

### How old do I have to be?
Traversence accounts are for adults: you must be 18 or older. You'll confirm it when you sign up. If your account was made before we asked, or you confirmed an earlier age, we'll ask once, the first time you comment or become a contributor.

### How do I choose where I'm looking?
Open the place picker at the top of the page. You can:
- open the **Trip Planner** to plan a trip: where, when, how long and what you're after;
- pick a **region**;
- type a **destination**, a **state, city or ZIP**;
- use **Auto-detect** to start near you.

In **Destination** you can type a town, a town area, a local name, or a landmark like **Grand Canyon**, **Lyman Lake** or a museum. Towns come first. You can add the state after a comma ("St. Johns, AZ"); the list keeps up while you type it. Choosing a landmark takes you to the town area around it.

Signed in, you can make a place your default (**My default**), so you start there next time.

### Does Auto-detect share where I am?
No. Auto-detect only picks a starting place for you on this visit. Your location is never shown to anyone.

## Exploring places

### What's on the North America page?
The big picture: the story of the continent and its regions, plus the newest journeys and guides from everywhere. From there you can browse by **Regions**, **Experiences** or **Parks & Landmarks**, or open the **Trip Planner**. It's where Let's Explore starts when you haven't chosen a place.

### How do I change the place I'm exploring?
Tap the pencil next to **Explore: <place>** at the top of Let's Explore or of any place page, and choose another place. On a place page, you go straight to the new place's page.

### Whose photos are at the top of a place?
Photos from journeys people have shared about that place, or about places inside it. A region shows photos from all its towns. Share a journey with photos and yours can appear there.

### What are Experiences?
Things to do that are worth the trip: paddling a lake at sunrise, a rodeo weekend, a night under dark skies. You take part, it belongs to the place, and someone local can make it happen. Find them in **Let's Explore → Experiences**, in season first. Each one shows when to go, how long it takes, how hard it is, who can take you, and journeys from people who did it.

### How do I post an experience?
Sign in and open **Dashboard → Your experiences**. Say what you do, where, what it's like and when to go, and add the local businesses that make it happen. You need to be 18 or older. Our editors review your first experiences before they show. If they don't approve one, you'll see their note and can edit it and send it again. Once two of yours have been approved, a well-described experience from you can go up at once.

### Where do the other experiences come from?
Some are posted by members. Some we suggest from what we already know: outdoor places and their activities, local guides, outfitters and museums, and journeys people shared. Our staff add others. Each one gets a credibility score from who posted it and how complete it is. The most credible go up at once and our editors check them afterwards. The rest wait for an editor.

### What's the difference between Experiences and National Parks?
National Parks are places. Experiences are what you do, in a park or anywhere else.

### What's on a place's own page?
Each place has its own guide: a **Regional guide** for a region, an **Area guide**, a **Community guide** for a group of towns, a **City guide** for a town and a **Neighborhood guide** for a local name. They all follow the same order: essentials, things to do, getting there, where to eat and stay, events, itineraries, what living there is like, and its local businesses. On a wide screen, at a glance, the places one step down (its towns, say) and landmarks & heritage run in a column beside the guide. A community of just one town (Farmington) opens that town's guide. A part with nothing in it yet is left out. In Let's Explore, **This place** in the ☰ menu opens it.

### How are places organized?
From large to small: North America, then regions, areas, communities (groups of towns), towns, and neighborhoods (the local names people use). Each has its own guide with its story, its journeys, and what's there.

### What do Visiting and Live here do on a guide?
They show the same guide in two orders. **Visiting** starts with the essentials and things to do, then what the place is like, getting there and where to eat. **Live here** starts with the essentials (who to call, road and weather alerts), local businesses and support. Your choice is remembered; coming from Get Local starts you on Live here.

### Where do a guide's weather and road conditions come from?
Weather and alerts come from the National Weather Service. Tap the weather by the guide's name to open the forecast (it starts folded): the next 24 hours as temperature, rain or wind, and the next seven days. It stays on the page; the weather service's own forecast is a link inside it. Arizona road work, lane closures, detours, incidents and alerts come from AZ 511 (its work zone feed uses the national WZDx format). Our server asks for the place's weather, not yours: your location is never sent. Weather updates every half hour, roads every ten minutes. Check AZ 511 before you drive.

### What is "Walking in the footsteps" in a guide?
The place's story, told where you can stand today: the peoples and families, trades, building materials, water and hard times that made it, each tied to a street, a building, a river crossing or a view you can visit. It's written from our heritage catalog: facts drawn from historic records (the Library of Congress's newspapers, photographs and maps, linked, never copied), the place's public records and our research, and only those backed by a historic record or two independent sources. Archaeological, sacred and burial places follow the law of the place: their story may be told, but never where they are or how to get there, and on a tribal nation's land nothing is told until the nation says.

### What does a highlighted name in a guide do?
Point at it (tap it on a phone) and a small card shows what it is, where it is, its phone number if it has one, and **See more**, which opens its page. Every business, landmark and outdoor place a guide names is highlighted this way, and so are the names in journeys, place stories, experiences and how-tos, so you can go deeper without losing your place.

### Why are names in my journey highlighted?
When you publish a journey (or an experience), we look for the places and businesses it names that are on Traversence, link them, and your piece shows under **Featured in** on their pages. If you've turned on AI Assist & Learning, Claude reads your words to find the names; if not, we only match names from our own directory, and nothing you wrote leaves Traversence. Right after you publish, and any time on your journey's page or its editor, you'll see what it's tagged with: tap × on any that aren't right and it comes off at once. Only you see that list. A name we don't have yet isn't added on its own: a person checks it first.

### What is "Featured in" on a listing or a landmark?
The published guides, journeys, place stories, experiences and how-tos that name it, so you can read what's said about it there. Group posts never show here: they're for the group's members. Nobody pays to be named.

### What do I get when I follow a business or a place?
Besides its own posts: when a guide, journey or story names a business you follow, or a place you follow gets a new guide or story, it shows in your feeds and your Pulse. A member's journey that names a business reaches that business's followers only if they also follow the person who wrote it or the place it's about.

### Who hears about it when I @mention someone?
The person you mention, once, in their Pulse, if they're in the group you posted in (so they can read it). Nobody else is told, and your post doesn't show anywhere outside the group.

### What's in a guide's Essentials?
Weather alerts and road conditions, who to call, and the nearest places for what you might need, three of each, nearest first. Everyone sees emergency and urgent care, pharmacies, police, fire and ambulance, fuel and charging, groceries, banks and ATMs, and visitor information and libraries. **Visiting** adds car trouble, getting around, pet emergencies, and laundry, mail and shipping. **Live here** adds the town hall and government offices, utilities and trash, schools and childcare, doctors, dentists and counseling, home repairs, and community help. **See more** opens the full list in Get Local. For an emergency, call 911.

### How do you choose the businesses a guide shows?
By what the section is about, how close they are, the kind of place (a quick bite or a sit-down meal), the season (winter leads with snow sports, summer with the lakes) and the weather right now: when rain, a storm warning, a heat wave, a deep cold or a strong wind keeps people in, museums, galleries and other indoor places come first, with a **Right now** note saying why; on a fair day the outdoors leads. Places our writers name come first. A paid plan never moves a business up. We only show public listings that are open, one per business: a business marked closed drops out of every guide.

### Who writes the guides?
Traversence compiles them. Our crawler first gathers what's public about the place: its Wikipedia article (read for facts, never copied), its facts on Wikidata, its town or county's own website, and the landmarks and outdoor places we hold nearby. Claude, the AI we use, then researches the place in depth (its peoples and founding, the families and events that shaped it, its faith, food and crafts, the festivals of its year and what it's like to be there) and keeps that research with the place. It writes the guide from, first, what people on the ground know (our team's notes from being there, and the journeys and experiences people have published about the place, a member's only if they've turned on AI & Learning), then that history, then what visitors search for there (counts only, never who). It searches the web only for what the crawler didn't find, and names only places it can back up. What the crawler gathers is kept with the place, so it's never fetched twice, and its facts (when the town was founded, its population) show under At a glance. Each guide gets a confidence score, from how sure Claude is, what it was written from, its sources and how many of its names we have. When the score is high enough, the guide goes live by itself, marked "Compiled by Traversence from public sources and local knowledge" with the date, and our team corrects or takes it down whenever something's off. Every month each live guide is checked again, the ones people have been reading first: whether what the crawler knows about the place changed, whether a date in it has passed, whether a place it names has closed, and whether its research is a year old. When something has, the parts Claude wrote are written again and scored the same way; anything our team wrote is flagged for them instead. See something wrong? Tap **Suggest a change** on the guide.

### Is everything named in a guide real?
Every business, place, landmark and road a guide names that we have links to its page with us. When a guide names something we don't have yet, it shows as plain text while we look for it: first on the map (OpenStreetMap) and its own website, then, if that finds nothing, by Claude on official sources. What we find gets a confidence score: when it's high and the place has an address, it joins the listings our crawler finds and goes through the same checks (a real place, a way to reach it, a category that fits, and not listed already). Once it passes, it's published and the name links, usually within the quarter hour; anything the checks can't settle, our team looks at. A shelter or safe house is never looked up outside: a person links it. A place that shares its phone or website with a listing under another name (a county office's line) is always checked by a person. A guide never names a place that may be sacred, burial or protected, or a business that's closed: it waits as a draft until those are taken out.

### I searched for a local name (like Wide Ruins) and landed on a town. Why?
Many small communities are known by local names that aren't on maps as towns. We file each local name under the town area it belongs to, so searching it shows that town area's places. **Get Local**'s Map shows them on the map.

### On a place page, what happens when I tap the kind, the agency or an activity?
It searches for more like it around there. Tapping **Campground** finds other campgrounds nearby. Tapping the managing agency finds other places it runs nearby, and tapping **Fishing** finds more places to fish. The search shows listings, outdoor places, stories and groups together.

### Can I link a lake, campground or trail?
Yes. Use the link icon in the corner of its page. It's saved with your other linked places.

### What does clicking a town name do?
It opens the directory for that town, so you can switch between the list and the map.

### How do tribal lands appear?
Tribal nations are sovereign nations, and we present them that way. We show public places and public events only. We never point to sacred or restricted sites.

## The directory (Get Local)

### Why do some kinds of business have different names than I expected?
We show each kind of business by the name people use, so you'll see "Doctors" rather than "Physicians & Surgeons" and "Self
Storage" rather than "Storage-Household & Commercial". Where a few of the trade headings mean the same thing, they show as one,
and choosing it shows every business in all of them. Searching by either name works.

### How do I search the directory?
Use the search bar at the top of any page. The panel that opens has:
- **Location** at the top: the place you're searching. Tap ✎ to change it.
- **Search Directory**: search **All**, or just **Discovery**, the **Directory**, **Community** or the **Marketplace**.
- **Distance**: slide from **My place only** to **∞** (no limit), or pick a distance like 25 miles. It starts at **10 mi** around your place and goes back to 10 mi whenever you change your place, so search doesn't look outside your place unless you widen it. Your place stays as you set it: searching, tapping a town in search, or opening another area's page never changes it. That page shows its area, and your place changes only when you choose a new one (or tap **Make it my place**). A link to an area sets your place only when you haven't chosen one yet. With no place chosen there's nothing to measure from, so the slider rests at ∞ until you choose one.
- **Search Results** on the left. Each one says which part of the site it's in: **Directory**, **Discovery** or **Community**.
- **Suggestions** under them, one per line: the categories that match a word you typed ("All Churches in Saint Johns"), each word on its own when nothing has them all, and other searches for the place. When your words describe a situation, its answer already says what kind of place helps, so no category lines show.
- **Quick Search** on the right: one-tap searches for the place, with how many each finds, your own, and the filters (**Near me**, **Open now**, **Has a phone**, **Has a website**, **Favorites**). **Recent Searches** are under them.
- **Show in directory list** opens your results as a list.

### Can I search for sports, like football or basketball?
Yes. **Sports** finds athletic fields, stadiums, gyms, golf courses, public pools, rodeos and sports clubs nearby. **Football**, **basketball**, **baseball**, **soccer** and other sports also find high schools, where most local games are played, and ball fields find parks. Try **gym**, **swimming**, **tennis**, **hunting** or **fishing** too. If a place you play or watch is missing, tap **Tell us what's missing**.

### Can I find natural places like badlands, arches and canyons?
Yes. Search for words like **badlands**, **arch**, **crater**, **hot springs**, **waterfall** or **canyon**, alone or with a place ("crater near Flagstaff"). Each landmark has its own page with a photo where there's a freely licensed one, a short description from Wikipedia, directions, and comments. Names and locations come from USGS Geographic Names. Some landmarks, like monuments and viewpoints, aren't in that data, and our team adds them by hand. If you know one that's missing, use **Tell us** on any page.

### Why don't some landmarks on tribal land show?
Tribal nations are sovereign nations, and each decides what visitors are welcome to see on its land. On a nation's land we show only its public places, the ones the nation itself lists for visitors (like Four Corners Monument), and public businesses. Other places there aren't shown. We never show the location of sacred or restricted sites, and anything a nation asks us to close is closed at once.

### Where do I find parks and landmarks?
In **Let's Explore**, open **Parks & Landmarks**. National parks, monuments and historic sites come first. Under them are natural landmarks like arches, buttes, canyons and falls: the ones near the place you're exploring, or, with no place chosen, the best-known ones with photos. Tap one for its page. Data from the National Park Service itself is coming.

### How do I switch between my accounts or roles?
If you help run a business, or you're on the Traversence team, you can act as yourself or in that role. On a computer, hover over the person icon at the top right: the menu shows your **Current Account**, your **Personal Account**, and your Traversence staff login if you have one. Tap one to switch. If you manage listings, tap **Your listings** to open them right in the menu: tap one to switch to it and open its Overview, where all its tools are. Your first three are there, and **See all** opens every one in a pop-up with a search box. **All your accounts** opens a page with all of them, a brand's locations grouped together. ✎ opens your profile. Clicking the icon itself opens your dashboard. On a phone, tap your initial at the right of the bar under the top of the page.

### I'm on the Traversence team. How do my staff login and my own account fit together?
They're separate: the staff login (a staff ID like TRV-0001, your work email and a password) opens the admin side; your own account is for everything else. An owner links them for you from the admin (People → Staff logins → Link a member account), and we email your own account to confirm: sign in as it, open the link and tap **Link them**. You can unlink from your own account (Dashboard → Privacy → Staff login) at any time. After you sign in with the staff login, the account menu switches to your own account and back without signing in again, for up to 12 hours. Signing in with your own account never opens the admin side.

### I'm on the Traversence team. Where is everything in the admin?
The admin has 11 modules, in the menu on the left (☰ on a phone). Each module has its own sections, listed in the menu next to the page title. Your role and department decide which modules you see. Anything your role can't do is greyed out, with the reason. A section that's coming is greyed out in that menu and opens a page saying what it will do. Lists work the same way everywhere: a search box, filters, and the stage of each item. Tap an item to open it in a panel at the side. The panel shows where the item stands, how long it's been there, and the next steps you can take. The round icons beside the section menu open tools from other modules in a panel over your work, so you don't lose your place, and Back closes the panel. Managers and up can change the steps work goes through, and who may take each one, in **System Configuration → Workflows**. The older admin pages still open from their old links and the site map, with a note saying where their work is now.

### What does Peek do in search?
Tapping a result opens its page. A listing also has a **Peek** button: tap it for a quick card with the address, Call and Directions, without leaving the page you're on.

### How do I add a photo of a place?
Tap the camera button at the bottom right of the photo at the top of a place or a business. It starts a journey with the place already filled in: add your photos or video and a few words, and publish. Once it's published, your photo shows at the top of that page, credited to you. A business's own photos come first, and visitors' photos follow them. If you run the business, the camera is **Photos & video** instead: it chooses the page's own photos. Places on a tribal nation's land follow the nation's rules for photos and pins.

### Why do some names look different from other sites?
Some listings come from public data that shortens words, like "Visitor Ctr" or "Natl Pk". We show them in full ("Visitor Center", "National Park"). Once a business claims its listing, we show the name exactly as the owner writes it.

### Why does a listing say "Also known as"?
Some places go by more than one name: a new name after a change of owner, a legal name on official records, or an older name people still use. "Infinia at Show Low" and "Tall Pines Care and Rehab" are one nursing home, for example. The listing keeps its other names and shows them as **Also known as**, and searching any of them finds it. When our crawler finds the same place under another name (the same address, phone and kind of place), it adds what it learned to the existing listing and keeps the name, instead of listing it twice.

### Why does a listing say "Part of" another name?
Some organizations have several locations: a school district's schools, a health center's clinics, a chain's stores, an agency's offices. Each location has its own listing, with its own address, phone and hours, and says which organization it's **Part of**. Stores of a franchise brand show **Part of** the brand too, but each is its own business: its owner manages only that store's listing. Tap that name to see every location on one page, nearest you first when your place is set. Under each location's details, **Other locations** lists the nearest few, each with Call and Directions. Searching the organization's name finds all of its locations. Our team links them, from the organization's own website (its own list of its schools, clinics or stores) or records, never from a similar name alone. A chain's shared website never makes two of its stores one listing: each store is its own place. Owners claiming all their locations at once is coming.

### Why do several listings share one address and phone number?
Clinics, medical centers and offices often house several providers or businesses under one roof and one front-desk number: a behavioral health center and the counselors who work there, say. Each is its own listing, so you can find the person or service you need. When our crawler finds a business that is already listed under a shortened name ("Apache Behavioral Health Svc" and "Apache Behavioral Health Services"), with the same phone, street address and ZIP, it adds what it learned to the existing listing instead of making a second one, and never overwrites what's there. Our team can also link a facility and its providers, so the facility's page lists them (see "Part of" above).

### Can I see a place's photo bigger?
Yes. Tap the photo at the top of a place, a listing or a profile to open it full screen, with its credit. If there are several, swipe or use the arrows to move between them. You can like it, share it or go to the comments from there too. Tap outside the photo, or ×, to close it.

### Why do some map spots show a number and "approx."?
They're listings whose exact spot isn't on the map yet. Most listings came to us with only their ZIP code, so they all sat on the middle of the ZIP area. Instead of fanning them out as if they were real places, the map shows one dashed circle with how many there are, like **157 approx.** Tap it to see them as a list, then tap one to open it. We place their street addresses on the map a batch at a time, every time our crawler runs, and each one moves to its real spot as soon as it's placed. Some addresses can't be placed that way, like PO boxes and rural routes, so those get their spot from the business's own website, from our team, or from you: open the listing and tap **Wrong spot? Fix the pin**.

### Why did my journey's pin move or disappear?
On a tribal nation's land, a pin shows only at a place the nation opens to visitors. If your pin is within half a mile of one of those places, it moves onto that place. Anywhere else on the nation's land the pin isn't shown, and your journey says where you were in your own words instead. This follows the nations' own rules: many areas are homes, grazing land or sacred places, and aren't open to visitors.

### Where do the visitor rules on a tribal place come from?
From the nation's own website. Our team checks each nation's rules for permits, fees, guides, photography, drones and alcohol, and the page says when they were last checked, with a link to the nation's visitor information. Rules change, so check that link before you go. If a nation asks us to close a place, we close it at once.
### I searched for something and got places from somewhere else. Why?
On Let's Explore, if there's nothing for your search in the place you're exploring, we look across the open regions and say so at the top of the results. Words for landscapes work too: try **badlands**, **canyon**, **petroglyphs**, **ruins** or **dark sky**.

### Why does search show different things on Let's Explore and Get Local?
Search reads where you are. On **Let's Explore** it puts the place's journeys, stories, outdoors, groups and topics first, with businesses after them. On **Get Local** businesses come first. The place you're searching sits at the top left (tap × to search everywhere), and **☆ Save** at the top right keeps the search. Tapping × on a place you typed (like "near Grants") searches the rest of your words everywhere.

### Can I search the way I'd ask a friend?
Yes. Type it the way you'd say it: "new pizza place in town", "a good taco spot nearby", "pizza place in Show Low", "going fishing for apache trout", "where can we camp". When you name something to do, like fishing, hiking, camping or swimming, we look for places to do it, and anything else you mention ("apache trout") puts the places that mention it first. Search reads the place, the kind of business and the time ("open now"), and leaves out words that only describe it, like "new", "good" or "spot". "In town" and "nearby" mean the place you're searching. We can't yet tell which businesses opened recently, so "new" doesn't narrow the list; that's coming. "Where to buy new" reads as shopping, not as a search for the word "new".

Search reads each part of what you type for its job, even without a full sentence:
- **The thing you want**: "plumber", "hardware store", "oil change".
- **Words that narrow it**: "emergency", "urgent", "24 hour" or "after hours" put places open now first and leave out the ones known to be closed ("emergency plumber"). "Emergency room" is still a place, not a filter. Other words in front of the thing ("commercial plumber", "heavy duty generator") put the places that mention them first without hiding the rest.
- **Who it's for**: "for kids", "kids activities", "for seniors", "for veterans"; "for beginners" looks for lessons and classes.
- **Where**: "in", "near" and "around" mark the place, and so does a town at the end with no little word ("hardware store St Johns"). "St", "Mt" and "Ft" read as Saint, Mount and Fort.
- **What's going on**: phrases like "power went out" or "stuck on the highway" are situations (below).

### Can I just describe what's going on?
Yes. Type it the way you'd say it: "the power went out", "stuck on the highway", "lost my keys", "job interview coming up", "I can't pay my bills". Search recognises the situation and shows what it needs, each part on its own: for being stuck on the road, **Towing**, **Roadside repair**, **Gas** and **Highway patrol**, each in your place first and then nearest. When something is urgent, the phone number and **Call** come first, and places known to be open now lead. Everything else your words found is one tap away under **Everything else**. You don't have to use any particular words: search also reads what they mean. "Bit by something", "a dog bit me" and "got stung" are all an injury; "my sink is leaking" is a plumbing problem; "ran out of my meds" is about prescriptions.

### Why does search say "If anyone is in danger, call 911"?
For a few situations where someone could be in danger, like a car accident, smelling gas, a wildfire or being stuck on the road, that line comes first. Everything else follows below it.

### Why isn't a business showing for something urgent?
For an urgent need, a business only shows if its listing has what you'd need to get help: for towing, a phone number. A listing missing it still shows in the directory, but not as an answer when it can't complete the task. If it's your business, claim your listing and add the missing details.

### What shows while I'm typing a search?
Only predictions, so nothing half-finished gets in the way. First **Search for "…"**, then ways your words could carry on (what you typed, with the rest in bold), and sometimes a **Maybe try**: a phrase the search noticed works well, offered as its own suggestion (only from words it understands, never anything personal), then **Go straight to**: what you might be thinking of ("Power out", "Gas for the car"), things whose names start with what you've typed, kinds of things, and towns and counties anywhere in the US (ours first, then the bigger towns elsewhere, marked "no listings yet"). It offers as many as match, up to 15, and keeps up with every letter until you stop, press Enter or pick one. While it's still one word ("for"), places and kinds of things come first, then listings and outdoor places that start with it, then stories, groups and experiences. When your words end in a town you're still typing ("moving to sea"), it offers them with each town that fits ("moving to Seattle, WA"). States show too, from two letters or the state's code ("ok" offers **Oklahoma**): "wash" offers **Washington**, the state, and its searches stay on the state, never Washington, DC. When something's name matches only past your Distance setting ("Ford": Show Low Ford, 38 mi, with 10 mi set), one row says how far the nearest is; tap it to widen to that distance. A word search doesn't know, like a model number ("Ford F150"), doesn't stop it: it suggests from the words it knows ("Ford") and the kind of place those names are (car dealers), and asks if you'd like to suggest the new word. When no name has all your words ("hearing test"), it shows the places the full search would find instead, so there's always something to tap. Many places that share a name at one spot ("Big Lake Dam…", "Big Lake Boating…") show as one line, **Big Lake · 9 places**; tap it to see them all. Until you type a space, search treats your last word as unfinished and finishes it the way you most likely mean: "live mu" offers **live music** and shows music venues, "self stor" shows Self Storage. Words that only describe a thing ("new", "live", "best") narrow what it is instead of changing it, so "new music" means music places. When your words clearly mean a kind of place, that kind and its places come first; a business whose name only shares a word comes after (a nurse named Musick isn't music). When many visitors who typed the same words picked the same kind, its line says "most picked": a label only, it never moves anything up. That's counted by the words and the kind only, never who, and each visit counts once, so one person repeating never changes it. When your words name a situation ("can't find my car"), its answer shows right there with the nearest places and **Call**. Tap any of them, or press **Enter**, and the full results appear with Suggestions and Quick Search. Clicking into the box selects what's there, so typing replaces it. **Esc** clears the words; press it again to close. ### What happens when I search "near me"?

If you haven't set a place, it asks first: **Please set your location**, with **Use my location** (your browser asks permission, once) or **Choose a place**. It never quietly searches everywhere. While you're still typing "near m", it offers **near me** to finish it. We don't keep your location; it's only used to set the place you search from.

### Why does search ask me to set my location?
When you search for somewhere to go (a kind of place like pizza or a plumber, a thing to do, or words like "near me", "local", "in town" or "how far") and no place is set, results would come from everywhere. So search asks **Where should we look?** right above the results, without covering them: type a town or ZIP in the box there and pick it, or tap **Use my location**. Searching a business by name, a town, or a situation that asks on its own (like "flat tire") doesn't ask.

### What happens if I misspell something?
Search never changes your words on its own. If a word isn't one it knows, it asks **Did you mean…?** and offers up to three of the closest words, the likeliest first: the ones that keep the most of what you typed, then the same first letter and the same sound. Each option says what it would find, like "bathroom: A restroom". Tap the one you meant. It asks while you type, too, once a word is finished. It only offers real slips: one letter off in a short word, two in a longer one. A word search understands, like "cardio" or "x-rays", is never "corrected".

Because they give that care, even when their name doesn't say "mental health". ### How does search know what a word means?
It knows the kinds of place that serve everyday words, even when no business uses the word in its name. "X-rays", "xray" and "x ray" all mean Diagnostic Imaging Centers. "Cardio" and "gym" mean gyms and fitness centers. "Haircut" means barbers and salons. Each kind of business has the everyday words people use for it. An English dictionary (WordNet) and word meanings learned from a large body of everyday writing carry those further: "bread" reaches the bakery and the grocery store, and so do sourdough and bagels; "burrito" finds restaurants; "sedan" finds car dealers. That's worked out ahead of time on our server: your search isn't sent anywhere to be understood. While you type, those kinds show with how many are in your place. After **Enter**, **Closest kinds for "…"** lists them, one tap each. When it's only a loose guess, it says it's not sure and offers the guesses as **Maybe**. If nothing matches by name or kind, search shows what **mentions** your words in its description, then suggests changing your search (fewer words, or another word for it) and offers **Suggest it**. What people open after a search keeps improving it. When people keep searching for a kind of place we don't have yet, like cooking classes, search adds that kind on its own with the words people used, sends our crawler to look for such places in every town we cover (checking each one on its own website), and takes the kind away again if nothing comes of it.

### What happens to words search didn't understand?
Search learns them. It already knows most everyday words from a dictionary: it knows a purse, a passport and keys are
someone's belongings, and that a hamster is a pet. So "I found someone's purse" or "someone stole my backpack" works
without anyone teaching it. For words the dictionary doesn't have (local names, new slang), search works out what they
mean on its own, from the words around them and from how our listings and trusted public sources use them, with no
person needed. What people tap afterwards never decides what a word means. Our team can see everything it learned and
undo any of it. Only counts are used, never who searched.

### How does search know which "fall" I mean?

### Where does a business's information come from?
From the evidence. Each listing's details (name, address, phone, website, hours) come from what we can find about the business: its own website, official registries, and other pages that match it by name and address or phone. When those disagree with what we had, **the evidence wins**: the value with the most independent sources goes on the listing by itself. Our team can see every change and undo it. Google, Yelp and Facebook pages only confirm what we found; we never copy from them. Every source we use is on our list with its licence, and the licence decides what we may do with it: keep its facts, show it with credit, link to it, or only use it to confirm. OpenStreetMap, for example, only tells us a place exists and links to it; Wikipedia gives us facts, never its words.

Once a business is **claimed and confirmed** (✓ Verified), its owner decides. Nothing we find changes it on its own. ### Where do the listings come from?

To keep the people inside safe. Domestic-violence shelters and safe houses don't take walk-ins: you call, they talk with you, and they arrange where to meet. Their listings are also never sent to a search or map service to be checked: we check them against our own records only. ### What's a Dynamic GO Code?
A short trvgo.us address (like trvgo.us/k7qm2x) in a code: after you've printed it you can still change where it goes, switch it off, and see how often it's scanned. Dynamic GO Codes need an account and the QR plan ($14.95 a month or $119.40 a year; free while billing isn't switched on yet), or a business's Core plan and up. Find them under **Manage GO Codes**.

### What happens to my printed codes if my plan ends?
Nothing you've printed stops working. A business's Dynamic GO Codes go to its listing on Traversence; a person's keep going wherever they pointed last. You just can't change where they go until the plan is back on. GO Codes you bought keep doing what you made them do. Only a code switched off by its owner, or by our team because it's misused, stops working; scanning it then says it isn't active.

### Why does my GO Code go through trvgo.us?
So you can see how often it's scanned, and so a mistake can be put right after you've printed it. Every GO Code except Wi-Fi holds a short trvgo.us address: a link code goes straight to your link, a contact card or event downloads to the phone, and a phone number, text message or email starts the call, text or email straight away (if a phone's browser wants a tap first, it shows one button that does it). A Wi-Fi code holds the network itself, so phones join without going online first. Scans are counted the same privacy-first way as other trvgo.us links (the day and kind of phone, nothing else). If Traversence ever closes, you'll be able to download your GO Codes re-made to hold what they do directly, so they keep working without us ([Terms](/terms.php#go-codes)).

### Why do shared links start with trvgo.us?
When you tap **Share** or **Copy link** on a listing, a place, a journey or a photo, you get a short trvgo.us address that opens the same page. It's easier to text or print, and it's the same short link for everyone sharing that page. The **QR** button beside Share shows the page's code, to scan or print.

### What's counted when someone scans my code?
The day, and the kind of phone (iPhone, Android or computer). Nothing else: no name, account, IP address, location or cookie. Link previews (when a link is pasted into a chat) and bots aren't counted. There are no ad trackers on trvgo.us.

**See your public profile** shows you what other people see.

### What shows on my public profile?
Only what you publish. Your dashboard opens on your profile: a cover photo, your card (photo, name, @handle, status) and your details (where you live, languages, a mantra, work, education and more). Fill in whatever you like with the pencils. Each item has an eye: **green means public**, plain means only you see it. Nothing you add is public until you switch on its eye. Your name always shows. **See it as others do** opens your profile as a visitor sees it. On a phone your details start folded away so your Pulse is near the top: tap **Details** in your card to open them (we remember your choice on that device).

### What is the Pulse?
Everything coming in, in one place: messages, your groups and connections, and news from the places, businesses and sellers you link. Each source has a pill with its count; tap one to see only that, tap it again to see everything. The bell shows what needs you, and the gear sets how the Pulse looks (a list, one stream, or a tab per source) and which sources it shows. **Pulse ▾** switches between your accounts: personal, a business you manage, or staff.

### What can I do with an item in my Pulse?
Tap ▾ to open it in place: its photos, the full text and a comment box. The chain icon goes to where it came from. Reply, forward, pin, report, move to a folder and add to a collection are on each row, and commenting, attachments and Publish, Save as draft or Schedule are in the open item. Most of these are coming: for now they show where they'll be.

### Where are all my photos?
In your **Gallery** (the camera in your menu, or the **Gallery** tab of your dashboard, which shows your newest 12 and your albums with **Open your gallery** for the rest): photos and videos you uploaded, your profile and cover photos, and the photos in your journeys, grouped by when they were taken. Tap one to see it full size: swipe or use the arrows, pinch to zoom, and close it with ×, Esc or a tap on the dark background. Its ⋯ menu edits the caption, alt text and date of your own photos, saves it to your phone or computer, or shares it outside Traversence. Visitors to your profile see only what you've shared: your public albums and published journeys.

### What are the tabs on a profile?
Quick ways into each tool: **Dashboard** (your profile with your Pulse under it, where your account opens; tap it to come back from any other tab), **About**, **Contacts** (your private Address Book, only you see it), **Gallery** (for you, every photo you've uploaded; for visitors, the photos from your published journeys), **Connections** (how many people follow you, if you publish it), **Journeys**, and **Check-ins**, **Events** and **Reviews**, which are coming. Visitors see the tabs for what you share.

### How do I frame my profile photo?
Tap the pencil on your photo, then **Reposition and size**. Drag the photo to move it inside the square, and use the slider (or + and −) to make it bigger or smaller. We keep a private, resized copy of the photo you chose, so you can reframe it later without uploading it again. When you remove the photo, that copy goes too. The cover photo drags up or down the same way.

### Can I make all my photos public at once?
Yes. When you add or change a photo, tick **Make all my photo uploads public**. We remember it, and each new photo starts out public. You can still hide any one photo, and untick the box to go back to choosing each time.

### Can people look at my profile photos?
Yes, if you've made them public. On your public profile, a tap on your cover or profile photo opens it full screen with your name, likes, comments and Share; on a phone, swipe up from the photo to its comments, and drag the photo down onto the × to close it. Liking, commenting on and sharing photos are coming. To change a photo, use the pencil on it in your dashboard.

### Why do I show as "Member #" and a number?
You haven't chosen a display name yet. We never show your email, so until you pick a name, people see a member number. Set one under **Profile**.

### What names can I use?
Any name from 2 to 40 characters, using letters, numbers, spaces and simple punctuation. Leave out phone numbers and email addresses. Names that suggest you speak for Traversence, such as "Traversence", "Admin", "Staff" or "Support", are reserved.

### What's the difference between a public and a private profile?
- **Public:** anyone can see your name and your journeys, and members can follow you.
- **Private:** people must send you a connection request instead of following, and you can't publish journeys, because journeys show your name.

Either way, your email and your location are never shown.

### What does Verified mean?
A **✓ Verified** mark on someone's profile means they agreed to have the details they gave us checked, and the
checks are done: their email (with the link we sent them) and that they're 18 or older. We only check what a person
gave us. We never search for anything more about them, and nothing else about them is shown. Checking a phone with a
texted code is coming. A business owner's business is checked on its own, against its website and the public
registries we list.

### Do I have to be verified?
No, not to use Traversence. Browsing, searching, saving, commenting and messaging never need it. When you create an
account we ask once, as its own step, and you can answer **Not now**. You need Verification switched on only to write
as a contributor, claim a business, start a community, or work on Traversence's staff. Each of those asks for it
in place, with one tick. Switch it on or off any time in **Privacy Settings → Your tools**. Switching it off removes
the Verified mark, not your account. We keep a record of when you said yes or no and the exact words you were shown.

### Who can see my email?
No one on the site: your email is never shown on Traversence. Traversence staff can see it only to verify your
account, or to contact you officially when we can't reach you on Traversence itself, and only when it's truly
needed. Each time they look, it's recorded.

### Why is there a "verify you're human" check when I sign in?
It stops bots from guessing passwords and making fake accounts. It's Cloudflare Turnstile: usually it passes on its own in a second, with no puzzle to solve. It's on the sign-in, sign-up and "forgot password" forms (signing in with Google, Apple, Microsoft or Facebook skips it, since they check already). It isn't used for ads.

### How is the Traversence team's access protected?
Anyone on our team who can use the admin side signs in in two steps: their password (or Google and the others), then a 6-digit code we email them, before any admin page opens. They can trust their own browser for 30 days, and changing their password makes every browser ask again. Two-step sign-in for business owners is coming, before businesses can connect payments.

### What can Traversence staff see about my account?
We don't look at accounts unless there's an issue or you ask us for help. When we do, staff say why before they
open yours (a report, your request, a claim, a check), and that's recorded with their name. What they see is the
basics from your sign-up: your display name, when you joined, whether your email and age are confirmed, whether
you're Verified, the roles you hold, and the privacy choices you made. Your email and phone stay hidden unless they're
needed to contact you, and showing them is recorded separately. No one, in any role, can see your private
messages, your Address Book or your contact cards.

### How will Traversence contact me?
On Traversence: through your notifications, your Pulse and a notice at the top of the page. We use your email or
phone only for official contact when we can't reach you here (for example, if you can't sign in), and only when
it's needed. If you contact us for help or sales, we reply to you, or to the contact you name, about what you asked.
A business's address is used only for that business (claiming it, or confirming it); we don't ask members for a
postal address.

### Can my account be limited or revoked?
Yes, for breaking the [posting guidelines](#posting-guidelines-and-safety) or misusing the site. You always hear from
us first, on Traversence, with what happened and the steps to put it right:
1. **Warning.** You have 24 hours to confirm you'll follow the steps, or to ask for a review.
2. **Limited, with a final warning.** If we don't hear from you, you can still browse, but you can't post, comment or
   send messages. You have 24 more hours.
3. **Access revoked.** If there's still no reply, you can't sign in. We email you what happened, how to recover, and
   a link to ask for a review.

Something serious (threats, scams, sharing someone's private information) goes straight to step 3. Asking for a
review at any step stops the clock until a person at Traversence has looked at it, and they reply by email. Your
posts aren't deleted at any step. Your notice is at [Your account notice](/user/notice.php). If you can't sign in,
you can get the review link sent again there.

### What lowers my standing?
Mostly, something you posted being taken down after people reported it. Each one lowers your account's trust score,
and when the score drops too low, a warning opens on its own. Confirming your email raises it. A take-down that's
reversed gives it back.

### Can anyone read my private messages?
No. No one at Traversence, in any role, can read your private messages or your contact cards.

### Does Traversence use AI on what I write?
Only if you turn it on. **AI Assist & Learning** is off until you switch it on in Privacy Settings. Private messages and contact cards never enter shared learning, whatever you choose. [Our approach](/our-approach.php) has the details.

### Do you sell my information or show me targeted ads?
No. We don't sell or rent your information, share it for advertising, target ads, or track you around the web. [Our approach](/our-approach.php) explains what we never do, and the [Privacy Policy](/privacy.php) says exactly what we collect and why.
